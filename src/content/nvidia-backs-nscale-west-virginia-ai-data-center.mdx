## Nvidia Weighs Residual Debt Guarantees for Nscale AI Data Center

### Chipmaker in talks to back construction financing for Anthropic campus

Nvidia is in talks to guarantee billions of dollars in debt financing for AI cloud provider Nscale's planned data center campus in West Virginia. The proposed residual value guarantee is intended to secure power and construction funding for facilities hosting Anthropic workloads as lenders grow cautious about massive AI infrastructure capital expenditures.

## Key details

Under the proposed arrangement reported by The Information and corroborated by Bloomberg, Nvidia would provide a residual value guarantee covering electricity and construction costs for Nscale's West Virginia data center campus. If Nscale defaults on construction payments, Nvidia would step in to temporarily take over the facility and assist in finding a new buyer and tenant. While Nvidia previously supported cloud providers by underwriting leases and hardware purchases, backing physical construction obligations represents a notable expansion of the chipmaker's financial commitments.

The West Virginia development is slated to host model developer Anthropic, which signed a $44.6 billion contract covering the project's first 460 MW building. Anthropic and Nscale are also discussing an additional contract valued at roughly $45 billion for a second building at the campus, designed around Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin chips. Nscale, which is preparing an initial public offering targeting a valuation of up to $35 billion, had approximately 25,000 advanced AI accelerators active in August 2026 toward an ultimate target of nearly 500,000 deployed chips.

Nvidia has committed roughly $170 billion across investments and credit support mechanisms to neocloud operators and key buyers across the industry, including a $1 billion convertible financing allocation to Nscale agreed upon in September 2026. The new credit backing aims to unlock bank and institutional financing for large-scale electrical infrastructure and structural buildouts that standalone specialized cloud firms cannot easily fund from operational cash flows alone.

### Why this matters

The arrangement illustrates the escalating financial friction surrounding multi-gigawatt AI infrastructure projects. Specialized cloud providers often lack investment-grade credit ratings or diversified cash-flow businesses to underwrite multi-billion-dollar utility interconnections, substations, and compute shells. When infrastructure operators struggle to raise low-cost debt, equipment suppliers with substantial cash reserves face pressure to act as de facto cosigners to prevent project cancellations and maintain chip sales momentum.

Furthermore, capital markets have increasingly scrutinized neocloud balance sheets, with public valuations for comparable operators dropping sharply in late 2026. Because Anthropic is simultaneously navigating its own initial public offering and faces credit ratings that could increase its borrowing costs, supplier-backed debt guarantees have become a vital mechanism to keep power and facility contracts viable.

### Context

Capital-intensive AI buildouts are confronting physical and regulatory hurdles alongside financial ones. Nscale's West Virginia development faced scrutiny and negotiations over site storm runoff that flooded nearby residences before obtaining state certification in September. Elsewhere, grid availability continues to dictate project feasibility; earlier in October, Microsoft withdrew from a planned UK project with Nscale after utility grid connections were delayed until the 2030s.

Across the United States and Europe, utility interconnection queues and localized power constraints have driven developers toward massive multi-hundred-megawatt campuses. These facilities require extensive upfront power commitments and substation investments years before revenue begins, forcing developers and hardware manufacturers into novel co-financing structures.

## Risks and open questions

Taking on construction risk exposes chipmakers to potential balance sheet liabilities if demand shifts, inference costs fall, or tenants downsize long-term commitments. If Nscale encounters cost overruns, operational setbacks, or execution delays, Nvidia could be obligated to manage or liquidate partially completed physical real estate in addition to hardware inventory.

A key question is whether institutional lenders will accept residual value guarantees without imposing restrictive debt covenants on power off-take agreements. Observers are also watching whether similar cosigning arrangements will emerge across other major data center projects as utility interconnection fees and electrical equipment costs continue to rise.

## What happens next

Negotiations between Nvidia and Nscale remain ongoing as Nscale prepares its planned public offering. If finalized, the debt guarantee structure could establish a precedent for equipment suppliers financing the physical energy infrastructure required to deploy next-generation AI accelerators.

In West Virginia, site preparation and electrical work are moving forward under state certification. Further regulatory disclosures regarding long-term power supply contracts and substation connections are expected as the first 460 MW phase approaches commissioning.
