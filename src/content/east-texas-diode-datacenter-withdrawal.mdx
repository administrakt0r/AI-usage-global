## Texas AI Data Center Withdrawn Amid Cedar Creek Lake Water Backlash

### Developer Diode Ventures cancels East Texas project following community concerns over local lake levels and Abbott's strict directives

The developer Diode Ventures has officially withdrawn its proposal to build a large-scale data center near Cedar Creek Lake in Henderson County, Texas. This decision follows mounting backlash from local residents and officials who feared the facility would deplete their primary water supply during severe drought conditions. The cancellation represents a direct response to Texas Governor Greg Abbott's recently tightened regulatory expectations for resource-heavy AI infrastructure.

## Key details

Local residents and officials in Henderson County raised intense concerns over water withdrawals from Cedar Creek Lake, which supplies the Fort Worth metropolitan area. With low rainfall and dropping lake levels this year, the prospect of a massive industrial water consumer sparked significant community anxiety. On July 23, 2026, Governor Abbott announced that Diode Ventures would not pursue the project. Co-presidents Rachel Attebery and Josh Johnson acknowledged that close proximity to the rural residential community did not meet responsible development standards.

This withdrawal aligns with Governor Abbott's June 10, 2026, directive to the Public Utility Commission (PUC) and the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT). These state expectations mandate that data center developers must:
- Ensure grid interconnections reduce residential electrical bills.
  - Pay for all of their own electric infrastructure costs to protect residential ratepayers.
  - Expand the state's electrical capacity rather than just increasing demand.
  - Utilize water-efficient technologies, such as closed-loop cooling systems, to prevent draining local water resources.
  - Implement setbacks, noise-reduction technology, and other community protection measures.

Furthermore, the governor plans to seek legislation requiring large data centers to annually report electricity and water usage to the PUC, while repealing outdated sales tax exemptions.

### Why this matters

Data centers hosting advanced AI workloads are highly resource-intensive, requiring millions of gallons of water daily for cooling. When projects attempt to tap into rural or suburban water systems, they directly compete with residential, agricultural, and municipal needs. This high-profile cancellation demonstrates that local public resistance and state-level policy interventions are becoming major obstacles to rapid AI infrastructure expansion.

### Context

Texas is the second-largest data center market in the United States, historically favored by developers due to its cheap land, fast-tracked permitting, and generous tax exemptions. However, the cumulative strain of AI compute demand on the state's power grid and water resources has ignited a political backlash. This project cancellation is the first major casualty since Governor Abbott suggested banning data centers in rural areas, signaling that the era of unregulated, subsidized infrastructure growth in Texas is coming to a close.

## Risks and open questions

While Diode Ventures has retracted its proposal, Henderson County leaders warn that local communities remain vulnerable. Under current Texas law, counties lack zoning and land-use authority, meaning they cannot legally prevent another developer from purchasing the same land for a similar project. Without formal statewide legislation codifying the governor's directives, rural resources remain exposed to future development bids.

## What happens next

Texas lawmakers are preparing to introduce comprehensive data center oversight bills in the upcoming legislative session to codify the governor's strict resource mandates. These bills will likely enforce mandatory closed-loop cooling systems and eliminate sales tax exemptions. In the meantime, Henderson County and other rural districts are passing local resolutions to petition the state for direct zoning and regulatory authority over incoming AI projects.
