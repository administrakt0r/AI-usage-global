## US House Passes Ratepayer Protection Act to Limit AI Grid Costs

### Bipartisan legislation requires state regulators to enforce full cost recovery from large data center loads.

The U.S. House of Representatives has passed the bipartisan Ratepayer Protection Act, requiring state utility commissions to ensure large data center developments cover 100% of their incremental grid upgrade costs. The measure addresses growing nationwide concern that utility investments for energy-hungry AI facilities are driving up residential electricity bills.

## Key details

Co-sponsored by Representatives Gabe Evans (R-Colo.) and Kathy Castor (D-Fla.), the bill mandates that state utility regulators and unregulated utilities establish standards ensuring full cost recovery for generation, transmission, and distribution upgrades required by large loads of 100 MW or more. Key provisions include requiring data center operators to pay upfront for system impact studies, provide financial security assurances before construction begins, and pay exit fees if a project is canceled or scaled down.

The legislation gives state regulators a one-year window to begin formal consideration of large-load tariffs and two years to finalize decisions. Industry analysts note that while the bill reinforces a trend where 37 states already enforce or evaluate large-load requirements, its inclusion of power generation costs could significantly alter project timelines by requiring data center developers to secure their own power generation.

### Why this matters

As artificial intelligence data centers scale toward multi-gigawatt facilities that consume as much power as medium-sized cities, electric utilities face unprecedented capital expenditure requirements for transmission line expansions and new generation capacity. Without strict cost-allocation frameworks, utilities risk shifting multi-billion-dollar infrastructure expenses onto residential consumers, leading to double-digit utility rate hikes across regions experiencing rapid data center expansion.

### Context

The federal legislative push comes as state utility commissions and regional grid operators like PJM Interconnection struggle to manage soaring interconnection queues. In recent months, multiple states have enacted local ratepayer protection laws or imposed temporary data center moratoriums. The House measure aligns with broader efforts, including Senator Martin Heinrich's proposed GRID Savings Act, to establish federal standards for large-load grid interconnections.

## What happens next

The Ratepayer Protection Act now advances to the Senate, where its legislative path remains uncertain ahead of upcoming midterm elections. State public utility commissions are expected to continue proactively implementing state-level large-load tariffs and "bring-your-own-power" mandates regardless of federal progress.
