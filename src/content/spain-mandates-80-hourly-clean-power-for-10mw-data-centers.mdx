## Spain Mandates 80% Hourly Clean Power for 10MW Data Centers

### Royal Decree limits water use to 0.1 L/kWh and averts €5B consumer surcharge

Spain's Council of Ministers has approved a landmark Royal Decree establishing binding energy, water, and sovereignty requirements for data centers exceeding 10 MW. The regulation compels operators to match up to 80% of power consumption with newly added renewables on an hourly basis, heading off an estimated €5 billion surge in electricity bills. As AI infrastructure requests swell to 35 GW, Spain becomes the first major European economy to impose strict hourly clean-power correlation and water efficiency caps on hyperscale computing.

## Key details

The decree introduces stringent conditions for grid access. Facilities above 10 MW must demonstrate that 80% of their electricity is matched hour-by-hour with renewable energy until renewables exceed 90% of Spain's generation mix. That clean generation must come from renewable assets commissioned no more than 36 months before the facility begins operations, backed by self-consumption, storage, or power purchase agreements lasting at least six years.

The decree provides a five-year transitional pathway for facilities in Spain's Automatic Power Reduction System (SRAP), granting grid operators emergency curtailment authority. Enrolled centers must source 60% additional clean energy during peak tariff hours through 2028, rising to 70% in peak and mid-peak hours through year five. Pipeline projects have six months to adapt technical filings—or three months if awaiting a grid auction—or surrender connection rights without penalty.

Beyond power, the decree establishes strict resource baselines. Pending the EU data center rating framework under Directive (UE) 2023/1791, facilities must maintain a Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE) at or below 0.1 liters per kilowatt-hour, effectively banning open evaporative cooling. Facilities must also meet a Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) cap of 1.25. Data centers larger than 500 kW must submit annual sustainability metrics, while those exceeding 1 MW must verify adherence to the EU Code of Conduct on Data Centre Energy Efficiency.

### By the numbers

- **10 MW**: Capacity threshold triggering mandatory hourly renewable matching and water standards.
- **80%**: Required share of electricity matched with renewable generation on an hour-by-hour basis.
- **0.1 L/kWh**: Maximum allowable Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE) under the transitional cooling regime.
- **1.25**: Maximum allowable Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) cap for new grid-connected campuses.
- **35 GW**: Total data center grid applications in Spain, comprising 15 GW granted and 20 GW in the queue.
- **€5 billion**: Estimated electricity surcharge for consumers averted by preventing unmanaged gas generation.
- **25%**: Projected increase in industrial electricity costs calculated to occur without the decree.
- **3 years**: Maximum suspension of grid connection rights for facilities that violate compliance obligations.

### Why this matters

Data centers draw flat baseload power. In grids with high variable solar generation like Spain's, steady loads that lack flexibility cannot rely on midday solar alone. Without hourly matching or storage, continuous demand forces utilities to fire combined-cycle natural gas turbines during evening and night hours, inflating wholesale prices across the power system.

Ecological Transition Minister Sara Aagesen emphasized the financial stakes. Government modeling showed that absorbing tens of gigawatts of inflexible computing demand would have imposed a €5 billion surcharge on consumer bills and raised industrial electricity prices by 25%. Restricting WUE to 0.1 L/kWh also shields arid river basins from evaporative cooling withdrawals that have sparked public opposition across drought-prone regions.

### Context

Spain has become a primary European digital gateway, but speculative proposals overwhelmed the grid. The 15 GW in granted access permits alone equaled roughly 40% of Spain's national peak electricity demand.

The decree mirrors global regulatory moves targeting AI's resource footprint. In the United States, grid operator [PJM Interconnection](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/pjm-mandates-grid-ride-through-rules-for-50mw-data-centers) recently proposed mandatory grid stability rules to protect networks from large loads. Earlier discussions over [Spain's renewable energy revisions](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/spain-revises-data-center-renewable-mandate) highlighted fierce industry resistance to hourly green accounting. By enacting binding hourly correlation into law, Spain establishes a concrete model that outpaces wider European targets.

## Risks and open questions

Industry association SpainDC condemned the measures as a de facto moratorium, warning that adapting active projects within six months and facing potential toll surcharges of 300% to 500% threatens foreign investment.

Additionally, Spain's Council of State raised 14 legal objections regarding regional planning competencies, and Madrid announced plans to challenge the decree before the Supreme Court. Securing long-term PPAs and battery storage in a tight equipment market remains a major hurdle.

## What happens next

The decree takes effect upon publication in the Official State Gazette. Developers with pending applications must certify compliance within three to six months or withdraw without penalty. MITECO will subsequently establish regional PUE calculation methods, while the European Commission finalizes permanent continental efficiency benchmarks.
