## ERCOT Sets December Target for Texas Data Center Audits

### Grid operator prepares RFI audit of 300 large facilities and 157 medium sites

The Electric Reliability Council of Texas announced a detailed roadmap to comply with state directives auditing data center interconnections. The grid operator aims to submit a comprehensive audit report to state regulators by December 10, 2026.

## Key details

Speaking at an open meeting, ERCOT officials outlined the audit process triggered by Governor Greg Abbott's order pausing new data center interconnections. The audit targets roughly 300 large facilities—defined as 75 MW or greater—currently navigating the Batch Zero interconnection queue. Additionally, ERCOT is rolling out community impact reviews for 157 medium-sized data center and cryptocurrency sites representing 8,800 MW of anticipated demand.

ERCOT will begin issuing Requests for Information (RFIs) to large load developers starting in late August and September 2026, with follow-up rounds extending through November. Officials also highlighted that 17 large projects representing 6.6 GW of peak demand have completed stability assessments but must pass the audit before receiving approval to energize. While total queue requests exceed 474 GW, ERCOT noted that much of this volume represents speculative or duplicative capacity.

### Why this matters

Texas hosts one of the largest concentrations of AI infrastructure developments in the United States. Requiring rigorous verification of power readiness, water supply, and community impacts establishes a regulatory framework that prevents speculative capacity reservations from distorting regional grid planning.

### Context

The ERCOT audit follows broader state and federal efforts to protect electrical grids and residential ratepayers from uncoordinated hyperscale expansion. With data center interconnection queues exceeding peak grid demand by fivefold, grid operators across North America are instituting stricter verification standards.

## What happens next

ERCOT will issue RFI templates to developers through September and assemble audit findings for a formal filing with the Public Utility Commission of Texas on December 10, 2026. Regulators will evaluate the findings during their December open meeting to determine updated timelines for the Batch Zero interconnection process.
