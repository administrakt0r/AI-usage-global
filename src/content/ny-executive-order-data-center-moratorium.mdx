## New York Governor Signs Nation's First Data Center Moratorium

### Executive order pauses new facilities to study energy and water impacts

New York Governor Kathy Hochul has signed an executive order establishing the first statewide moratorium on new data center development in the United States. The move aims to address mounting concerns over the massive energy and water requirements of AI infrastructure.

## Key details

The executive order, signed on July 15, 2026, halts the approval and construction of new data centers across New York State. This pause is intended to provide state agencies time to evaluate the long-term impacts of these facilities on the electrical grid, local water supplies, and community noise levels.

The Information Technology and Innovation Foundation (ITIF) responded critically to the order, with President Daniel Castro stating that while issues like energy demand and water use deserve "thoughtful consideration," a total freeze on investment could push AI infrastructure development overseas. The moratorium comes as New York and other states grapple with the rapid expansion of AI projects that threaten to outpace existing grid capacity and climate goals.

### Why this matters

New York's decision marks a significant regulatory shift, as it is the first time a state has used executive power to halt data center expansion specifically citing resource constraints. For AI companies, this creates a major bottleneck in one of the country's most significant tech markets and sets a precedent that other states facing grid strain might follow.

### Context

This executive order follows months of legislative debate in New York regarding AI's environmental footprint. Previous efforts, such as proposed statewide bills and local moratoriums in places like the Village of Endicott, signaled growing political resistance to the resource-intensive nature of the AI boom. Nationally, data center electricity consumption is projected to grow significantly, with AI workloads accounting for an increasing share of that demand.

## Risks and open questions

The primary risk is that the moratorium could lead to a loss of technological leadership and economic investment if AI developers move projects to regions with fewer restrictions. There is also the question of how long the "temporary" pause will last and whether the resulting standards will be achievable for the industry without compromising performance.

## What happens next

New York state agencies are expected to begin a comprehensive review of data center resource usage and environmental impact immediately. The findings will likely form the basis for new, stricter standards for energy efficiency, water conservation, and grid integration that facilities must meet once the moratorium is eventually lifted.
