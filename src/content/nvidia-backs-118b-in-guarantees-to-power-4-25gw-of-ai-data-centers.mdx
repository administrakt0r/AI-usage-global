## Nvidia Backs $118B in Guarantees to Power 4.25GW of AI Data Centers

### SoftBank’s 4.25-gigawatt Ohio campus and neoclouds receive debt backstops as grid power becomes the chief bottleneck for AI

Nvidia disclosed up to $118 billion in financial guarantees to backstop land, power, and data center shell facilities for customers, confirming that electrical capacity has replaced semiconductor fabrication as the primary constraint on artificial intelligence. The commitments, revealed alongside third-quarter data center revenue of $107.6 billion, position the chipmaker as a debt guarantor for massive computing infrastructure, led by SoftBank’s 4.25-gigawatt PORTS-Pike Technology Campus in Ohio.

## Key details

The disclosures, reported by [Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/data-center-chips/nvidia-ai-surge-comes-with-118-billion-in-guarantees), show Nvidia committing its balance sheet to bridge an infrastructure funding gap for developers, utilities, and AI laboratories. 

The cornerstone is a $105 billion credit backstop for SB Energy’s PORTS-Pike Technology Campus in Pike County, Ohio. Spanning nine buildings, the project is designed for 4.25 gigawatts (4,250 megawatts) of capacity—surpassing the output of two commercial nuclear power plants. The facility will host Nvidia hardware under 20-year tenant leases to OpenAI.

These guarantees phase in gradually. Each activates only after a building is completed and energized, with the first phase targeted for fiscal 2029. Gross exposure will peak at $105 billion before declining over two decades as OpenAI makes lease payments. As of October 25, 2026, Nvidia had made zero payout payments.

Nvidia also disclosed $13 billion in additional infrastructure guarantees:
- **$9.5 billion** in power procurement and systems credit support.
- **$3.5 billion** in land, power interconnect, and shell guarantees for specialized neocloud providers.
- **$32 billion** in separate commercial commitments under neocloud agreements that take effect once operators secure project financing.

To fill these power shells, Nvidia’s supply purchase commitments climbed to $390 billion, up $111 billion in a single quarter. The surge reflects multi-year procurement of high-bandwidth memory, advanced packaging with Amkor and Siliconware Precision Industries (SPIL) through 2029, and ajinomoto build-up film substrates with Kinsus, which plans to expand dedicated capacity 3.5-fold by late 2028.

### By the numbers

- **$118 billion**: Maximum gross financial guarantee exposure for customer land, power, and shell infrastructure.
- **$105 billion**: Credit support dedicated to SB Energy’s PORTS-Pike campus in Ohio hosting OpenAI.
- **4.25 GW**: Planned electrical capacity across nine buildings at the PORTS-Pike campus.
- **$390 billion**: Total supply commitments locked in by Nvidia for memory, substrates, and packaging.
- **$13 billion**: Infrastructure guarantees allocated to neocloud providers for power interconnection and shells.
- **20 years**: Duration of tenant lease agreements between SB Energy and OpenAI backed by Nvidia.

### Why this matters

The $118 billion guarantee package signals a structural shift: capital expenditure risk is moving from silicon to electrical substations, transmission lines, and utility interconnects. 

As cluster power requirements escalate to multi-gigawatt campuses, developers face prohibitive financing hurdles. Commercial lenders hesitate to finance multi-billion-dollar electrical infrastructure for startups whose balance sheets cannot independently guarantee 20-year contracts. By cosigning lease debt, Nvidia uses its $65.3 billion quarterly net income to absorb construction and power delivery risks.

The energy demand is immense. At 4.25 gigawatts, SB Energy’s Ohio campus will draw as much power as roughly 3.4 million average US homes, placing unprecedented strain on the regional PJM transmission grid.

### Context

Nvidia’s strategy builds on its August 2026 equity stake in SB Energy, covered by [AI Usage Global](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/nvidia-invests-1-5b-in-sb-energy-ohio-datacenter), when it invested $1.5 billion into SoftBank’s clean energy arm to accelerate PORTS-Pike. 

The move highlights how compute demand has outrun utility queues, which stretch up to seven to ten years across North America. While Nvidia reported that its Vera Rubin platform delivers 10 times more tokens per watt than GB300 on the MLPerf Endpoints benchmark, analysts stress that efficiency gains will not lower aggregate electricity consumption. Lower cost per token incentivizes laboratories to run larger models, ensuring total grid demand continues to climb.

## Risks and open questions

The primary risk lies in counterparty exposure and construction timelines. If delays or transmission backlogs stall buildings at PORTS-Pike, guarantee schedules could be disrupted. Backing $105 billion for a single tenant, OpenAI, also concentrates commercial risk if software revenue growth lags compute costs. Furthermore, as regional grid operators propose strict cost-recovery mechanisms to ensure large loads pay for grid upgrades, Nvidia-backed sites could face higher transmission tariffs or curtailment rules.

## What happens next

Construction on the first phases of the PORTS-Pike campus will proceed toward an initial energization milestone in fiscal 2029. Nvidia will file its full Form 10-Q with the SEC in early December 2026, detailing default covenants and collateral terms. Regulators and utilities in Ohio will evaluate the substation and transmission expansions required to deliver the first gigawatt blocks of power.
