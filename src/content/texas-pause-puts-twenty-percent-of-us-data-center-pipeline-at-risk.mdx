## Texas Pause Puts 20% of US AI Pipeline at Risk of Delay

### Bloomberg NEF warns of billions in lost leasing revenue under Abbott’s grid audit

The pause on Texas data center grid interconnections puts roughly 20% of the total U.S. data center pipeline at risk of delay, according to a Wednesday analysis by Bloomberg NEF. The research highlights the immediate financial and operational fallout from Texas Governor Greg Abbott’s directive to audit all data centers in the state's main grid.

## Key details

On Monday, Governor Greg Abbott implemented a grid connection pause affecting almost 49.8 GW of active projects in the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) queue. Abbott ordered a comprehensive audit of data centers' energy demands, water usage, and state tax incentives, prompting the grid operator to delay its Batch Zero review under the new large load interconnection process.

According to Bloomberg NEF (BNEF), the financial consequences of this halt could be immense. BNEF forecasted approximately 1.2 GW of new data center additions in ERCOT between the second quarter of 2026 and the first quarter of 2027. If 60% of this delayed capacity is AI-related, direct revenue losses for operators could reach up to $8 billion by the first quarter of 2027 alone. BNEF's modeling indicates that high-density AI computing infrastructure can earn around $1.76 billion per gigawatt per month in leasing revenues.

Furthermore, Abbott's letter revealed that ERCOT’s total interconnection queue now stands at 474 GW, with data centers accounting for approximately 90% of those new power requests. This indicates a massive mismatch, as the total request volume is more than five times ERCOT’s historic peak demand record.

### Why this matters

This story demonstrates the direct clash between the rapid scaling of resource-heavy artificial intelligence and public power grid stability. With AI models requiring highly concentrated compute resources, developers are flooding energy markets like Texas with multi-gigawatt requests. When a major energy regulator halts interconnections, it creates a massive bottleneck that delays the global rollout of advanced AI hardware and severely impacts the economics of hyperscalers.

### Context

Prior to this freeze, Texas was a premier destination for data center builders due to its deregulated energy market, abundant land, and fast-track connection policies. However, the sheer volume of incoming AI demands has forced state authorities to intervene to protect local consumers from skyrocketing residential utility bills and grid reliability failures. This intervention mirrors growing regulatory pushbacks in other data center hubs, such as Virginia, New York, and Ireland, where local utilities are grappling with unprecedented energy and water constraints.

## Risks and open questions

The principal risk is that the audit will drag on, turning a temporary pause into a prolonged moratorium. If the freeze persists into the 2027 Texas legislative session, lawmakers could introduce permanent restrictions or impose heavy surcharges on high-load compute facilities.

Additionally, there are open questions about how many of the 474 GW of requests in the ERCOT queue are "phantom" duplicative proposals designed to hoard grid capacity, versus real projects with committed funding. If developers must supply their own off-grid generation, it could accelerate the construction of fossil-fuel-powered gas turbines, contradicting corporate climate goals.

## What happens next

State regulators and ERCOT officials will begin auditing existing queue submissions to assess their grid dependence, water footprint, and economic contributions. Developers with projects caught in the delayed Batch Zero review will have to wait for the audit results, potentially shifting investments to regions with more predictable regulatory landscapes. Industry watchers will monitor how hyperscalers respond to the threat of losing billions in leasing revenues as they navigate this unexpected regulatory hurdle.
