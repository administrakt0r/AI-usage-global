## Aurora Passes Data Center Moratorium as Vantage Drops 334 MW Trident Site

### Colorado's third-largest city froze new data center applications through September 2027 on the same night Vantage Data Centers abandoned its 860,000-square-foot campus beside three residential neighborhoods.

Aurora City Council voted unanimously on November 23, 2026, to halt new data center applications across most of the city through September 30, 2027, hours after Vantage Data Centers confirmed it is abandoning its planned Trident campus near East 48th Avenue and E-470. The project's 334 megawatts of planned computing load (with total campus power estimates reaching up to 500 MW) would have made it more than twice as large as any existing facility in Colorado.

## Key details

According to [Sentinel Colorado](https://sentinelcolorado.com/metro/aurora-city-council-passes-data-center-moratorium-through-september-2027/), council members present at the November 23 meeting voted unanimously for the partial pause after earlier attempts failed in August and October (Councilmember Curtis Gardner was excused). The moratorium bars new data center applications west and south of the intersection of Interstate 70 and Powhaton Road, referred to in agenda language cited by [LandmAIn](https://landmain.io/resources/aurora-data-center-moratorium-trident-site-dropped/) as the proposed Aerotropolis Parkway interchange, until after September 30, 2027, or until the council enacts permanent land-use rules.

Two categories remain outside the freeze:

- Vacant land on Aurora's far eastern edge north and east of the I-70 boundary stays open to new filings under existing zoning.
- Applications already accepted by the city before the vote are exempt, meaning the moratorium itself would not have blocked Trident.

Instead, neighborhood opposition killed the East 48th Avenue site. [CBS Colorado](https://www.cbsnews.com/colorado/news/aurora-passes-partial-data-center-moratorium-trident-cancels-site-plans/) reported that residents from the Windler, Green Valley Ranch, and Reserve communities filled council chambers to protest water use, diesel generator emissions, and noise next to homes and a senior community, only for City Attorney Peter Schulte to announce mid-meeting that Vantage had dropped the parcel.

"In response to the recent community concerns, Vantage voluntarily initiated a review of alternative sites, and the examination of viable alternatives continues," Misty Allen, Vantage's vice president of public policy for North America, said in a statement quoted by [Sentinel Colorado](https://sentinelcolorado.com/metro/aurora-city-council-passes-data-center-moratorium-through-september-2027/).

Council members also took initial unanimous action on a separate ordinance banning future sales and use tax rebates for data centers. That measure, which requires one more vote before taking effect, leaves four existing city incentive agreements intact. Those agreements carry up to roughly $83 million in combined rebates, including up to $38.9 million tied to Trident over 10 years if it met a 50-job threshold.

### By the numbers

- **334 MW**: Planned computing capacity for Trident cited by Vantage in [Sentinel Colorado's September 2026 project breakdown](https://sentinelcolorado.com/metro/proposed-trident-data-center-proposal-in-northeast-sparks-anger-among-residents/), more than double the 160 MW QTS campus that currently ranks as Colorado's largest data center.
- **Up to 500 MW**: Total campus power demand cited across local development tracking, including [LandmAIn](https://landmain.io/resources/aurora-data-center-moratorium-trident-site-dropped/).
- **~860,000 sq ft**: Planned building footprint for Trident on East 48th Avenue near E-470, roughly a quarter-mile from homes in The Reserve.
- **28**: Emergency diesel generators planned alongside battery storage for the Trident facility.
- **September 30, 2027**: Expiration date of Aurora's partial moratorium unless permanent zoning standards pass sooner.
- **750 feet / 0.5 mile**: Draft buffer rules under council review, banning new facilities within 750 feet of residential zoning and requiring conditional-use permits out to half a mile.
- **~$83 million**: Maximum tax rebates Aurora already committed across four existing data center agreements, including **$38.9 million** earmarked for Trident.

### Why this matters

Trident's collapse shows how residential proximity and backup power infrastructure now carry veto power even when a developer holds valid zoning. The East 48th Avenue parcel sat inside Aurora's Airport District, where data centers were permitted "by right." That designation allowed administrative review only, with no mandatory public hearing. Residents discovered the 860,000-square-foot proposal only after site-plan reviews were underway in September 2026, sparking a petition with more than 2,500 signatures.

Cooling and generator demands drove the conflict. In September 2026, Aurora unanimously banned water-intensive evaporative cooling systems for new facilities; Vantage planned closed-loop cooling and battery storage to cut water draw and generator count, yet still proposed 28 backup diesel generators next to housing. Even closed-loop campuses draw hundreds of megawatts from regional grids, forcing utilities to build substations and transmission capacity while neighbors bear diesel testing noise and industrial land conversion.

### Context

Aurora's freeze fits a pattern of Colorado Front Range and national municipal pushback. Neighboring Brighton enacted a one-year data center ban in October 2026, and Arapahoe County is rewriting its own zoning standards, narrowing where displaced megawatt-scale campuses can relocate without crossing into another jurisdiction's buffer zone.

Across the United States, local bans and zoning rewrites have accelerated throughout 2026. Earlier this year, communities staged [142 data center protests across 42 states](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/us-data-center-protests-go-national), while [Tucson adopted strict water and noise rules](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/tucson-large-scale-datacenter-regulations) for large-scale campuses and [voters in Wrightstown, Wisconsin, rejected municipal utility support](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/wrightstown-data-center-referendum-defeat) for a proposed AI facility. As [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/colorado-aurora-data-center-moratorium-trident-12762912) noted in its report on the Aurora vote, local officials are increasingly concluding that temporary construction jobs (estimated at 1,500 to more than 3,000 during Trident's buildout versus 30 to 50 permanent roles) no longer justify unvetted industrial sitings near suburbs.

## Risks and open questions

- **Displacement to eastern plains and neighboring counties**: Exempting land north and east of I-70 and Powhaton Road protects existing suburbs today, but residential subdivisions approved on paper could still end up adjacent to new builds if permanent buffers omit planned housing.
- **Where Trident's 334 MW to 500 MW load goes next**: Vantage remains headquartered in Denver and says it still wants a Colorado site. Moving farther east keeps the load on the same regional Front Range grid, shifting land-use friction without reducing total electricity demand.
- **Tax-incentive carveouts**: Aurora's pending rebate ban applies only to future applicants, leaving up to $83 million in previously approved incentives untouched unless existing recipients fail to hit job targets or abandon their sites.

## What happens next

Aurora City Council is scheduled to take its second and final vote on the data center tax-incentive ban in the coming weeks while drafting permanent code changes ahead of the September 30, 2027 moratorium deadline. Those draft rules include the 750-foot residential setback, half-mile conditional-use ring, mandatory noise and low-frequency vibration studies, and potential community benefit agreements. Vantage has not yet named an alternative site for the Trident campus.
