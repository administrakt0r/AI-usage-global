## Nscale's Norway AI Data Center Delayed Six Months

### Setbacks at 30 MW Glomfjord site highlight operational challenges in European AI expansion

Nscale's 30-megawatt AI data center project in Glomfjord, Norway, is running six months behind schedule following a transformer fire, cooling system leaks, and construction hurdles, according to Bloomberg reports and public records.

## Key details

Only 6.4 MW of the planned 30 MW capacity is currently operational at the Glomfjord facility in northern Norway. Construction delays prompted Nscale to tow in the Kalmar floating hotel to house workers nearby in an effort to accelerate progress.

The site experienced a transformer fire in December 2025 that destroyed switchgear, forcing the operator to rely on temporary generators to power cooling systems. Additionally, leaks in the cooling system required weeks of remediation, though Nscale sources noted the cooling installation was completed three months ago without customer penalties.

Despite $103 billion in total contracted value—driven by major agreements with AI firms including Anthropic and Microsoft—Nscale's financial disclosures reveal growing capital pressures. For the six months ending June 2026, Nscale reported $140.6 million in revenue (a 1,252 percent increase year-over-year) alongside losses of $1.02 billion, up from $368.9 million in the prior-year period.

### Why this matters

The delays in Glomfjord illustrate the mounting operational and infrastructure risks facing neocloud providers as they scale gigawatt-scale AI compute commitments. Power distribution failures, specialized cooling leaks, and permitting backlogs directly impair revenue realization while capital expenditures and operating losses escalate sharply.

### Context

Nscale is preparing for an initial public offering seeking valuations up to $35 billion, having published its SEC IPO prospectus listing construction delays and permitting hurdles as core risk factors. The sector faces broader market turbulence: Australian neocloud Firmus recently canceled its IPO citing market volatility, after delivering only 43 MW of a planned 1.6 GW Australian expansion.

## What happens next

Nscale is working to bring the remaining 23.6 MW of capacity online at Glomfjord while preparing for its planned public market debut. Investors and industry analysts will closely monitor whether Nscale can meet delivery milestones across its 12 additional data center development projects to fulfill its multi-billion-dollar hyperscale compute contracts.
