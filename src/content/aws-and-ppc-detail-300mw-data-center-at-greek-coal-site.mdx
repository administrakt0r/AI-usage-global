## AWS and PPC Detail 300MW Data Center at Greek Coal Site

### A 737-page environmental filing outlines plans for four buildings, 203,000 cubic meters of annual cooling water, and an expansion route to 1GW in Western Macedonia

Amazon Web Services and Greece's state-controlled utility, Public Power Corporation (PPC), have submitted a 737-page environmental impact assessment for a planned 300-megawatt data center campus in Kozani. The project repurposes the brownfield site of the retired 1,550 MW Agios Dimitrios lignite power station, consuming up to 203,000 cubic meters of water each year for cooling while tapping heavy transmission links left behind by Greece's largest coal plant.

## Key details

The environmental filing, reported by Greek business publication [OT.gr](https://www.ot.gr/2026/11/23/epixeiriseis/dei-i-energeiaki-ypodomi-tou-data-center-i-pista-tis-amazon-kai-i-simasia-tis-psyksis/) and [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/details-shared-of-aws-300mw-data-center-in-kozani-greece/), details four identical data halls across a 45-hectare plot in northern Greece. Each 11-meter-tall building will contain five data halls rated at 12 MW of IT load, creating a combined 240 MW IT payload. Electrical losses, auxiliary loads, and mechanical cooling push total maximum site demand to 300 MW.

To cool the servers, the facility uses a hybrid design. Air cooling will handle heat rejection when ambient temperatures remain low, covering 10% to 40% of operations with zero water loss. When outside temperatures rise above 25 degrees Celsius (77 degrees Fahrenheit), an evaporative wet-cooling loop will engage. The filing estimates this hybrid configuration cuts annual water consumption by 133,000 cubic meters compared to a continuous wet-cooling system, capping full-load freshwater withdrawal at roughly 203,000 cubic meters annually from the nearby Polyfytos reservoir on the Aliakmonas River.

PPC is committing €1 billion ($1.1 billion) to the civil and energy infrastructure, while AWS will invest a matching €1 billion for cloud hardware and networking equipment. Grid connection requires two new underground 400-kilovolt lines running to the existing Agios Dimitrios substation, avoiding overhead towers and reusing transmission headroom freed by the coal plant's closure earlier this year.

### By the numbers

- **300 MW**: Total maximum grid power capacity planned for the Phase 1 campus.
- **240 MW**: Dedicated IT computing load across 20 data halls in four buildings.
- **203,000 cubic meters**: Estimated annual water consumption under full load (about 53.6 million US gallons).
- **1,000 MW**: Exclusive expansion capacity option held by AWS through May 2027.
- **€2 billion**: Total initial investment split equally between PPC and AWS ($2.2 billion).
- **45 hectares**: Site footprint occupying the former Agios Dimitrios lignite station grounds.

### Why this matters

Repurposing shuttered coal stations has become a primary land-and-power acquisition strategy for hyperscalers facing severe utility interconnection delays across Western Europe and North America. By siting the facility at Agios Dimitrios, AWS bypasses the multi-year queues typical of greenfield grid applications, securing direct access to a 400 kV extra-high-voltage node that previously evacuated 1.55 GW of thermal generation.

The cooling disclosure highlights the persistent trade-off between power efficiency and local watershed demand. In Mediterranean climates, operators often rely on evaporative water cooling to lower server temperatures and prevent electrical efficiency penalties during summer heat waves. Sourcing 203,000 cubic meters annually from Lake Polyfytos draws on a shared basin that also supplies regional irrigation and drinking networks across Western Macedonia.

### Context

AWS has faced growing scrutiny over its operational footprint as generative AI workloads increase electricity and water demands. In earlier sustainability filings, [Amazon reported consuming billions of gallons of water](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/amazon-first-data-center-water-disclosure) across its global data center fleet, while its corporate emissions [climbed due to infrastructure buildouts](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/amazon-2025-emissions-surge-ai-boom). 

To balance the regional power load, Amazon previously contracted four Power Purchase Agreements in Greece covering roughly 110 MW of wind generation, including the Vermio, Mesokorfi, and Koukouras wind farms. However, matching a round-the-clock 300 MW baseload profile with intermittent wind will require PPC to coordinate broader storage and hydro assets from its portfolio.

## Risks and open questions

The environmental assessment does not resolve how the site will handle waste heat disposal if local district heating networks cannot absorb the thermal output. While the filing notes potential heat reuse for municipal buildings, regional greenhouses, or district heating in Kozani, no binding off-take contracts have been signed.

A larger question surrounds Phase 2. Amazon holds an exclusive option until late May 2027 to expand the campus to a 1,000 MW gigawatt-scale facility. Scaling to 1 GW would quadruple water withdrawals and require significant new power generation in a region transitioning away from fossil fuels. If Amazon declines the option, PPC retains the right to market the remaining 700 MW to other hyperscalers.

## What happens next

Greece's transmission system operator, IPTO (ADMIE), is scheduled to issue formal grid connection terms in late 2026. PPC and AWS plan to finalize binding contracts for the 300 MW project during the first quarter of 2027. If signed on schedule, equipment procurement begins in spring 2027, followed by ground-breaking in summer 2027 and commercial commissioning by late 2028.
