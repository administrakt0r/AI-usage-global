## Microsoft Files for 422MW Data Center Campus in Georgia

### Hyperscaler plans $360M three-building facility in Floyd County

Microsoft has filed a Development of Regional Impact application to construct a 422-megawatt data center campus in Floyd County, Georgia. The expansion highlights the continued strain AI infrastructure requirements place on regional power grids and municipal water supplies across the southeastern United States.

## Key details

According to filings submitted on October 20, 2026, the proposed campus—dubbed ATL80—will span 349 acres at 0 Huffaker Road in Rome, Georgia. Microsoft originally acquired the site in 2023 under the codename "Project Firecracker." The development plan features three data center buildings encompassing 1,659,000 square feet (154,126 square meters) of total floor space.

Microsoft estimates the completed project will represent a $360 million capital investment and generate $35 million in annual local tax revenue for Floyd County. Utility provider Georgia Power is slated to supply the site with 422 megawatts of electrical capacity. Filings also indicate that the facility will require a maximum daily water supply of 13,500 gallons (51,103 liters) to support operations. Construction is projected for completion by 2032, with an expected operational staff of 150 workers.

### Why this matters

The 422MW power commitment for ATL80 underscores how hyperscale AI workloads continue to drive massive utility allocation requests in Georgia. Securing hundreds of megawatts for a single facility compresses available grid capacity and necessitates infrastructure expansions, raising questions regarding long-term regional electricity rates and power distribution stability.

### Context

This filing follows Microsoft's September 2026 application for ATL50, a 910,000-square-foot campus in Union City, Georgia, and aligns with its broader buildout across the state, including active developments in Fairburn, Douglasville, Palmetto, East Point, and Tyrone. As hyperscalers expand outward from primary hubs like Atlanta into surrounding counties, local utilities face growing cumulative demands on electricity grids and regional water resources.

## What happens next

The Development of Regional Impact application will undergo interagency review by regional authorities and local governments in Northwest Georgia to evaluate environmental, traffic, and utility impacts. Regional planning bodies and Georgia Power will review grid integration timelines as Microsoft targets completion by 2032.
