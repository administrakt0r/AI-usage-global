## Meta Tests Data Center Robotics to Cut AI Infrastructure Costs

### Tech giant evaluates robotic arms and autonomous systems across hyperscale facilities to automate maintenance and lower operational expenses.

Meta is actively testing robotic automation across its hyperscale data centers to automate server maintenance, cabling, and power management. As the company rapidly scales its AI infrastructure, integrating specialized robotics aims to curb operational costs and address technician shortages in rural facility hubs.

## Key details

Reports reveal that Meta is deploying robotic hardware from vendors including Kinova, ABB, and Watney Robotics across key facilities, such as its Altoona, Iowa campus and New Albany, Ohio location. The tests evaluate robotic systems for delicate server management tasks previously reserved for human technicians.

At its Altoona facility, Meta is testing a Kinova Gen3 robotic arm for power-cycling servers and cutting electricity to individual units during maintenance. Additionally, dual-armed Watney robots are being trialed for swapping networking cables, with internal estimates suggesting autonomous systems could automate up to 80% of routine technician workloads. In Ohio, four-wheeled ABB robots equipped with scissor lifts and six-axis arms are being deployed to reseat hardware components.

The automation drive comes as hyperscalers face mounting labor constraints and escalating operational expenditures driven by massive AI cluster deployments. While specialized tasks—such as high-density cabling for Nvidia GB300 supercomputers—still present technical hurdles, robotics offer consistent operation at higher thermal thresholds without requiring human ambient cooling or lighting.

### Why this matters

Operating hyperscale AI infrastructure demands continuous maintenance and massive capital expenditure. Automating physical server management directly reduces long-term operational overhead while enabling data centers to operate at elevated temperatures, lowering facility cooling and energy costs.

### Context

The deployment of robotics fits into a broader industry push to optimize AI data center economics. With hyperscalers investing tens of billions of dollars in hardware, automation allows companies to scale facility operations efficiently in remote locations where skilled technical labor is scarce.

## What happens next

Meta plans to expand its robotics testing across additional hyperscale campuses while developing AI-driven maintenance software. Industry analysts will monitor whether automated maintenance enables higher-density server configurations and reduced thermal cooling requirements in upcoming data center builds.
