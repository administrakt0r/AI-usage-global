## Google Backstops Debt Financing for TeraWulf AI Campus

### Tech Giant Guarantees Debt for 480MW Kentucky Facility

Google has agreed to serve as an investment-grade financial credit backstop for TeraWulf's 480-megawatt Justified Data Campus in Hawesville, Kentucky. The move underpins a 20-year, $19 billion lease agreement with artificial intelligence lab Anthropic, demonstrating how hyperscalers are using balance-sheet strength to finance energy-intensive AI infrastructure.

## Key details

Data center infrastructure operator TeraWulf announced that Google will provide credit support to backstop debt financing for the development of its Justified Data Campus in Hancock County, Kentucky. Google holds senior unsecured debt ratings of AA+ from S&P Global Ratings and Aa2 from Moody's, effectively guaranteeing project debt should TeraWulf default and lowering the developer's overall borrowing costs.

The 790-acre campus is being constructed on a former aluminum smelting property purchased from Century Aluminum, which ceased industrial operations in 2022 after more than five decades. The facility has an anticipated capacity of 480 megawatts (MW), with the first building scheduled to come online in 2027. Anthropic previously committed to a 20-year lease valued at $19 billion for capacity across the campus, while TeraWulf sought roughly $3.5 billion in debt financing to fund ongoing construction. TeraWulf also finalized a definitive engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) contract with Fluor Corporation to build three data center facilities on the site.

### Why this matters

The agreement highlights the immense capital intensity required to build out multi-hundred-megawatt power infrastructure for frontier artificial intelligence models. As interest rates and construction expenses fluctuate, pure-play infrastructure developers face steep borrowing premiums unless supported by investment-grade balance sheets.

By stepping in as a debt guarantor, Google enables TeraWulf to secure lower financing rates for 480 MW of power capacity, ensuring that Anthropic receives its planned compute allocation without destabilizing project economics. It also reflects how hyperscalers act as quasi-utilities and financiers to secure physical energy capacity before regional grids face further allocation constraints.

### Context

TeraWulf has actively expanded its footprint across Kentucky, developing both the 480 MW Justified Data Campus and the planned 1-gigawatt Muskie Data Campus. The company previously partnered with Google on a joint venture with Fluidstack in Texas, establishing a pattern of tech giants providing financial backstops to accelerate large-load deployments.

The arrangement comes amid broader market scrutiny regarding circular financing and speculative debt in the AI infrastructure sector. Financial institutions, including the Bank of England and the Bank for International Settlements, have recently highlighted the risks of multi-billion-dollar credit facilities tied to data center assets, where power access and off-taker commitments dictate long-term project solvency.

## Risks and open questions

The credit support mechanism concentrates financial risk among major tech corporations and large debt syndicates if AI compute returns fail to meet revenue expectations. While Google's high credit ratings insulate lenders, default scenarios could leave backstoppers holding specialized high-voltage infrastructure assets.

Additionally, regional utilities and grid operators face ongoing challenges managing 480 MW loads alongside existing residential and commercial power demand. Hancock County and regional energy providers must balance transmission line capacity, substation buildouts, and reserve margins as heavy computing loads come online in 2027.

## What happens next

TeraWulf and Fluor will proceed with initial construction phases across the 790-acre site ahead of the planned 2027 operational launch for the first facility. Lenders and credit syndicates will finalize terms for TeraWulf's anticipated $3.5 billion debt package backed by Google's credit guarantee.

Regulators and utility commissions across the PJM and MISO grid boundaries will continue to monitor large-scale load interconnection requests, with other AI developers likely seeking similar corporate credit guarantees to de-risk capital-heavy data center developments.
