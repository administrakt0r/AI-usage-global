## Tucson Approves Strict Large-Scale Data Center Regulations

### New rules mandate water, power, and noise disclosures for facilities exceeding 20 MW

On August 5, 2026, the Tucson Mayor and Council voted 6-1 to adopt some of the most stringent regulations in the United States for large-scale data centers. The new Unified Development Code amendment establishes clear municipal guardrails for facilities exceeding 20 megawatts of energy or 25,000 square feet. This landmark decision marks a significant shift toward local oversight of artificial intelligence infrastructure and its massive resource demands.

## Key details

Under the new regulations, large-scale data centers will transition from being virtually unregulated locally to becoming among the most highly regulated land uses in the City of Tucson. Key elements of the Unified Development Code (UDC) amendment include:

* **Definition of Facilities:** Large-scale data centers are defined as any contiguous site with a gross floor area greater than 25,000 square feet or an energy use exceeding 20 megawatts (MW).
* **Power and Water Mandates:** Operators must prove access to adequate water and electrical supply. Additionally, they must disclose their full energy mix, including the percentage of renewable energy sources utilized.
* **Zoning Restrictions:** Facilities will no longer be allowed by right or special exception. Instead, they must secure a rezoning or a major amendment through Planned Area Development (PAD) or Planned Community Development (PCD) processes, which require public hearings and final approval by the Mayor and Council.
* **Noise and Environmental Mitigations:** Applicants are required to conduct comprehensive sound impact studies, install physical noise barriers, and adhere to strict operational decibel limits.
* **Landscaping and Off-site Protection:** Projects must include enhanced green space and landscaping around the facilities. The rules also offer the Mayor and Council the option to mandate the protection of sensitive lands off-site at a 2:1 ratio.

### Why this matters

As artificial intelligence model training and inference drive an unprecedented global buildout of high-density compute infrastructure, local governments are bearing the brunt of the environmental and utility strain. Tucson’s strict regulations force tech companies to be completely transparent about their utility consumption. By requiring developer disclosures of their energy and water mix, the city sets a precedent that prevents AI operators from quietly monopolizing local grids and water tables.

### Context

Tucson's year-long public process reflects a wider national backlash against unmitigated data center expansion. Across the United States, states like New York have proposed total moratoriums, and Texas has halted new interconnections to audit grid capacity. By choosing rigorous, structure-based regulation over an outright ban, Tucson is establishing a repeatable framework for municipal zoning that accommodates high-tech growth without compromising community values, especially in water-stressed desert environments.

## Risks and open questions

While the regulations provide robust guardrails, several open questions remain. It is unclear if the strict noise and aesthetic standards will discourage AI developers from investing in Tucson altogether, potentially shifting development to neighboring counties with laxer rules. Additionally, enforcing compliance with operational noise limits and auditing complex energy mix disclosures will require technical expertise and municipal resources that the city's Planning and Development Services Department must now establish.

## What happens next

The newly approved regulations will officially go into effect on September 4, 2026. Any new large-scale data center project proposed after this date must undergo the full public review process, including neighborhood coordination, review by the Zoning Examiner, and a public hearing before the Mayor and Council. Observers will watch how this code amendment impacts Tucson's regional economy and whether neighboring municipalities adopt similar strict zoning ordinances.
