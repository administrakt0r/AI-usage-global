## UK Green Party Calls for Nationwide Data Center Moratorium

### Proposed policy would restrict new facilities to small-scale local projects to curb grid strain and emissions

The UK Green Party has officially called for a nationwide moratorium on new hyperscale data center construction across Great Britain. Party leadership urged ministers to halt large-scale infrastructure approvals and permit only small, community-focused facilities until stringent environmental and energy efficiency standards are enacted.

## Key details

Under the proposed policy, the government would immediately suspend planning consent for all commercial hyperscale data center facilities. The Green Party's framework would restrict future development exclusively to "local scale data centers for the local community," designed to serve immediate municipal needs rather than international AI model training and inference workloads.

The call for a moratorium comes as the UK power grid faces an unprecedented backlog of connection requests. Official figures indicate that data centers currently account for several gigawatts of committed grid capacity, with total connection queues topping 73 GW of prospective demand. Green Party spokespersons emphasized that unconstrained data center expansion threatens to consume a disproportionate share of the nation's clean electricity generation, undermining national net-zero carbon targets and driving up wholesale power costs for domestic consumers.

### Why this matters

As artificial intelligence infrastructure expands rapidly, political pushback against hyperscale data centers is escalating from local zoning disputes to national party platforms. The UK Green Party's proposal targets the core resource requirements of AI computing—namely massive electricity draw, grid capacity reservation, and localized environmental degradation. If adopted or integrated into parliamentary debate, such policies could significantly increase regulatory risk and deployment timelines for tech companies and cloud providers seeking to build AI hubs in Western Europe.

### Context

The proposal reflects a growing international trend of legislative and regulatory intervention in AI infrastructure development. Across the United States and Europe, jurisdictions including New York, Seattle, and Reno have enacted or proposed data center moratoriums to assess cumulative impacts on water tables, electrical grids, and utility rates. In the UK specifically, energy regulator Ofgem recently initiated reforms to clear "phantom" data center projects from connection queues, illustrating the systemic pressure AI workloads exert on national energy infrastructure.

## What happens next

The Green Party intends to introduce the moratorium policy as an amendment during upcoming parliamentary debates on energy and infrastructure planning. While the governing party holds a legislative majority, the proposal is expected to amplify pressure on Ofgem and the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero to enforce stricter environmental criteria, mandatory heat reuse, and on-site renewable energy requirements for future data center permits.
