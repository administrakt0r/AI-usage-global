## Google Invests $160 Million in Malaysian Water Treatment

### Tech giant funds public and data center water works in Negeri Sembilan

Google has committed $160 million to build shared water infrastructure in Negeri Sembilan, Malaysia, to support its upcoming hyperscale data center campus. The investment funds an off-river storage reservoir and drinking water treatment plant designed to serve both the surrounding community and Google's computing facilities.

## Key details

The $160 million capital project is being developed in partnership with the state government of Negeri Sembilan and local water utility Syarikat Air Negeri Sembilan Sdn Bhd. Engineering and construction firm Gamuda Berhad has been selected to execute the infrastructure works.

The off-river storage reservoir and treatment plant in Port Dickson will have a daily capacity to produce 65 million liters (approximately 17.17 million gallons) of treated drinking water. The system will supply the planned 389-acre data center campus, which is projected to host between 500 megawatts and 1 gigawatt of IT load, while feeding potable water into the municipal distribution grid.

Google stated the funding aligns with its corporate goal to replenish 120 percent of the freshwater volume it consumes globally. The hyperscaler also committed to paying for 100 percent of the power consumed by the campus and all direct transmission grid interconnection costs.

### Why this matters

Data center cooling demands place immense stress on local municipal supplies, particularly as high-density AI clusters push water evaporation rates upward. Hyperscalers expanding across Southeast Asia face intense scrutiny over whether compute infrastructure will divert water from households and agriculture.

By directly funding municipal-scale intake and treatment facilities, Google is attempting to neutralize local pushback by generating net-positive potable water supplies for the host community rather than drawing down existing public utilities.

### Context

Port Dickson sits approximately 90 kilometers south of Kuala Lumpur along the Strait of Malacca, forming part of Google's broader $2 billion investment push in Malaysia. The company previously broke ground on a 100MW data center development at Elmina Business Park in Selangor.

Across the data center industry, water availability has emerged alongside electricity transmission queues as a primary bottleneck for new hyperscale campuses. Communities from the American West to Southern Europe have enacted moratoriums over data center water use, prompting operators to explore shared infrastructure investments, closed-loop cooling, and off-river storage reserves.

## Risks and open questions

While Google's assessment indicated that local water sources in Negeri Sembilan are not under acute scarcity risks, operating a campus scaling up to 1GW will draw substantial baseline resource volumes once fully fitted with high-density server racks.

Questions remain regarding whether the 65-million-liter daily capacity will comfortably absorb future industrial expansions in the Port Dickson corridor, and how wastewater discharge and cooling tower blowdown will be managed to protect nearby coastal ecosystems.

## What happens next

Gamuda Berhad will proceed with civil engineering and construction on the off-river reservoir and treatment plant alongside site preparation on Google's 389-acre Port Dickson land parcel.

Regulators and regional utilities will monitor water flow metrics and grid connections as the first phase of the development approaches commissioning.
