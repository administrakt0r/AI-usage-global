## Crusoe Raises $3.9 Billion for Modular 'Spark' AI Factories

### Fresh Series F capital funds truck-transportable compute units to bypass grid and community constraints

Data center developer Crusoe has raised $3.9 billion in a Series F funding round, pushing its valuation to $30.9 billion. The capital infusion will fund both large-scale facilities and manufactured modular AI factories designed for rapid, flexible deployment near available power sources.

## Key details

The $3.9 billion Series F round was co-led by Atreides Management, Mubadala Capital, and Valor Equity Partners, with participation from Nvidia, Founders Fund, GIC, Qatar Investment Authority, Radical Ventures, and TPG. The financing follows a $1.38 billion raise at a $10 billion valuation ten months earlier.

Crusoe plans to use the funds to expand major infrastructure projects, including its Abilene, Texas data center campus servicing OpenAI, alongside its proprietary "Spark" modular units. Manufactured in-house, these self-contained AI factories can be transported by truck and connected to large power sources in diverse locations, eliminating multi-year site construction and heavy on-site workforces.

### Why this matters

The deployment of truck-transportable modular compute units represents a strategic shift in addressing power availability bottlenecks and community resistance. By delivering factory-built infrastructure directly to stranded or remote power supplies, developers can bypass multi-year electrical grid interconnection queues and avoid local zoning opposition against static mega-campuses.

### Context

Crusoe operates a three-pronged revenue model: leasing data center shell capacity, renting out proprietary GPU clusters, and selling inference compute power. The company has secured high-density compute contracts across major tech players, including Meta, Microsoft, Oracle, and a recently reported $13 billion, five-year cloud deal with quantitative trading firm Jane Street.

## Risks and open questions

While modular units minimize on-site construction delays, connecting gigawatt-scale aggregate capacity via dispersed modules creates complex power management and fuel delivery logistics. Furthermore, remote power connections must still navigate local environmental standards and long-term fuel availability.

## What happens next

Crusoe is preparing for an initial public offering following recent discussions with major financial advisers, while scaling production of its modular units and expanding infrastructure across its North American sites.
