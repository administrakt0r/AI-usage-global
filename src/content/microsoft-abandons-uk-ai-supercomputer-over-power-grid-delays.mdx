## Microsoft Abandons UK AI Supercomputer Over Power Grid Delays

### Power grid transmission backlog pushes expected 90 MW data center startup to the 2030s

Microsoft has terminated its agreement with British AI infrastructure startup Nscale for a planned flagship data center in Loughton, Essex, after learning that necessary electric power grid capacity would not be available until the 2030s. The decision underlines growing infrastructure bottlenecks as massive compute cluster power demands outpace national electrical transmission capacity.

## Key details

The Loughton facility was designed as a 50-megawatt (MW) data center with expansion capability up to 90 MW, intended to house 23,040 Nvidia GB300 GPUs for Microsoft's Azure cloud service. Under the initial September 2025 announcement, the facility was scheduled to begin operations and receive its first chips in early 2027. However, local distribution operator UK Power Networks warned Nscale that while connection to the regional distribution network is feasible, required upgrades to the national high-voltage transmission grid would delay full power delivery until the early to mid-2030s.

Because Microsoft was the anchor customer for what Nscale marketed as the UK's largest sovereign AI supercomputer campus, its withdrawal leaves the site without a major tenant. Nscale stated that it remains committed to developing the site using alternative power sources while pursuing other UK expansion locations. The site itself had previously faced delays, having remained in use as a scaffolding yard through early 2026 before land clearing commenced.

### Why this matters

The cancellation illustrates how electrical grid capacity and transmission buildout timelines have become the primary bottleneck for hyperscale AI deployment. Despite multi-billion-dollar capital commitments and available server hardware, AI projects cannot materialize without high-voltage grid infrastructure capable of supplying continuous tens-of-megawatts loads. When connection timelines extend to nearly a decade, hyperscalers are forced to reallocate capital to regions with faster power availability or behind-the-meter generation options.

### Context

This exit represents a significant setback for the UK government's ambition to position the country as an AI infrastructure hub. The Loughton project was touted as a centerpiece of Microsoft's £22 billion UK investment plan announced in late 2025. Across the UK, power connection requests from data centers surged from 41 GW to over 80 GW within a single year, prompting energy regulator Ofgem to introduce stricter financial requirements to clear speculative "phantom" applications from the grid queue. Similar power grid delays in Northern Virginia, Texas, and parts of Europe are driving operators toward off-grid gas generation, fuel cells, or nuclear power partnerships.

## What happens next

Nscale is currently evaluating alternative on-site power generation and microgrid options to proceed with the Loughton development without relying solely on the national grid. Meanwhile, UK energy regulators and network operators continue implementing grid queue reforms to prioritize viable projects. Industry observers will monitor whether Microsoft reallocates its UK compute capacity to other European markets or behind-the-meter projects with faster grid interconnection timelines.
