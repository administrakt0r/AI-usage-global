## Oracle Issues Force Majeure Notice for $165 Billion AI Campus

### Rejection of gas pipeline extension threatens timeline for 2.5 gigawatt Project Jupiter facility in New Mexico

Oracle has issued a formal force majeure notice to alternative asset manager Blue Owl Capital regarding its 2.5-gigawatt Project Jupiter data center campus in New Mexico. The legal maneuver seeks to protect Oracle from potential cost surges and defer contractual payments if the $165 billion facility fails to meet its planned 2028 operational target following state regulatory rejections of required gas pipeline infrastructure.

## Key details

Project Jupiter represents one of the largest planned artificial intelligence compute installations in the world, spanning 1,400 acres and four primary data center buildings. The contractual and regulatory standoff highlights severe energy delivery challenges:

- **Force majeure notice:** Oracle issued the force majeure notice to Blue Owl Capital—which acquired lead developer Stack Infrastructure in 2024—seeking to defer financial obligations rather than forfeit its tenancy if construction delays occur.
- **Pipeline permit rejections:** State officials rejected a natural gas pipeline extension required to fuel the campus's primary power generation in July, following an initial application denial in March.
- **Capital investment scale:** Stack Infrastructure and BorderPlex Digital Assets previously announced plans to invest up to $165 billion in the 2.5 GW campus, with expected economic benefits for New Mexico estimated at $4.7 billion.
- **Clean energy and research pivots:** Oracle recently issued a Request for Proposals for 2 GW of new renewable energy capacity in New Mexico and pledged $1 million for carbon capture research to support power delivery at Project Jupiter.

### Why this matters

The force majeure declaration underscores the mounting friction between hyperscale AI compute ambitions and regional energy delivery timelines. When multi-gigawatt facilities cannot secure immediate utility grid interconnections or pipeline permits for natural gas generation, project timelines collapse under financial pressure. By invoking force majeure, Oracle is attempting to reallocate financial liability for infrastructure and permitting delays inherent in gigawatt-scale AI developments.

### Context

Oracle was announced as the primary tenant for Project Jupiter in January 2026. The facility was designed to utilize on-site gas generation and fuel cell microgrids to bypass grid interconnections. However, state regulators in New Mexico have increasingly scrutinized fossil fuel infrastructure expansions feeding data centers, mirroring broader national pushback against dedicated fossil generation for AI workloads.

## Risks and open questions

It remains unclear whether Blue Owl Capital will accept Oracle's force majeure declaration or challenge the invocation, which could trigger complex legal disputes over liability for delayed capital deployment. Furthermore, while Oracle maintains that Project Jupiter remains on schedule for 2028, securing alternative energy solutions—such as procuring 2 GW of utility-scale renewables—within four years presents significant transmission and supply chain hurdles in the Southwest.

## What happens next

Oracle, Stack Infrastructure, and Blue Owl Capital will navigate contractual terms regarding the force majeure claim while seeking alternative power arrangements for Project Jupiter. Federal and state regulators in New Mexico are expected to review subsequent energy and environmental filings, which will test whether utility-scale solar, wind, and fuel cell combinations can replace rejected gas pipeline infrastructure.
