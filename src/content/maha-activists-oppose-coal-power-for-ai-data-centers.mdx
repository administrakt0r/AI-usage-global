## MAHA Activists Urge Trump to Reject Coal Power for AI Data Centers

### Advocates warn that relying on coal power to fuel AI infrastructure expansion threatens public health and environmental progress.

Nearly 200 activists aligned with Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s "Make America Healthy Again" movement sent a joint letter to President Donald Trump criticizing federal efforts to promote coal power for artificial intelligence data centers. The letter marks a major mobilization of health advocates challenging energy policy choices driven by surging AI electricity demand.

## Key details

A coalition of nearly 200 health and environmental advocates, including prominent figures Zen Honeycutt and Charles Eisenstein, sent a letter on Friday urging the Trump administration to halt policies boosting coal-fired power plants for AI infrastructure. The letter specifically challenges a 2025 executive order designed to bolster coal generation to meet the immense electricity requirements of hyperscale data centers. The activists warn that utilizing fossil fuels to expand AI compute capacity imposes a "toxic burden" on local communities through increased air pollution and hazardous coal ash accumulation.

While acknowledging the strategic importance of national AI development, the coalition requested transparent reviews of data center siting, mandatory power sourcing disclosures, and strict adherence to federal environmental permitting requirements. They urged federal officials to prioritize renewable alternatives, such as solar and geothermal energy, rather than prolonging coal plant operations or restarting retired facilities to support power-hungry data centers.

### Why this matters

AI data center buildouts require gigawatt-scale power commitments, prompting utilities and policymakers to consider keeping carbon-intensive coal plants online. Relying on coal generation to supply continuous baseload power for AI accelerators increases greenhouse gas emissions and toxic particulate matter, reversing progress on grid decarbonization and raising health risks for nearby populations.

### Context

As artificial intelligence workloads grow exponentially, tech companies and utilities face severe grid capacity constraints and multi-year interconnection delays. In response, federal and state policy proposals have increasingly examined legacy fossil fuel assets and behind-the-meter generation to deliver rapid power. However, local communities and advocacy groups across dozens of states have actively pushed back against fossil-fuel-powered AI infrastructure due to escalating utility rates, water depletion, and air quality concerns.

## Risks and open questions

It remains uncertain whether federal agencies will revise environmental review standards or energy permitting pathways for data center interconnections in response to political pressure from health advocates. Additionally, utilities face growing challenges in balancing the immediate, massive power demands of AI hyperscalers with statutory clean energy goals and ratepayer protection mandates.

## What happens next

Federal agencies receiving the petition, including the Department of Energy and Environmental Protection Agency, may face increased scrutiny ahead of upcoming congressional hearings on grid reliability and data center energy impacts. Lawmakers and municipal leaders are expected to continue debating local zoning rules, clean power mandates, and environmental disclosures for future data center developments.
