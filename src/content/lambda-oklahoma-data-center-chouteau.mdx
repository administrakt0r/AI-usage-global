## Lambda Plans Grid-Reactive AI Data Center in Oklahoma

### AI cloud provider targets Chouteau with closed-loop cooling and $500 million in local tax commitments.

AI cloud and GPU compute provider Lambda announced plans to develop a new AI data center in Chouteau, Oklahoma, situated within the MidAmerica Industrial Park. The facility will incorporate closed-loop cooling and a grid-reactive design to reduce resource consumption and ease regional power grid strain.

## Key details

Lambda's planned facility in Mayes County, Oklahoma, is designed to address resource efficiency concerns through closed-loop liquid cooling technology, eliminating constant freshwater withdrawal. Additionally, the project will feature a grid-reactive architecture capable of dialing back non-essential compute loads during periods of peak power demand on the local grid.

Financially, Lambda estimates the facility will generate approximately $500 million in local tax revenue over the next 10 years. Under Oklahoma's Data Center Customer Ratepayer Protection Act, Lambda will fully cover all infrastructure upgrades required for power and site operations, shielding residential ratepayers from subsidizing energy buildout costs. The site in the 9,000-acre MidAmerica Industrial Park—previously prepared in part by Cloverleaf Infrastructure—places Lambda near Google's long-standing Oklahoma data center campus.

### Why this matters

As GPU compute density increases, AI data centers are coming under intense scrutiny for their heavy water and electricity consumption. Lambda’s design commitments reflect a growing industry trend toward waterless or closed-loop cooling architectures and demand-response capabilities. By integrating grid-reactivity and adhering to state ratepayer protection laws, the project highlights how AI infrastructure developers are adapting to local environmental and utility requirements.

### Context

Lambda currently operates out of 15 data center locations across the United States, targeting gigawatts of AI compute capacity by 2030. The announcement comes as Oklahoma and several other U.S. states implement strict ratepayer protection frameworks to ensure hyperscale AI workloads do not trigger residential electricity bill spikes or deplete municipal water reserves.

## What happens next

Lambda will continue local community consultations and finalize engineering designs alongside the Grand River Dam Authority and MidAmerica Industrial Park leaders. As the company prepares for a planned initial public offering (IPO), execution on its Oklahoma site will serve as a key test for its sustainable infrastructure commitments.
