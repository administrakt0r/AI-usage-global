## L&T's LTN Compute to Deploy Nvidia B300 GPUs at 250MW Chennai Campus

### A multi-year contract worth ₹2,500 crore to ₹5,000 crore adds Nvidia B300 accelerators to a 10,000-GPU cluster backed by 150 MVA of grid readiness in southern India.

Larsen & Toubro subsidiary LTN Compute has won a multi-year contract to deploy additional Nvidia B300 graphics processing units at its Chennai campus, expanding a 10,000-GPU cluster for an unnamed inference provider. Disclosed in a November 23 [regulatory filing to BSE India](https://www.bseindia.com/xml-data/corpfiling/AttachLive/6961c994-01be-4b8d-85ac-5c857530ea43.pdf), the engagement falls under L&T's "large" order tier, valued between ₹2,500 crore and ₹5,000 crore. The deal turns Vyoma.AI's 250 MW Phase 1 Chennai site from a construction roadmap into live high-density AI compute capacity.

## Key details

LTN Compute operates as the AI infrastructure subsidiary of Vyoma.AI, a Larsen & Toubro Group company. Under the multi-year agreement, the subsidiary will host additional Nvidia B300 GPUs inside its Chennai Data Center to power a commercial inference platform for training, fine-tuning, and serving specialized AI models.

According to [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ltn-compute-to-deploy-10000-gpu-cluster-at-chennai-data-center/), the Chennai campus already runs a 30 MW live data center alongside 150 MVA of power infrastructure readiness. In October 2026, Vyoma.AI broke ground on an adjacent 52 MW facility at the same site. Together, those builds anchor Phase 1 of what L&T calls its Chennai AI Factory, engineered for 250 MW of total capacity with long-term expansion planned toward a gigawatt-scale hub.

L&T did not name the inference provider or disclose the exact GPU count in this tranche, noting only that the hardware joins the broader 10,000-GPU cluster taking shape in Chennai. As [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/l-t-bags-large-order-worth-5-000-crore-to-deploy-ai-cluster-in-chennai-126112300275_1.html) and [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/lt-bags-large-ai-order-in-chennai-nvidia-b300-deployment-to-join-10000-gpu-cluster) reported following the exchange filing, LTN Compute pairs colocation shells with bare-metal clusters, high-throughput parallel storage, and ultra-low-latency interconnects under Bare Metal-as-a-Service and GPU-as-a-Service contracts.

### By the numbers

- **₹2,500 crore to ₹5,000 crore**: Value band for L&T's "large" order classification on the multi-year engagement.
- **10,000 GPUs**: Total scale of the Chennai campus cluster that the new Nvidia B300 deployment joins.
- **250 MW**: Designed Phase 1 electrical capacity for Vyoma.AI's Chennai AI Factory.
- **150 MVA**: Substation and electrical infrastructure readiness already in place at the campus.
- **52 MW**: Capacity of the second Chennai data center that broke ground in October 2026.
- **30 MW**: Existing operational capacity at the Chennai site, alongside L&T's 2 MW facility in Navi Mumbai.

### Why this matters

Chennai has become a primary landing zone for high-density AI clusters in India because the city pairs subsea cable landings with industrial substation capacity, two assets that take years to permit elsewhere. Securing 150 MVA of electrical readiness ahead of tenant contracts lets LTN Compute absorb power-hungry Nvidia B300 systems without waiting on multi-year grid upgrades.

The contract also shows how India's largest engineering conglomerates are capturing more of the AI power stack. Rather than merely pouring concrete and wiring substations for foreign hyperscalers, L&T is buying accelerators, integrating liquid-cooled server racks, and selling GPU hours directly to commercial inference providers. At 250 MW in Phase 1 alone, the Chennai campus will draw roughly as much electricity at full buildout as hundreds of thousands of Indian households.

### Context

The Chennai expansion lands amid a wave of domestic AI infrastructure bets across India. Earlier this year, HCLTech committed ₹14,257 crore ($1.48 billion) toward a [sovereign AI data center in Bhubaneswar, Odisha](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/hcltech-odisha-sovereign-ai-datacenter), partnering with Sarvam AI on up to 50 MW of planned capacity.

Outside India, regional cloud and energy firms are chasing the same high-density Nvidia B300 hardware cycle. Operators in [Georgia and the United Kingdom](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/georgias-silk-cloud-deploys-nvidia-blackwell-ultra-gpus) recently contracted Nvidia Blackwell Ultra systems while locking in pre-allocated grid connections to sidestep utility queues. In February 2026, L&T pledged to reach 250 MW of domestic data center capacity overall; its 2025 annual review listed two live sites and two more under development.

## Risks and open questions

L&T's filing leaves key technical metrics undisclosed. Neither the exact power draw of the 10,000-GPU cluster nor the cooling architecture and water-withdrawal budget for the 52 MW and 250 MW Chennai expansions were specified. In a coastal metropolis that faces seasonal heat spikes and periodic freshwater stress, running dense Nvidia B300 racks reliably requires either closed-loop liquid cooling or reclaimed industrial water. The company has not yet published those details.

Scaling beyond the 150 MVA currently ready at the substation toward the full 250 MW Phase 1 target, and eventually gigawatt scale, will also depend on Tamil Nadu's state transmission utility delivering additional high-voltage capacity and renewable power purchase agreements on schedule.

## What happens next

LTN Compute will install the contracted Nvidia B300 servers into its Chennai campus under the multi-year rollout while construction continues on the 52 MW hall started in October 2026. Grid watchers and municipal planners will track whether Vyoma.AI discloses power-usage effectiveness, water sourcing, and clean-energy contracts as the campus moves from 82 MW of combined live and under-construction halls toward its 250 MW Phase 1 target.
