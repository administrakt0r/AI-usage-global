## Pallone Proposes National AI Data Center Moratorium

### Energy committee ranking member warns of grid risks and rising costs

Representative Frank Pallone, Jr. (D-NJ) has called for a national moratorium on AI data center construction, citing urgent risks to the U.S. power grid and rising electricity costs. The proposal comes as new research from Berkeley Lab projects that data centers could consume 15% of all U.S. electricity by 2030.

## Key details

During an Energy Subcommittee markup on June 24, 2026, Ranking Member Frank Pallone highlighted that data center energy consumption doubled between 2017 and 2023. He cited a recent Berkeley Lab report indicating that this footprint is set to triple by 2030, potentially reaching 15% of the nation's total electricity use.

Pallone also noted the immediate economic impact on consumers, reporting that mid-Atlantic ratepayers paid over $9 billion last year to fuel the electricity needs of Big Tech's AI ambitions. Beyond energy, the congressman raised alarms over environmental issues including the use of dirty diesel generators, unanalyzed water consumption in stressed regions, and the fast-tracked approval of PFAS chemicals for cooling systems.

### Why this matters

A national moratorium would represent the most significant regulatory intervention in the AI industry to date. It highlights a growing political consensus that the current pace of AI infrastructure expansion may be unsustainable without federal safeguards for energy affordability and environmental protection.

### Context

The call for federal action follows a wave of local moratoriums in New Jersey towns like Asbury Park and Red Bank. It also aligns with recent warnings from the North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC), which identified data centers as a "significant risk" to the reliability of the national power grid due to their massive, concentrated loads.

## What happens next

While Representative Pallone's proposal marks a significant escalation in rhetoric, it faces a complex legislative path. The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) is currently working on new "rules of the road" for grid interconnection, but the debate over a national freeze is likely to intensify as the Energy and Commerce Committee continues its markup of several data center-related bills.
