## Vantage Drops 500MW Colorado Data Center After Aurora Moratorium

### Unanimous city council pause through September 2027 ends developer's 108-acre plan near Denver neighborhoods

Vantage Data Centers abandoned its proposed 500 MW Trident campus in Aurora, Colorado, on November 23, 2026, after months of neighborhood opposition over water, power, and noise. On the same night, the Aurora City Council voted unanimously to pass Resolution R2026-146, pausing new data center applications across western and southern parts of the city through September 30, 2027.

## Key details

Vantage had planned two two-story buildings totaling about 860,000 square feet (80,000 square meters) on 108 acres near East 48th Avenue and E-470, adjacent to the Windler and Green Valley Ranch communities. According to [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/vantage-drops-plans-for-trident-data-center-after-colorados-aurora-city-passes-moratorium/), the campus was designed for 500 MW of total electrical capacity and 334 MW of critical IT load, leaving 166 MW for cooling, power conversion, and facility overhead. That scale would have made Trident the largest data center in Colorado.

To address Front Range water scarcity, Vantage proposed a closed-loop cooling system paired with investments in local water replenishment projects. The developer also projected more than 3,000 construction jobs and 30 to 50 permanent operational roles, excluding outside maintenance and security contractors.

The project had already cleared planning approvals under Airport District zoning, with filings dating back to August 2025, meaning Aurora's new moratorium could not retroactively block it. [Mile High CRE](https://milehighcre.com/aurora-city-council-approves-data-center-moratorium/) reported that Misty Allen, Vantage's vice president of public policy for North America, told the council the company had voluntarily started reviewing alternative sites away from homes and would no longer pursue the East 48th Avenue parcel.

### By the numbers

- **500 MW**: Total planned electrical demand for the Trident campus, including 334 MW of critical IT load.
- **860,000 sq ft**: Proposed building footprint (80,000 square meters) across two two-story data centers on a 108-acre parcel.
- **September 30, 2027**: Expiration date for Aurora's Resolution R2026-146 moratorium, unless permanent zoning rules pass sooner.
- **160 MW**: Capacity of a separate QTS data center already near completion in Aurora, which is exempt from the pause.
- **3,000+**: Estimated skilled construction jobs Vantage said the cancelled campus would have created.

### Why this matters

Trident collapsed despite closed-loop cooling plans and existing Airport District zoning approvals. Residents in nearby developments, including the 55-plus Reserve at Green Valley Ranch community, protested outside council offices and packed a five-hour public hearing after learning the campus was approved without posted neighborhood notices.

In October, [CBS Colorado](https://www.cbsnews.com/colorado/news/aurora-city-council-vote-data-center-moratorium-community-pushback/) reported that Aurora Mayor Mike Coffman reversed his earlier tie-breaking vote against a six-month pause, rejecting Vantage's offer to look elsewhere only if the city suspended new data center rulemaking for 90 days. Resolution R2026-146 freezes new applications west and south of the Interstate 70 and Powhaton Road intersection, creating a buffer near homes while leaving eastern industrial corridors open.

### Context

Aurora's vote follows municipal freezes in other US cities facing electric and water utility strain. On November 23, Providence Mayor Brett Smiley signed an executive order upholding [a citywide zoning ban on commercial data centers](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/providence-reaffirms-100-data-center-ban-and-orders-grid-review) pending a study of electric distribution and water supply limits.

For Vantage, losing the Aurora parcel complicates its broader North American buildout just weeks after the operator agreed to buy [1 GW of solid-state transformers from Heron Power](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/vantage-signs-1-gw-solid-state-transformer-deal-with-heron-power) to feed high-density AI racks.

## Risks and open questions

Resolution R2026-146 exempts applications Aurora already accepted, so QTS's 160 MW facility in the city will proceed toward completion. QTS told Data Center Dynamics it pays its own energy costs and uses no additional water once operational, though city councilors have not yet finalized permanent noise, setback, or utility disclosure rules for future applicants.

Vantage has not named a replacement site or disclosed whether moving farther east in Colorado would require new transmission extensions, substation upgrades, or fresh state water augmentation filings.

## What happens next

Aurora City Council postponed a separate resolution encouraging Vantage to relocate the Trident project and is scheduled to revisit that measure on December 7, 2026. City planning staff will spend the moratorium window drafting permanent zoning ordinances covering residential setbacks and noise studies before September 2027.
