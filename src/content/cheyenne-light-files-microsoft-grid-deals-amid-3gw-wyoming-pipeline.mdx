## Cheyenne Light Files Microsoft Grid Deals Amid 3GW Wyoming Pipeline

### Three planned Microsoft data centers test a 46,000-customer Wyoming utility's 439-MW grid

Cheyenne Light, Fuel and Power Co. filed agreements on November 24, 2026, with the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission ([FERC docket 20261124-5256](https://elibrary.ferc.gov/eLibrary/filelist?accession_number=20261124-5256&optimized=false)) to build distribution and transmission infrastructure for three planned Microsoft data centers in southeast Wyoming. The utility, which serves roughly 46,000 retail electric customers and set an all-time peak load record of 439 MW in July 2026, is managing a 3 GW near-term data center pipeline alongside 2.5 GW in early-stage requests, according to [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/cheyenne-light-microsoft-google-data-center-bright-horizon/875739/).

## Key details

Under the redacted agreements, Microsoft will fund the required local distribution and transmission additions through upfront "contribution in aid of construction" payments. Cheyenne Light, a subsidiary of Bright Horizon Energy Corp. formed by the November 13 merger of Black Hills Corp. and NorthWestern Energy Group, told FERC it does not believe federal regulators hold jurisdiction over the retail contracts. The utility filed them for review "out of an abundance of caution" after recent FERC guidance on transmission cost recovery for large loads.

The Microsoft projects sit inside a much larger buildout around Cheyenne. On a November 5 earnings call, Black Hills officials reported 600 MW of Microsoft and Meta projects inside the utility's 3 GW near-term pipeline. In September, the utility also signed contracts running through December 2048 to power a separate Google campus in Cheyenne, per Black Hills' [Q3 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001130464/000119312526449130/bkh-20260930.htm).

### By the numbers

- **439 MW**: Cheyenne Light's all-time system peak demand record set on July 20, 2026, up from 379 MW in June 2025
- **3 GW**: Near-term data center pipeline on the utility's system, plus 2.5 GW in emerging inquiries
- **590 MW / 2.1 GW**: Grid-connected supply and third-party microgrid power contracted for Google's Cheyenne campus
- **$1.8 billion**: Estimated capital cost for a new 564-MW natural gas-fired plant to serve most of Google's grid load
- **$686 million**: Combined cost of two proposed transmission projects intended for recovery from large-load customers

### Why this matters

Cheyenne's combined 5.5 GW of near-term and emerging data center demand is more than twelve times the utility's 439-MW historical peak. Serving even a fraction of that load forces multi-hundred-million-dollar grid upgrades and new fossil generation in a service territory of 46,000 retail households and businesses.

To insulate existing ratepayers from those capital expenses, Cheyenne Light is pairing Microsoft's upfront construction payments with a Large Power Contract Service tariff and a proposed Large Customer Transmission Cost Adjustment Mechanism. Those mechanisms aim to assign transmission expansion costs, including the $686 million in proposed lines, directly to large-load customers.

### Context

The utility filings add power-grid detail to [Microsoft's earlier 3,200-acre Cheyenne expansion](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/microsoft-wyoming-datacenter-expansion), where the company committed to restoring 566 million gallons of water across two river basins. The Google campus alone relies on a 2.7 GW total resource mix with reserves, including 564 MW of new utility-owned gas generation built between 2027 and 2029 at the Cheyenne Prairie Generating Station and 26 MW of market and tariff power.

## Risks and open questions

Cheyenne Light's FERC filings omit the MW capacities, site locations, and exact dollar totals of Microsoft's three projects because the contracts were submitted in redacted form. On the generation side, Black Hills warned in its Q3 filing that the Google contract creates concentration risk by tying future revenue, including a projected $150 million annual net income boost starting in 2030, to a single customer and AI demand trends. Google has already increased permitted refundable advances for long-lead generation equipment to $398.8 million through June 30, 2027.

## What happens next

FERC will review Cheyenne Light's filing for jurisdictional and cost-allocation compliance, though no decision date was announced in the utility's notice. Separately, the Wyoming Public Service Commission is expected to rule on Cheyenne Light's Large Customer Transmission Cost Adjustment Mechanism by mid-2027. Google's Cheyenne campus is scheduled to begin taking power in late 2027 and ramp to peak load by the end of 2030, while construction on the 564-MW gas plant is slated for 2027 through 2029.
