## Applied Digital Signs $5.4B 18-Year Hyperscale Lease for 150MW AI Load

### An 18-year agreement with an undisclosed tech giant locks in 150 megawatts of critical capacity, pushing the builder's contracted base revenue past $41 billion.

Applied Digital Corporation has signed an 18-year lease agreement with an unnamed investment-grade hyperscaler for 150 megawatts of critical IT capacity. The contract provides approximately $5.4 billion in base-term revenue, expanding to $12.7 billion over 33 years if the tenant exercises all renewal options. The transaction underscores how the largest technology firms are signing utility-length contracts to guarantee high-voltage electricity and cooling infrastructure for artificial intelligence clusters years before sites open.

## Key details

According to reporting by [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/applied-digital-signs-54bn-18-year-lease-with-unnamed-hyperscaler/), the agreement covers 150 MW of critical IT load across a multi-building campus design. Delivery of the initial computing capacity is scheduled to begin in the first half of 2028. The agreement pushes the developer's total contracted base-term lease revenue past $41 billion, with total potential lease revenue reaching approximately $100 billion over full renewal terms.

The deal expands the company's contracted footprint to more than 1.6 GW of critical IT capacity across five campuses, backed by 2.2 GW of grid-connected utility power. To fund development ahead of operational cash flows, the Dallas-based company is deploying a tiered capital plan:

- Initial construction equity will come directly from cash reserves.
- The company will draw on a $5 billion programmatic equity partnership established with Macquarie Asset Management.
- The builder also secured a $300 million senior secured bridge loan facility with a 364-day maturity priced at SOFR plus 300 basis points to support construction at Delta Forge 1 outside Alexandria, Louisiana.
- Project-level debt financing for Delta Forge 1 and Polaris Forge 3 in North Dakota is scheduled to close in the first quarter of 2027, followed by financing for Delta Forge 2 in the second quarter of 2027.

Founded in 2021 as Applied Blockchain before pivoting into high-performance computing and AI hosting, the firm operates campuses in North Dakota (Polaris Forge 1, 2, and 3) and is constructing new facilities in Louisiana (Delta Forge 1) and Alabama (Delta Forge 2).

### Why this matters

The $5.4 billion contract shows the financial scale required to secure grid power in an era of transmission backlogs. Hyperscalers are paying roughly $2 million per megawatt per year simply for access to powered shells, substation hookups, and liquid cooling distribution, before spending a dollar on servers, networking silicon, or optical switches.

Signing an 18-year lease—with options extending to 33 years—represents a shift in data center economics. Commercial cloud hosting contracts historically spanned three to five years, aligning with server hardware refresh cycles. Because regional grid operators now require five to eight years to build new transmission lines and substations, tech giants are treating energized data center space like regulated utility assets, locking down physical power connections across multiple decades.

A 150 MW data center consumes roughly the same amount of electricity as 120,000 average US homes. As hyperscalers contract gigawatt-scale blocks across smaller regional power markets, transmission capacity that previously served local economic development is dedicated entirely to remote model training.

## Risks and open questions

The identity of the tenant remains undisclosed. Company disclosures describe the customer only as an "investment-grade hyperscaler," leaving observers to weigh Microsoft, Amazon, Google, or Meta as potential off-takers. Each company maintains different carbon accounting standards and 24/7 clean energy targets, leaving it unclear how much of the 150 MW load will rely on local fossil fuels.

Execution timing carries supply chain risks. With delivery promised in the first half of 2028, the company must procure high-voltage step-down transformers, switchgear, and liquid cooling chillers that currently face manufacturer lead times of two to three years.

Financing risk also remains until early 2027. Initial construction relies on cash reserves and a 364-day bridge facility carrying a variable interest rate of SOFR plus 300 basis points. Long-term project debt financing for Delta Forge 1 and Polaris Forge 3 will not close until the first quarter of 2027, exposing the builder to capital market fluctuations.

## What happens next

Site work will proceed at the Delta Forge campus, with the builder drawing down on its $300 million bridge facility. Management expects to finalize long-term project debt syndication for Delta Forge 1 and Polaris Forge 3 in the first quarter of 2027, followed by Delta Forge 2 project financing in the second quarter of 2027. Commercial turnover of the 150 MW capacity to the hyperscaler is scheduled to begin in early 2028.
