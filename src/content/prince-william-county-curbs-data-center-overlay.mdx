## Prince William County Curbs By-Right AI Data Center Zoning

### Northern Virginia county cuts default data center overlay district by two-thirds amid growing power and water concerns

The Board of Supervisors in Prince William County, Virginia, voted to significantly reduce the designated area where data centers can be constructed by default without special local approval. The decision cuts the county's data center opportunity zone overlay district by two-thirds, reducing it from 9,700 acres down to 3,500 acres.

## Key details

Prince William County forms a core component of Northern Virginia, which represents the largest data center market globally. Under previous rules, digital infrastructure projects constructed within the 9,700-acre overlay district enjoyed "by-right" status, allowing developers to proceed without individual board scrutiny or site-specific public hearings.

Under the new policy passed by county supervisors, the overlay district shrinks to 3,500 acres. Any developer seeking to construct data center facilities outside this reduced area must now navigate the full municipal zoning and special-use permitting process. To ease the transition, supervisors established a 90-day grace period before the new zoning restrictions take full effect.

Locations removed from default by-right approval include the site previously designated for the Prince William Digital Gateway, a proposed multi-gigawatt AI data center campus that was abandoned earlier in 2026 following legal challenges. Local officials explicitly cited public alarm over massive electricity draw, water consumption, and land use as primary drivers for curbing default permissions.

### Why this matters

The policy shift demonstrates how local governments in primary technology hubs are taking action to constrain unchecked infrastructure growth. As AI training and inference clusters demand unprecedented amounts of electrical capacity and cooling water, local municipalities are asserting greater regulatory authority over land allocation and utility impacts.

### Context

This decision closely follows actions across Northern Virginia and other major US data center hubs where utilities and municipalities are grappling with grid strain. Nearby Loudoun County recently instituted a 12-month pause on new data center applications to update its comprehensive plan, while state legislatures across the country are introducing ratepayer protections and resource reporting requirements.

## What happens next

Developers with pending projects are expected to rush permit applications during the 90-day grace period before by-right status expires for excised parcels. Projects submitted after the grace period will face discretionary approval processes, public hearings, and stricter environmental and resource evaluations from county supervisors.
