## Oracle Issues 2 GW Renewable RFP for New Mexico AI Campus

### Cloud giant targets 100 percent carbon-free energy matching by 2031 for Project Jupiter.

Oracle has issued a Request for Proposals (RFP) seeking 2 GW of new renewable energy generation in New Mexico to support its under-development Project Jupiter AI campus. Alongside the energy procurement effort, the company announced an up to $1 million research grant to evaluate carbon capture and sequestration (CCS) technology for on-site fuel cells.

## Key details

The RFP solicits solar, wind, geothermal, and other renewable energy projects located within New Mexico, prioritizing developers that can deliver capacity on an accelerated timeline. The power generated through these contracts will support Project Jupiter, a 2.5 GW, 1,400-acre AI data center campus being developed by Stack and BorderPlex Digital Assets with a total planned investment of up to $165 billion.

To meet initial power demands, Oracle previously expanded an agreement with Bloom Energy to supply 2.8 GW of solid oxide fuel cells (SOFC) at the New Mexico site. Because these fuel cells rely on natural gas, Oracle's new $1 million research grant will fund academic and non-profit studies into capturing CO2 emissions from Bloom fuel cells and identifying permanent geological storage options in New Mexico.

Oracle aims to achieve 100 percent carbon-free energy matching for Project Jupiter by 2031, aligning with New Mexico's Energy Transition Act goals ahead of its 2045 deadline.

### Why this matters

Gigawatt-scale AI data centers require unprecedented amounts of electricity, forcing cloud operators to deploy on-site fuel cells and natural gas infrastructure when local power grids cannot deliver sufficient power. By issuing a 2 GW renewable RFP and funding fuel-cell carbon capture research, Oracle is attempting to decarbonize a massive fossil-fueled microgrid and secure long-term clean energy in a grid-constrained region.

### Context

Project Jupiter has faced significant infrastructure hurdles, including state regulatory rejections of proposed natural gas pipeline extensions needed to feed its on-site fuel cells. Oracle's turn toward a massive 2 GW renewable RFP highlights how hyperscalers and AI infrastructure developers must secure utility-scale clean power additions to offset the environmental footprint of temporary or off-grid power solutions.

## Risks and open questions

While the RFP seeks 2 GW of renewable additions, integrating such large volumes of intermittent wind and solar power into New Mexico's grid presents technical and transmission challenges. Furthermore, commercial carbon capture and sequestration for solid oxide fuel cells remains largely unproven at gigawatt scale, leaving questions about the feasibility and cost of capturing and permanently storing on-site emissions by Oracle's 2031 target.

## What happens next

Oracle will evaluate proposals from New Mexico energy developers based on project maturity, technical viability, delivery timing, and capacity growth potential. Meanwhile, research partners funded by Oracle's $1 million grant will begin evaluating geological, environmental, and economic conditions for permanent carbon storage in New Mexico.
