## Texas Halts New Data Centers as Governor Orders Grid Audits

### Governor Abbott mandates state-level checks on electricity and water usage

Texas Governor Greg Abbott has announced a sweeping mandate requiring all new data center projects in the state to undergo comprehensive audits before connecting to the power grid. Under the new directive, both the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT) and the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) will collect critical operational data from developers to manage the unprecedented surge in grid connection requests. This decision marks a significant shift from the state’s historically laissez-faire approach to business regulation and highlights the growing strain AI infrastructure is placing on state utilities.

## Key details

Under the new directive, ERCOT and the PUCT will collect a wide range of operational data from proposed data center facilities, including their projected electricity and water consumption, use of tax incentives, ownership details, noise-mitigation plans, and light controls. The move comes as ERCOT's interconnection queue reached 474 gigawatts of connection requests, with data centers making up approximately 90% of those requests.

The volume of requested capacity has more than doubled in less than six months, rising from 233 gigawatts in January. The current queue of 474 gigawatts represents more than five times ERCOT's total peak demand. Historically, Texas has been a premier hub for data center development, second only to Virginia, driven by affordable electricity rates, abundant natural gas, and rapid solar expansion, which grew fourfold between 2021 and 2025. However, the sheer scale of proposed projects has prompted state leadership to transition from a voluntary survey approach to a mandatory auditing mechanism.

### Why this matters

The audit mandate represents a major turning point for the AI infrastructure boom, demonstrating that even the most energy-abundant regions are reaching their limits. With data centers making up 90% of the state's massive 474-gigawatt queue, regulators are forced to step in to protect the grid from collapse. This move signals that resource availability—specifically power and water—has become the primary bottleneck for AI scaling, forcing developers to provide granular data on resource efficiency to secure access.

### Context

Historically, Texas has favored lighter regulation and business-friendly policies to attract industrial development, including large-scale tech infrastructure. However, the rapid expansion of AI workloads and cryptocurrency mining has already begun driving up local electricity rates, according to the Energy Information Administration (EIA). The sheer volume of new connection requests in the ERCOT queue represents five times the current peak grid demand, making grid stability a critical political and economic issue for the state.

## What happens next

Following the governor's directive, PUCT and ERCOT will begin designing and implementing the formal audit process for all pending and new data center interconnection applications. Developers seeking to build in Texas must prepare detailed disclosure statements covering water and power footprints. Depending on the audits' findings, state regulators may implement stricter utility rates, resource quotas, or capacity limits, potentially slowing down the pace of AI infrastructure deployment in one of the world's most critical data center markets.
