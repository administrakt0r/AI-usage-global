## Virginia Senator Proposes Statewide Data Center Moratorium

### Senate Bill 843 would freeze new facility approvals for three years to curb power grid strain and rising electricity bills.

Virginia State Senator Russet Perry has introduced legislation that would enact a three-year statewide moratorium on new data center planning approvals starting July 1, 2027. The proposed freeze aims to give state lawmakers time to establish comprehensive environmental and grid protection standards for the world's largest data center market.

## Key details

Under Senate Bill 843, Virginia state agencies would be prohibited from approving new data center planning applications for a three-year period. Senator Perry, a Democrat representing Loudoun County, emphasized that multi-billion-dollar AI and cloud infrastructure growth has expanded without sufficient guardrails, leaving residential families to absorb rising electricity costs and infrastructure strain.

The bill addresses critical resource pressures, including the expansion of high-voltage transmission lines near residential communities and potential depletion of groundwater supplies. In August 2026, Senator Perry teamed up with Republican Senator Richard Stuart to request a formal state review into data center impacts on Virginia's water table.

### Why this matters

Virginia represents the world's highest concentration of hyperscale data centers, serving as the primary digital backbone for major AI labs and cloud providers. A statewide moratorium in Virginia signals a pivotal policy shift, demonstrating that even top-tier infrastructure hubs are reaching physical grid capacity and political limits regarding unchecked resource consumption.

### Context

This legislation follows localized policy restrictions across Northern Virginia. Loudoun County recently approved a 12-month moratorium on new data center applications, while neighboring Prince William County voted to reduce its designated data center overlay district by two-thirds. Statewide pauses or regulatory freezes have also been enacted or proposed in Texas, New York, and Oregon.

## What happens next

Senate Bill 843 will be considered during the upcoming Virginia legislative session. While Governor Abigail Spanberger has stopped short of endorsing a full moratorium, her administration recently established a Data Center Accountability Framework that bans non-disclosure agreements and establishes strict noise regulations for hyperscale facilities.
