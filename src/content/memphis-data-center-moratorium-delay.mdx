## Memphis Council Delays Decision on Year-Long Data Center Moratorium

### Protests Erupt as Lawmakers Postpone Vote on 12-Month Pause

Lawmakers in Memphis have postponed a vote on a proposed 12-month data center moratorium for the second time, despite intense community protests. The delay comes as local residents push for restrictions following xAI's installation of unpermitted gas turbines to rapidly power its local supercomputer facilities.

## Key details

The Memphis City Council voted 7-5 to postpone a decision on a 12-month moratorium proposed by Councilmember JB Smiley Jr. that would pause new data center developments while local officials assess their social, health, and environmental impacts. The decision has now been rescheduled for a vote on October 20, 2026.

More than 100 residents attended the council meeting to demand restrictions, leading to disruptions and chants that prompted police officers to clear the chamber. Demonstrators focused their opposition on Elon Musk's xAI (now operating as SpaceXAI), which built data centers in Memphis over the past two years to power its Grok chatbot and host inference workloads for third parties. The facilities have drawn multiple lawsuits from environmental and civil rights organizations alleging permit violations stemming from the use of gas turbines deployed to bypass utility grid connection delays.

Opponents of the moratorium argued that a pause could drive tech investment and tax revenues to neighboring counties across regional and state borders.

### Why this matters

The decision highlights growing friction between rapid AI infrastructure expansion and municipal governance over off-grid energy generation. To bypass multi-year power grid interconnection backlogs, AI operators have increasingly turned to combustion gas turbines, shifting localized air quality and environmental burdens onto host communities.

### Context

Memphis is among dozens of U.S. municipalities considering or enacting temporary freezes on hyperscale digital infrastructure. Similar moratoriums have been proposed or enacted at state and municipal levels across New York, Virginia, Washington, and California as communities evaluate the cumulative strain of gigawatt-scale data center builds on power grids, water tables, and air quality.

## What happens next

Memphis City Council is scheduled to take up the moratorium vote again on October 20, 2026. In the interim, environmental groups and local community organizations are pursuing ongoing legal challenges against xAI's gas turbine permits, while neighboring jurisdictions evaluate competing zoning and economic development policies.
