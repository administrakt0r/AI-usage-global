## AI Boom to Drive €6.8 Billion in Water Spend for European Data Centers by 2036

### Report highlights water as a critical constraint for digital infrastructure growth in Europe

A new report from Bluefield Research warns that water-related spending across Europe's data center sector is projected to reach €6.8 billion by 2036. The surge is driven by the rapid scaling of AI and hyperscale computing, which is placing unprecedented pressure on regional water resources and power grids.

## Key details

According to the report, "Europe Water for Data Centers: Market Trends, Opportunities, and Forecasts, 2026–2036," approximately 62% of the projected €6.8 billion spending will be directed toward capital investments. These include advanced cooling systems, water treatment and reuse infrastructure, and municipal connections.

Under a high-growth scenario—driven by accelerated AI adoption and favorable permitting—spending could climb as high as €7.1 billion. The analysis notes that water availability is now emerging as a key factor alongside power access in determining project feasibility and site selection across the continent.

### Why this matters

The study highlights that water is no longer a secondary concern but a critical design and operational constraint. As AI models require more intensive cooling, data centers are consuming larger volumes of water, often in regions already facing water stress. This shift is forcing developers to invest heavily in water-efficient technologies and reuse systems to secure regulatory approvals.

### Context

The growth of the European data center sector is unfolding amid tightening regulatory requirements, such as the Energy Efficiency Directive (EED) which mandates reporting on water and energy use. These requirements are converging with stricter local permitting regimes and rising regional water stress, particularly in Southern and Central Europe.

## What happens next

Data center operators are expected to accelerate the adoption of liquid cooling and closed-loop systems to minimize their water footprint. Municipalities and regulators will likely introduce more stringent water-use effectiveness (WUE) benchmarks as part of the permitting process for new hyperscale facilities.
