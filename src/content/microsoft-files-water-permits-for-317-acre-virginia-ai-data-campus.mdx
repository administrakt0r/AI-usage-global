## Microsoft Files Water Permits for 317-Acre Virginia AI Data Campus

### Tech giant plans five data centers, a substation, and dedicated water storage in rural Mecklenburg County

Microsoft has filed federal permit applications to construct a 316.7-acre, five-building hyperscale data center campus in Mecklenburg County, Virginia, expanding its southern Virginia computing footprint. The proposed project, designated the LVL-16 Datacenter Campus, would establish dedicated electrical substation infrastructure, industrial wastewater pipelines, and on-site water storage facilities across three rural parcels. The filing highlights how technology hyperscalers are extending infrastructure corridors deeper into southern Virginia as northern hubs face mounting power grid delays and land constraints.

## Key details

According to regulatory filings submitted to the [U.S. Army Corps of Engineers Norfolk District](https://www.nao.usace.army.mil/Missions/Regulatory/Public-Notices.aspx), Microsoft is seeking Department of the Army authorization under Section 404 of the Clean Water Act to discharge fill material across federally protected aquatic resources. The 316.7-acre project site is located north of Phillis Road, east of Rose Hill Road, and south of the Tobacco Heritage Trail near Boydton, within the Roanoke River drainage basin. Initial reporting from [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/microsoft-files-for-3167-acre-data-center-campus-in-mecklenburg-county-virginia/) indicates the development will feature five single-story data center buildings to support expanding cloud and artificial intelligence operational workloads.

The project encompasses extensive on-site utility works, including a dedicated high-voltage electrical substation, domestic and fire suppression water connections, sanitary sewer conduits, and an industrial wastewater treatment and disposal system. The site plan also incorporates stormwater conveyance networks and multiple retention ponds designed to manage surface runoff. To clear the three parcels for construction, Microsoft estimates that site grading and facility footprints will cause permanent fill impacts to 4.75 acres of palustrine forested wetlands, 0.02 acres of palustrine emergent wetlands, and 219 linear feet of natural stream channel. The application also notes 0.22 acres of permanent wetland conversion and temporary impacts to 237 linear feet of stream channel during construction.

### By the numbers

- **316.7 acres**: Total land area spanned across three parcels for the proposed LVL-16 campus
- **5 buildings**: Number of single-story hyperscale data center facilities planned on the site
- **4.75 acres**: Palustrine forested wetlands slated for permanent fill under Clean Water Act review
- **219 linear feet**: Natural stream channel facing permanent fill within the Roanoke River watershed
- **Dec. 19, 2026**: Deadline for public comments submitted to the Army Corps of Engineers Norfolk District

### Why this matters

Data center cooling systems and facility site preparation place substantial strain on local watersheds and municipal treatment systems. By planning on-site water storage and dedicated industrial wastewater infrastructure, Microsoft is preparing for high-density cooling requirements without relying solely on municipal utilities. However, filling nearly five acres of forested wetlands permanently alters local hydrology, reducing natural flood attenuation and runoff filtration in the Roanoke River basin.

Furthermore, the expansion reflects a geographic dispersion of AI compute away from Northern Virginia's saturated "Data Center Alley" in Loudoun and Prince William counties. While moving south alleviates immediate grid congestion in the PJM interconnection queue, it introduces multi-hundred-acre industrial developments, substations, and water treatment demands into rural counties with smaller municipal engineering departments and less redundant utility grids.

### Context

Mecklenburg County has served as an anchor for Microsoft's Southern Virginia Regional Network Group since the company built its initial Boydton facility in 2010 for $500 million. Microsoft has repeatedly expanded its presence in the region, adding a 490-acre proposal earlier this year alongside earlier campus filings across Butler Farm Road, Tunstall Road, and Lakeside Commerce Park. In 2023, the company also procured round-the-clock nuclear energy from Constellation Energy to power its Boydton operations.

The move south comes amid escalating legislative and utility friction in Virginia's northern counties. Earlier this year, state lawmakers debated a [state-wide data center moratorium](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/virginia-senator-proposes-three-year-state-wide-data-center-moratorium) to study cumulative grid and water impacts, while Microsoft has also contested [transmission cost allocation rules](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/microsoft-prepares-appeal-over-virginia-data-center-power-costs) enacted to protect retail ratepayers from footing the bill for massive utility substations.

## Risks and open questions

The public notice does not specify the total electrical capacity in megawatts or the daily water consumption volumes required for cooling the five data center buildings. While Microsoft has committed to water-positive operations globally by 2030, the environmental filing outlines significant local wetland loss that must be offset through a proposed permittee-responsible mitigation package involving off-site stream restoration and livestock exclusion. It remains uncertain whether local groundwater wells, surface withdrawals from nearby reservoirs, or closed-loop air cooling will serve the facility's heat dissipation needs during peak summer operations.

## What happens next

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers Norfolk District is accepting public comments on the Section 404 permit application (docket NAO-2024-02558 / VMRC 26-1656) through December 19, 2026. Following the comment window, federal and state environmental regulators will evaluate environmental assessments and mitigation ratios before determining whether to issue a wetlands disturbance permit. Microsoft must also finalize local zoning approvals and site plan reviews with Mecklenburg County officials before breaking ground on the substation and campus structures.
