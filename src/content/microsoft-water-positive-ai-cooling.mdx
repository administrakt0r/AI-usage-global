## Microsoft Hits Water Positive Goal Early with AI Cooling Innovation

### Tech giant slashes data center water usage by 90% using closed-loop systems

Microsoft announced it has achieved its water-positive sustainability goal in fiscal year 2025, five years ahead of its original 2030 target. The milestone was driven by the deployment of AI-optimized data centers using advanced liquid cooling technologies that significantly reduce reliance on local freshwater supplies.

## Key details

Microsoft reported a 90% improvement in water efficiency across its data center fleet since the early 2000s. The company's average Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE) dropped from 2.3 liters per kilowatt-hour (L/kWh) to just 0.27 L/kWh in 2025. A major contributor to this shift is the transition to direct-to-chip liquid cooling.

In these AI-optimized facilities, a closed-loop system circulates coolant directly to high-performance chips, eliminating the need for water evaporation during normal operations. Microsoft estimates that each of these zero-water-cooling facilities saves over 125 million liters (125,000 cubic meters) of water annually compared to traditional evaporative cooling systems.

Beyond cooling, the company has scaled its use of alternative water sources. Its Singapore campus now relies on 99% recycled or non-potable water, while facilities in San Antonio and Quincy reach 79% and 74% respectively. New rainwater harvesting projects in Quebec are projected to capture 1.5 million liters per year.

### Why this matters

The rapid expansion of AI infrastructure has historically been linked to surging water consumption, placing immense pressure on local watersheds. Microsoft’s early achievement demonstrates that high-density AI workloads can be decoupled from freshwater consumption through architectural innovation. By reducing WUE to 0.27 L/kWh, the company is setting a new industry benchmark for resource-efficient compute.

### Context

This announcement comes as the tech industry faces increasing scrutiny over the environmental footprint of large language models. While early estimates suggested AI queries consumed significant amounts of water, the industry is pivotally shifting toward closed-loop and waterless designs. Microsoft’s breakthrough aligns with recent moves by Nvidia and startup Edged US to standardize zero-water cooling for high-performance AI clusters.

## What happens next

Microsoft plans to expand its closed-loop cooling architecture to all new data center builds globally. The company has set a secondary goal to reduce water intensity in its owned data centers by 40% by 2030, a target it is already 25% toward meeting as of 2025. Monitoring will continue through AI-powered leak detection systems developed in partnership with FIDO Tech.
