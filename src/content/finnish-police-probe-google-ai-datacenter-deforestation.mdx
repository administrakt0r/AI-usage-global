## Finnish Police Probe Google Over AI Data Center Deforestation

### Criminal investigation targets tree clearing and drainage at Muhos site

Finnish law enforcement has opened a criminal investigation into suspected environmental law violations at Google's planned artificial intelligence data center development in northern Finland. The inquiry focuses on tree felling and unpermitted drainage work carried out by Google's subsidiary before mandatory environmental reviews were completed.

## Key details

The Finnish Police (Poliisi) confirmed that an investigation was initiated on October 29, 2026, targeting Google's project subsidiary, Tuike Finland Oy. The inquiry examines suspected breaches of environmental law, including shortcomings and ambiguities in site drainage and surface water management during site preparations in Muhos.

The criminal inquiry follows reports that contractors for Tuike Finland felled roughly 250,000 trees across an estimated 300 hectares of forest land before obtaining the required environmental impact assessments. Finland's Licensing and Supervision Agency (LVV) previously issued a partial stop-work order suspending preparatory activities at both the Muhos and Kajaani project sites.

Google acquired 1,400 hectares across Kajaani and Muhos from Finnish state-run forestry agency Metsähallitus in 2024. In September 2026, Google committed €13 billion ($15.1 billion) to expand its Nordic digital and AI infrastructure across 2027 and 2028, supplementing its existing seawater-cooled campus in Hamina.

### Why this matters

The investigation highlights the intensifying ecological and regulatory friction surrounding hyperscale AI data center expansions. As tech companies accelerate physical land acquisition to support growing computing workloads, land clearing and altered hydrological systems risk severe local environmental disruption.

When artificial intelligence facilities require hundreds of hectares of land for server halls and substations, civil and criminal liabilities can arise if groundwork bypasses statutory environmental assessments. The case demonstrates that European regulators and law enforcement are actively enforcing environmental compliance against hyperscalers.

### Context

Nordic countries have historically attracted hyperscale data center operators due to abundant renewable electricity and cold climates that facilitate low-energy cooling. However, regional authorities are increasingly scrutinizing the resource consumption, land transformation, and infrastructure impact of these massive installations.

Finland recently moved to abandon its traditional first-come, first-served grid allocation model to curb speculative data center power queues. Combined with strict European Union Environmental Impact Assessment rules, local governments are pushing back against unvetted site development by international cloud providers.

## Risks and open questions

The primary risk for Google involves prolonged legal delays and potential administrative penalties if Finnish prosecutors file formal charges under the Environmental Protection Act or the Building Act. If Tuike Finland fails to comply with agency orders, Finnish authorities warned they may initiate broader enforcement proceedings that could halt construction permits indefinitely.

Another open question is how the investigation will affect Google's multi-billion-euro Nordic roadmap. With site work suspended at two of its planned Finnish campuses, timelines for bringing additional European computing capacity online could slip, complicating long-term data center deployment schedules.

## What happens next

The Oulu police department and regional environmental regulators will continue reviewing documentation, soil conditions, and drainage systems at the Muhos property. Google has stated it will cooperate with the investigation while maintaining dialogue with municipal authorities.

The Licensing and Supervision Agency will complete its environmental impact assessment reviews for Muhos and Kajaani before deciding whether to lift the partial work suspensions.
