## TVA Signs $1.7B Data Center Power Deal Under Large-Load Tariff

### The federal utility will collect $31 million upfront and 59 installments from an unnamed operator requesting more than 1 GW in Tennessee Valley capacity.

The Tennessee Valley Authority has signed a $1.7 billion capacity commitment agreement with an unnamed data center operator under its new large-load tariff, according to [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/tva-touts-17b-data-center-deal-under-new-large-load-tariff/875630/). The contract requires an initial $31 million payment at signing followed by 59 installments, priced at roughly $1.5 million per megawatt above a 5 MW threshold, per [TVA's FY2026 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1376986/000137698626000041/tve-20260930.htm). At that rate, the commitment points to a facility drawing more than 1,000 MW, close to the 1,106 MW output of TVA's Allen combined-cycle gas plant.

## Key details

In Note 26 of its annual filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, TVA disclosed that it executed the Capacity Commitment Charge agreement on November 10, 2026. The tariff took effect October 1, 2026, for new or expanding data center loads above 5 MW, with broader rate changes phasing in through 2029.

TVA spokesperson Melissa Greene told the [Chattanooga Times Free Press](https://www.timesfreepress.com/news/2026/nov/23/tva-to-receive-1-7b-in-payments-from-unnamed-data-center/) that the contract is the first signed under the new charge. Greene declined to identify the customer or project location, citing TVA's practice of keeping prospective economic development details confidential.

The utility said the tariff uses a cohort-based pricing methodology aligned with the federal Ratepayer Protection Pledge and may be adjusted for later customer cohorts. During a November 13 earnings call cited by Utility Dive, TVA CEO Mike Skaggs said the revised data center rates are helping finance life extensions for existing nuclear units and expand East Tennessee's nuclear fuel and supply hub.

### By the numbers

- **$1.7 billion**: Aggregate Capacity Commitment Charge payments owed under the November 10 contract.
- **$31 million**: Upfront payment due at signing, with the rest split across 59 installments.
- **$1.5 million per MW**: Capacity commitment rate applied to requested demand above 5 MW.
- **1,000+ MW**: Implied data center capacity indicated by the contract size, per the Chattanooga Times Free Press.
- **$13 billion**: Planned TVA capital spending over the next three years, after $4.9 billion in FY2026 and over $23 billion since 2020.
- **1,550 MW**: New natural gas generation approved by TVA's board in August 2026 for 2032, with $463 million already spent on long-lead equipment and up to $3.1 billion more budgeted.

### Why this matters

Large AI campuses require dedicated turbines, transmission lines, and substations years before peak server racks go live. By requiring $1.5 million per megawatt in non-bypassable capacity commitments, TVA shifts upfront buildout risk onto the data center developer rather than spreading it across the roughly 10 million residents and industrial customers it serves across seven Southeastern states.

At the same time, the utility's capital plan shows how AI load growth is locking in new fossil generation alongside nuclear work. Alongside receiving the first U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission construction permit in September 2026 for a GE Vernova Hitachi BWRX-300 small modular reactor at its Clinch River Site in Oak Ridge, TVA has transferred $463 million in equipment commitments to a 1,550 MW gas project targeted for 2032.

### Context

TVA approved the dedicated data center rate class and Power Interruption Provision in August 2026, as [AI Usage Global reported](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/tva-creates-data-center-tariff-to-shield-ratepayers), after projecting up to 32 GW of new regional capacity needs by 2040. The deal contrasts sharply with regulated state models elsewhere in the Appalachian and PJM regions. Just this month, West Virginia regulators approved a [$2.48 billion FirstEnergy natural gas plant](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/firstenergy-wv-approves-2-48b-gas-plant-1-2gw-data-center-load) for a 1,012 MW data center load using a surcharge billed to retail customers during construction.

## Risks and open questions

- **No public customer accountability**: Because TVA has not named the operator or project site, local residents and grid watchdogs cannot verify how much water the campus will use or where transmission upgrades will cross.
- **Supply chain cost escalation**: In its Form 10-K, TVA warned that inflation, tariffs, material shortages, and labor constraints are delaying projects and pushing up equipment costs into 2027, which could squeeze power rates even with upfront capacity fees.
- **Uncommitted nuclear timeline**: Although TVA holds an NRC construction permit for the Clinch River BWRX-300 reactor, its filing notes it has not yet decided whether to build the unit, leaving 2032 gas capacity as the firmer near-term supply.

## What happens next

TVA expects to book the $31 million initial receivable and a corresponding regulatory liability in November 2026 while determining the amortization schedule. Future data center cohorts seeking more than 5 MW in the Tennessee Valley will face updated pricing reviews as TVA moves through its $13 billion three-year construction cycle.
