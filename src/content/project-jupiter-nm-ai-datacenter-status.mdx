## Project Jupiter AI Data Center Faces Legal Hurdles in New Mexico

### A $165B hyperscale project encounters pipeline denials, fraud probes, and legislative fights

The $165 billion Project Jupiter hyperscale AI data center in Santa Teresa, New Mexico, is facing mounting legal, regulatory, and legislative challenges. The 1,400-acre development, backed by Stack and BorderPlex Digital Assets with major tenants Oracle and OpenAI, is under investigation by state authorities while lawmakers debate statewide data center moratoriums.

## Key details

Project Jupiter spans 1,400 acres in Santa Teresa, New Mexico, with an estimated development price tag of $165 billion. The facility is expected to emit up to 14 million tons of CO2 annually using initial power setups, though transitioning to Bloom Energy fuel cells could reduce carbon emissions by ~30% to 10 million tons per year and cut nitrous oxide emissions by 92%.

The project faces significant legal and regulatory obstacles. New Mexico Commissioner of Public Lands Stephanie Garcia Richard denied permission to build a natural gas pipeline required for the campus's microgrid power supply. Concurrently, the New Mexico Department of Justice opened an active investigation into allegations of fraudulent public comments submitted in favor of the project's air quality permits.

To satisfy water cooling requirements without seeking new municipal permits in the water-stressed Lower Rio Grande basin, developers purchased existing agricultural water rights from a sod farm west of Sunland Park. While developers classify the supply as non-potable industrial well water, state lawmakers note that agricultural and residential wells draw from the same local aquifer. In response, state lawmakers have proposed a statewide moratorium on new data center developments to establish an environmental and ratepayer protection framework.

### Why this matters

Project Jupiter illustrates the extreme infrastructure demands and political tension surrounding gigawatt-scale AI data centers. With projected emissions rivaling major municipalities and heavy groundwater extraction in arid regions, off-grid natural gas generation and private water transfers are becoming primary flashpoints between tech hyperscalers, state regulators, and local communities.

### Context

Across the United States, hyperscalers and data center developers are increasingly turning to off-grid natural gas microgrids and fuel cells to bypass utility interconnection delays. However, state legislatures from New Mexico to Pennsylvania and New York are introducing moratoriums, utility rate protections, and stricter environmental review standards to prevent AI infrastructure expansion from increasing consumer power bills or depleting regional water tables.

## Risks and open questions

Key uncertainties remain regarding whether the New Mexico Department of Justice investigation will impact the project's air quality permitting process and whether state lawmakers will enact a statewide data center moratorium during upcoming legislative sessions. Additionally, questions persist over whether off-grid fuel cells and natural gas generation will comply with New Mexico's Energy Transition Act mandate of 100% zero-carbon electricity by 2045.

## What happens next

State regulatory agencies and the New Mexico DOJ will continue their review of Project Jupiter's permit applications and public comment integrity. State legislators plan to refine proposed moratorium legislation and environmental framework guidelines ahead of the next legislative session, while Doña Ana County officials monitor the project's compliance with state water reporting and emissions disclosures.
