## Fleet Seeks Approval for Two Nevada Methane Plants for AI Data Centers

### On-site natural gas generation proposed to bypass grid queue delays for new facilities

Energy developer Fleet has submitted regulatory filings seeking approval to build two methane-powered generation plants in Nevada to supply electricity to upcoming data centers. The Public Utility Commission of Nevada is expected to issue a ruling on the proposals in the coming days.

## Key details

According to filings with the Public Utility Commission of Nevada (PUCN), Fleet is seeking utility authorization to construct and operate two natural gas (methane) power generation facilities designed specifically for dedicated data center power supply. The proposed plants aim to provide behind-the-meter or off-grid power to bypass multi-year utility interconnect queues that currently stall data center developments across the western United States.

While specific megawatt capacities for the power plants remain under regulatory review, the facilities represent a growing trend of behind-the-meter fossil fuel generation deployed specifically to meet the high baseload energy demands of AI compute clusters.

### Why this matters

The rapid expansion of artificial intelligence workloads requires continuous, gigawatt-scale power that local electrical grids often cannot supply without years of infrastructure upgrades. By turning to dedicated on-site methane plants, developers aim to accelerate deployment timelines for AI data centers. However, relying on fossil-fuel power directly conflicts with corporate net-zero carbon goals and increases local environmental emissions.

### Context

Across major U.S. data center hubs, utility transmission backlogs have pushed grid interconnection wait times to five years or longer. In response, data center operators and energy developers are increasingly turning to natural gas, microgrids, and nuclear power purchase agreements to secure reliable, uninterrupted energy. Nevada has become a key growth market for data center infrastructure due to its proximity to West Coast technology hubs and abundant land.

## What happens next

The Public Utility Commission of Nevada is expected to issue a formal decision on Fleet's application within the coming days. If approved, construction on the methane generation plants could begin shortly after, setting a regulatory precedent for behind-the-meter fossil fuel power projects dedicated to technology infrastructure in Nevada.
