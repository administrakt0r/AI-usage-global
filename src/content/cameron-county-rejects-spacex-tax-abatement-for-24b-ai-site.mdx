## Cameron County Rejects SpaceX Tax Abatement for $24B AI Site

### South Texas commissioners vote 3-2 to kill a 10-year property tax exemption for a planned supercomputing hub and 750MW gas plant

Cameron County commissioners voted 3-2 on November 17, 2026, to reject a 10-year property tax abatement requested by SpaceX for its proposed $24 billion data center and power plant campus at Starbase. The vote halts municipal tax subsidies for the artificial intelligence project, which plans to power high-density training workloads with an on-site 750-megawatt natural gas plant. The decision highlights growing local resistance to underwriting corporate AI infrastructure as communities demand that tech operators bear their full fiscal and environmental costs.

## Key details

The Cameron County Commissioners Court rejected a motion to publish notice and schedule public hearings on SpaceX's application for a 100% property tax abatement over 10 years. According to [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/texas-county-blocks-tax-break-spacex-data-center-12733396) and local station [KRGV](https://www.krgv.com/news/cameron-county-commissioners-reject-spacex-data-center-tax-break-request/), the filing detailed a $24 billion initial investment, a first-year taxable value of $13.8 billion, and roughly 500 jobs. The 444-acre parcel near the Port of Brownsville was recently incorporated into Starbase, as noted in [earlier AI Usage Global coverage](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/spacex-plans-24b-gas-plant-data-center-starbase-texas).

Cameron County Judge Eddie Treviño Jr. and Commissioner Joey Lopez cast the only votes to advance the application, while three commissioners voted against. "I felt that we needed to go ahead and review numbers so that we had all the facts and figures and, at that given time, we could have all said nay or yay, but, as of right now, there won't be a second meeting. So the [request] is dead," Commissioner Lopez said.

The rejection followed state regulatory clearance. On November 16, 2026, the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) issued an air permit authorizing SpaceX to install 35 natural gas turbines totaling 750 megawatts. The permit allows the facility to emit up to 248 tons of nitrogen oxides (NOx) annually.

### By the numbers

- **$24 billion**: Initial capital investment for the Starbase computing and power facility
- **$13.8 billion**: Estimated taxable property valuation of the campus in its first year
- **750 MW**: Electric generating capacity permitted across 35 on-site natural gas turbines
- **248 tons**: Authorized annual emissions of nitrogen oxides under the state air permit
- **444 acres**: Industrial project footprint along the South Texas coastal corridor
- **3-2**: Vote by Cameron County commissioners denying the 10-year, 100% tax exemption

### Why this matters

The decision underscores an operational and fiscal rift between AI hyperscalers and local host communities. Facing multi-year interconnection queues on regional transmission networks like ERCOT, developers are turning to behind-the-meter fossil generation to supply high-density training clusters. A 750-megawatt plant consumes roughly the power needed for 600,000 Texas households during peak summer conditions, while emitting ground-level ozone precursors into coastal airsheds.

By denying a 100% tax exemption, local officials signaled that municipal budgets will not absorb the collateral costs of hyperscale buildouts. Residents testifying before the court stressed that granting a decade-long tax holiday to one of the world's most valuable tech entities would shift the cost of expanded roads, drainage, and emergency services onto local taxpayers.

### Context

The South Texas vote mirrors a nationwide pushback against rapid data center expansion. Voters across Ohio, Michigan, and Kansas approved local restrictions or rejected zoning exemptions in November 2026, while jurisdictions in Virginia and Nevada implemented moratoriums to protect grids and water supplies.

In Texas, Governor Greg Abbott had directed regulators in September 2026 to pause data center environmental permits while ERCOT studied power and water strains. Although TCEQ resumed reviews this week, scrutiny is expected to intensify under incoming Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa, who campaigned on statutory safeguards for data center resource consumption.

## Risks and open questions

The vote blocks municipal property tax relief but does not legally halt construction. Because the project sits on private land and holds an active air permit, SpaceX can continue site preparation and turbine installation if it pays standard county taxes.

Environmental parameters remain unverified. While SpaceX pledged $220 million for municipal water and wastewater upgrades, filings omit the daily water draw or cooling technology for the 750-megawatt complex. Water availability remains a critical vulnerability across the drought-stressed Lower Rio Grande Valley.

## What happens next

Cameron County will not hold further hearings or negotiate tax abatement agreements for the project. SpaceX has not indicated whether it will seek alternative tax relief through school districts or proceed without subsidies.

Site work continues under the state air permit. The Texas Legislature convenes in January 2027, where bills on behind-the-meter generation, grid interconnection fees, and mandatory water disclosure will face committee debate.
