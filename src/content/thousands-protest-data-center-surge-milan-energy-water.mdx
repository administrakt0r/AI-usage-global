## Thousands Protest Data Center Surge in Milan Over Energy and Water

### Civic groups demand national moratorium and review of Lombardy law as pipeline reaches 11 GW

Thousands of demonstrators gathered in Milan outside City Hall in Piazza della Scala to oppose the rapid expansion of energy-intensive data centers across northern Italy. Organized by the National Network of Stop Data Center Committees, representatives from 40 civic groups demanded that national and regional authorities freeze new facility authorizations and address mounting strain on regional electricity grids, local water tables, and agricultural land.

## Key details

The protest comes in response to an unprecedented wave of facility proposals across northern Italy. Since Lombardy enacted Italy's first regional data center regulatory framework in June 2026, the region has received more than 60 project applications representing roughly 11 GW of proposed power capacity. Approximately 60% of these requests are concentrated within the province of Milan.

Lombardy currently hosts 67 operational data centers, accounting for approximately one-third of Italy's national total. Civic leaders and municipal representatives warn that current regional legislation contains significant loopholes, allowing developers to convert agricultural parcels into industrial compute facilities by paying double standard zoning fees. Regional Councilor Massimo Sertori announced that regional authorities would draft tighter regulatory amendments within a month to address land consumption and environmental concerns.

Civic coalition spokespeople highlighted that computing facilities consume massive volumes of water and power while generating localized heat waste and eroding open soil. Demonstrators called on Rome and regional leaders to suspend approvals immediately until a comprehensive national strategy evaluates cumulative impacts on water security, thermal pollution, and grid capacity.

### Why this matters

Northern Italy is experiencing intense developer pressure due to its strategic position in Southern Europe's fiber corridors and proximity to industrial hubs. However, accommodating 11 GW of proposed load poses severe risks for the regional power network and local water resources already stressed by recurrent Mediterranean droughts. When municipal frameworks lack stringent resource caps, developers can convert arable farmland into power-hungry facilities, transferring resource competition and grid upgrade costs onto surrounding communities.

### Context

Opposition to computational infrastructure is accelerating across the European Union. In addition to the Milan mobilization, activist networks under the Stop Dirty Data Centres campaign coordinated demonstrations across multiple European hubs, while protesters in Amsterdam recently occupied a Microsoft data center construction site. Similar pushback has emerged across Ireland, Germany, and the United States, where municipal moratoria and utility surcharges increasingly target rapid data center growth.

## Risks and open questions

The primary uncertainty is whether Lombardy's promised legislative tightening will impose binding resource limits or simply raise development fees. If regional revisions fail to cap cumulative water withdrawal or mandate waste-heat reuse, project applications already in the pipeline may still lock in gigawatts of grid capacity. Furthermore, without unified national guidelines across Italy, developers may simply shift speculative projects from Milan to neighboring regions with fewer environmental controls.

## What happens next

Lombardy's regional council is expected to debate and vote on proposed amendments to its data center regulations over the coming weeks. At the national level, parliamentary representatives face mounting pressure from municipal mayors and civic alliances to establish an Italian data center observatory and uniform environmental assessment standards for facilities exceeding 100 MW.
