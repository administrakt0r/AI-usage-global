## Data Center Trust Hits Breaking Point Over Resource Demands

### US facilities consumed 176 TWh of power and 1 trillion liters of water in 2025

The rapid expansion of AI infrastructure is colliding with community resistance as the massive resource requirements of data centers become public. A new industry report warns that public trust is becoming the greatest constraint for the sector, potentially overshadowing technical and power availability challenges.

## Key details

In 2025 alone, North American data centers consumed nearly 1 trillion liters of water for cooling—roughly equivalent to the annual demand of New York City. In the United States, these facilities drew 176 terawatt-hours of electricity, enough to power approximately 16 million homes. Much of this consumption is concentrated in local municipalities, leading to a surge in public opposition.

The report highlights that $64 billion worth of data center projects were delayed or blocked in the past year due to local backlash. In some regions, opposition has escalated from policy debates to physical protests, as residents raise alarms over grid instability and the depletion of local water tables.

### Why this matters

This story underscores the critical link between AI's physical footprint and its long-term viability. As AI models grow more complex, their reliance on high-density chips drives up heat generation, necessitating even more water and power. If the industry cannot solve the "credibility problem" associated with this consumption, regulatory moratoria and community rejection could halt the build-out of the very infrastructure AI depends on.

### Context

For years, the data center industry operated with relative opacity. However, the AI boom has made these facilities inescapable neighbors. The shift from traditional air cooling—which can account for 40% of a facility's energy use—to more efficient two-phase liquid cooling is being pitched as a solution. These systems can cut energy use by 50% and virtually eliminate facility-level water consumption, yet the transition remains in early stages while community pushback continues to mount.

## What happens next

Data center operators are under increasing pressure to treat local communities as stakeholders rather than obstacles. Expect to see more transparency in resource reporting and a faster pivot toward closed-loop cooling technologies to mitigate environmental impact. In the coming months, several US states are expected to consider new legislation specifically targeting data center resource transparency and independent power requirements.
