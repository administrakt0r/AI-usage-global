## Texas Governor Abbott Calls for Ban on Rural AI Data Centers

### Proposals include "bring your own power" mandate and tax break repeal

Texas Governor Greg Abbott has called for a ban on AI data centers in rural neighborhoods, signaling a sharp shift in policy for the nation's second-largest data center market. The governor's proposals would require new facilities to secure their own power and water resources independently of the public grid.

## Key details

At a campaign event in East Texas, Abbott stated that AI data centers must be prohibited from building in rural areas to protect local values and resources. He emphasized that any new AI infrastructure must "bring their own money, bring their own power, and use their own water" to avoid increasing electricity costs for residential ratepayers. Furthermore, Abbott called for the elimination of existing tax incentives that have previously drawn hyperscalers to the state.

### Why this matters

Texas is a primary hub for AI infrastructure, and these proposed restrictions could significantly disrupt the expansion plans of major tech companies. By mandating off-grid power and water solutions, the state is effectively shifting the entire resource burden—and the associated costs—directly onto AI developers.

### Context

Historically, Texas has been highly welcoming to data centers, offering generous tax exemptions and a deregulated energy market. However, recent grid strain and rising utility bills have fueled a growing backlash, leading both Republican and Democratic leaders to re-evaluate the state's relationship with the resource-intensive AI industry.

## Risks and open questions

It remains unclear how "rural" will be defined in future legislation or whether existing projects will be grandfathered in under old rules. If implemented, these mandates could force a rapid acceleration of behind-the-meter energy projects, such as modular nuclear reactors or on-site natural gas generation.

## What happens next

The proposal is expected to become a central issue in the upcoming November 2026 gubernatorial election. State energy regulators have already been instructed to submit reports on protecting residential ratepayers, suggesting that formal policy changes could be introduced in the next legislative session.
