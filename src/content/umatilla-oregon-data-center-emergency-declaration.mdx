## Oregon City Declares Emergency Over Lost AI Data Center Revenue

### Umatilla warns of $5B loss following governor's data center permit pause

The City of Umatilla, Oregon, has declared a local state of emergency in response to Governor Tina Kotek's statewide directive blocking environmental permits for new data centers. Municipal officials warn the pause threatens $5 billion in private investments from major developers, including Amazon Web Services and Sabey Data Centers.

## Key details

Umatilla City Manager Dave Stockdale declared a Local State of Emergency following a city council vote approving a resolution to address "threatened widespread financial loss." The declaration follows Governor Kotek's directive ordering state environmental regulators to pause issuing pollution permits to data centers through July 2027, creating a de facto moratorium across Oregon.

According to the city's resolution, the permitting pause threatens to delay or cancel more than $5 billion in planned private investments in the Umatilla area. The directive imperils at least 1,000 construction jobs and hundreds of permanent positions across six planned or ongoing facility developments. Additionally, Umatilla faces $6 million in direct municipal revenue losses, endangering planned funding for a new police station and critical water infrastructure upgrades. While the city retains strong reserve funds and has not spent uncollected revenues, officials emphasize that prolonged permitting uncertainty threatens long-term municipal planning.

### Why this matters

The emergency declaration highlights the severe local economic friction resulting from state-level efforts to control AI infrastructure growth. As states implement moratoriums to mitigate power grid strain, water table depletion, and rising carbon emissions, local communities that built municipal budgets around data center taxes face sudden financial disruption.

### Context

Located in eastern Oregon, Umatilla currently hosts four operating data centers, with one facility under construction and two additional campuses in planning stages. The region has attracted massive hyperscale investments from Amazon Web Services and Sabey due to abundant hydroelectric power and low taxes. However, statewide concerns over AI energy and water consumption prompted Governor Kotek to freeze state land sales and pause environmental permitting for large-scale data centers.

## What happens next

The Umatilla City Council will review the emergency declaration in 90 days while conducting a formal economic impact assessment of forecasted municipal losses. City leaders intend to request an immediate reconsideration from Governor Kotek, petitioning for exemptions or expedited reviews for municipalities heavily dependent on data center development revenues.
