## Belgium Halts €1B Google Data Center Over River Water Cooling

### Walloon inspectors stop work in Farciennes after Greenpeace flags construction tied to a revoked Sambre River permit

The Walloon Region halted construction on November 26 at Google's €1 billion data center complex in Farciennes, near Charleroi, after inspectors intervened over work tied to an invalidated cooling permit, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/26/google-waals-gewest/) and [ITdaily](https://itdaily.com/news/datacenter/google-data-centre-farciennes-halted/). Roughly 300 workers were on the 53-hectare site when Walloon delegated official Raphael Stokis confirmed the work-stoppage order.

## Key details

Google is developing the Écopole campus, which spans Farciennes, Aiseau-Presles, and Sambreville, through its European subsidiary Crystal Computing. The company bought the 53-hectare plot in 2021 and announced a €1 billion investment when construction began.

In 2024, Google won approval for three smaller air-cooled data centers on the site. In 2025, the company filed an amendment to merge two of those buildings into one much larger hall and switch to evaporative cooling fed by up to 600 cubic meters, or 600,000 liters, of water per hour from the nearby Sambre River, [ITdaily reported](https://itdaily.com/news/datacenter/google-data-centre-farciennes-sambre-water/). Barely a quarter of that withdrawn water would return to the river, with the rest lost as steam.

Walloon administrators approved the modification in April 2026, prompting a citizen appeal in May. In August, Walloon Minister of Spatial Planning and Infrastructure François Desquesnes revoked the amended permit during a summer drought that forced 15 municipalities to ban pool filling and garden watering and required releases from the Lacs de l'Eau d'Heure to sustain river levels, according to [Water News Europe](https://www.waternewseurope.com/belgium-walloon-minister-blocks-googles-use-of-river-water-for-data-centre/).

On November 23, [Greenpeace Belgium](https://www.greenpeace.org/belgium/fr/communique-de-presse/83017/la-region-wallonne-suspend-la-construction-du-data-center-de-google-a-la-suite-dune-enquete-de-greenpeace/) petitioned the Walloon government for an emergency halt, presenting satellite imagery showing active construction despite the August revocation. Earlier reporting by [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/farciennes-google-poursuit-les-travaux-d-un-data-center-sans-permis-denonce-greenpeace-11820807) noted that up to 1,200 workers had been active at the site in recent months.

### By the numbers

- **€1 billion**: Additional investment Google announced when construction started on the 53-hectare Farciennes campus.
- **600 m³/hour**: Maximum Sambre River water draw (600,000 liters per hour) sought in Google's revoked 2025 permit amendment, with roughly three-quarters lost to evaporation.
- **300 workers**: Construction staff active on site when Walloon authorities ordered the work stoppage on November 26.
- **1 billion liters/year**: Annual water pumped at Google's comparable Belgian data center in Saint-Ghislain, of which only a quarter is returned, according to Greenpeace.

### Why this matters

Evaporative river cooling transfers server heat directly out of local watersheds during dry months. At 600,000 liters per hour, peak draw at Farciennes would reach 14.4 million liters a day, competing with municipal and ecological river flows when summer droughts already strain southern Belgium.

The dispute also tests whether hyperscalers can keep building core structures while appealing water and zoning rejections. Desquesnes told RTBF in August that climate conditions require stricter limits on pumping from the Sambre, while Greenpeace Belgium spokesperson Nadia Cornejo said tech giants cannot treat revoked permits as routine delays.

### Context

Farciennes is Google's second major Belgian cluster after Saint-Ghislain near Mons, where Greenpeace says Google draws 1 billion liters a year and evaporates roughly three-quarters. Elsewhere in Europe, data center water demand is drawing tighter scrutiny, as covered in our [report on European data center water spending](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/europe-ai-datacenter-water-forecast). Water News Europe cited International Energy Agency estimates putting global data-center water consumption at 560 billion liters in 2023 and projecting a rise toward 1,200 billion liters a year by 2030.

## Risks and open questions

Google's original 2024 permit for three smaller air-cooled facilities remains legally valid, Desquesnes's cabinet told RTBF. Inspectors must now determine whether the concrete and steel structures erected since August match the smaller 2024 blueprints or the merged design from the canceled 2025 amendment.

Google has not published site-specific water figures for the Farciennes buildout. A company spokesperson told VRT NWS that Google remains committed to "an open and collaborative dialogue with the regional government and experts for investments in Wallonia."

## What happens next

Walloon officials are completing on-site compliance checks and will forward an official violation report to the public prosecutor's office if the work breaches the 2024 permit. Google appealed Desquesnes's August decision to Belgium's Council of State on October 17, and local residents and Greenpeace filed to intervene on November 16 ahead of a public hearing set for January 7, 2027.
