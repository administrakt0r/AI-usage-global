## AI Data Center Land Buying Hits $6 Billion as Power Premiums Rise

### Power Access and Grid Proximity Drive Land Values Up to $6 Million Per Acre

US land acquisitions for future data center campuses reached approximately $6 billion in the first half of 2026, marking a 79% increase compared to the previous year. According to market research from Avison Young, access to committed electric power has become the primary driver of site valuation, creating massive price premiums for power-ready land.

## Key details

The surge in land acquisitions reflects competition among hyperscalers, neocloud providers, and energy-focused infrastructure funds racing to secure site capacity for artificial intelligence clusters. In primary hubs such as Northern Virginia, entitled land without power commitments trades for roughly $4 million per acre, whereas powered land commands at least $6 million per acre — representing a $2 million per acre premium strictly for electricity certainty.

A separate transaction analysis by Altus Group examining 80 data center land deals found that committed utility load was the single strongest predictor of land valuation. Properties connected to 230-kilovolt (kV) or 500-kV transmission lines traded at 2.4 times the price of sites near 138-kV or lower infrastructure, as high-voltage capacity significantly reduces time-consuming grid upgrade requirements.

### Why this matters

As AI training and inference clusters scale to gigawatt requirements, grid interconnection queues have replaced physical real estate as the primary bottleneck for infrastructure expansion. Delayed power delivery forces operators to postpone capacity deployment, jeopardize client SLAs, and leave capital-intensive buildings idle. By paying multimillion-dollar premiums per acre for power-ready parcels, developers are effectively purchasing risk reduction and accelerating speed-to-market.

### Context

The $6 billion land boom comes as utilities across North America tighten queue rules to weed out speculative power requests. In Texas, Plug Power restructured a project sale to Stream Data Centers for $50 million, with up to $26.5 million in additional consideration contingent on the final megawatt capacity approved by ERCOT under Batch Zero agreements. Industry analysts note that in secondary markets, agricultural or industrial land worth $10,000 per acre can jump to six-figure valuations once a viable 18-month power delivery schedule and substation plan are secured.

## What happens next

To navigate multi-year utility interconnect delays, developers are increasingly turning to behind-the-meter generation and utility letters of credit to prove project viability. Utilities and state regulators are expected to formalize stricter "take-or-pay" power agreements and financial guarantees to ensure transmission investments serve committed AI workloads rather than speculative queue filings.
