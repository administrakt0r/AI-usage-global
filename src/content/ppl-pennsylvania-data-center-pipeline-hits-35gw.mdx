## PPL Pennsylvania AI Data Center Pipeline Hits 35 GW

### Regional utility expands capital plan to $25 billion as grid connection queue swells

US utility PPL Corporation reported that its pipeline of advanced-stage data center projects in Pennsylvania grew to 35.3 GW in the third quarter of 2026. The surge represents the utility's eleventh consecutive quarterly increase and highlights how artificial intelligence infrastructure continues to reshape regional power grids across the PJM Interconnection footprint.

## Key details

During its third-quarter earnings review, PPL confirmed that its Pennsylvania pipeline expanded by 3.5 GW from the second quarter, reaching 35.3 GW of advanced-stage projects. Across the territory operated by PPL Electric, contracted load signed under formal electric service agreements increased by 300 MW to approximately 11.4 GW. Of that contracted capacity, 8.5 GW is actively under construction, marking a 2 GW increase quarter-over-quarter.

PPL announced that it has energized 300 MW of contracted data center load across two operational facilities, with plans to ramp that initial capacity to approximately 2 GW. Through Invitium Energy, its 51-49 joint venture with Blackstone Infrastructure, PPL agreed to commercial terms to supply roughly 2 GW of generation to a hyperscale QTS data center campus in Salem Township, Pennsylvania. To power the facility, the joint venture plans to deploy two combined-cycle natural gas generation plants alongside on-site fuel cells and battery energy storage systems. The fuel cells and storage systems are scheduled to begin operating by 2029, while the dedicated natural gas generation units are targeted for commercial service in 2032 and 2033.

To manage the unprecedented volume of incoming large-load connection requests, PPL expanded its 2027 to 2030 capital investment plan to $25 billion, an increase of $2 billion driven by generation requirements in Kentucky, where its development pipeline reached 14.7 GW, including 12.8 GW of data center load. Under existing large-load regulatory tariffs in Pennsylvania and Kentucky, commercial data center operators are required to directly fund the transmission extensions and generation infrastructure necessary to serve their facilities.

### Why this matters

The growth of PPL's queue demonstrates the staggering scale of energy resources demanded by AI clusters. With 8.5 GW already under construction in a single utility service territory, the physical footprint of computing infrastructure is rivaling the peak electric demand of entire mid-sized states. Requiring gigawatt-scale data center operators to bring their own generation, such as natural gas turbines and fuel cells, shifts capital expenditures onto tech hyperscalers while testing state environmental commitments and long-term carbon emissions goals.

### Context

Pennsylvania has emerged as a primary destination for hyperscalers seeking relief from transmission logjams and municipal moratoria in Northern Virginia and California. With abundant access to Marcellus Shale natural gas and strategic proximity to the PJM wholesale market, developers are increasingly negotiating private utility partnerships. However, rapid industrialization has sparked legislative pushback, including Pennsylvania's recently introduced GRID standards and regional debates over whether massive computing campuses will accelerate fossil fuel reliance and strain transmission margins.

## Risks and open questions

A key question is whether local gas and transmission infrastructure can support the multi-year construction timelines required for combined-cycle power plants without triggering local air permit disputes. While PPL emphasizes that large-load tariffs protect residential consumers from capital costs, regional consumer advocates remain wary of indirect wholesale capacity price spikes across the broader PJM grid. Additionally, the timeline for deploying on-site fuel cells and batteries by 2029 leaves near-term projects reliant on strained regional grid interconnections.

## What happens next

PPL and QTS are finalizing the long-form definitive agreement for the 2 GW Salem Township campus, while Invitium Energy continues negotiations with additional data center tenants for potential announcements before the end of 2026. Regulators at the Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission and grid planners at PJM will review the required interconnection filings as early phases of the 8.5 GW in active construction come online through 2027.
