# Rolling Meadows Approves 156MW Digital Realty Campus and 240MW Substation

The Rolling Meadows City Council approved rezoning, subdivision, and tax incentives on November 24, 2026, clearing Digital Realty to demolish a 485,000-square-foot office building and construct a 156-megawatt data center campus served by a dedicated 240-megawatt ComEd substation.

## The numbers

- **156 MW**: Critical IT capacity planned across two two-story data center buildings totaling 975,000 square feet.
- **240 MW**: Grid capacity delivered by a new Commonwealth Edison (ComEd) substation on a 4.6-acre subdivided parcel.
- **$466 million**: Minimum initial capital investment committed by Digital Realty affiliate Digital Golf Road, LLC.
- **$81.5 million**: Purchase price paid to Brennan Investment Group in September 2026 for the 39.9-acre former Atrium Corporate Center site.
- **$113 million**: Estimated municipal tax and community fee revenue projected for Rolling Meadows through 2041.

## What happened

On November 24, 2026, the City Council of Rolling Meadows, Illinois, voted 5–1 to approve a special use permit and resubdivision allowing Digital Realty affiliate Digital Golf Road, LLC to redevelop the 39.9-acre Atrium Corporate Center property at 3800 Golf Road. The council also voted 4–2 to approve a Cook County Class 6B property tax incentive that reduces the site's property tax assessment level from 36% to 16% for twelve years. Under the plan, the developer will demolish an existing, vacant 485,000-square-foot office complex and construct two two-story data halls spanning 975,000 square feet (90,580 square meters), along with a separate 4.6-acre utility lot dedicated to a 240-megawatt [Commonwealth Edison](https://www.comed.com) substation.

The vote followed heated public testimony from more than 30 residents that forced Mayor Lara Sanoica to issue multiple decorum warnings as audience members shouted at presenters and aldermen, according to [Journal & Topics](https://www.journal-topics.com/articles/rolling-meadows-oks-data-center-amid-outcry-from-crowd-at-city-council-meeting/). Opponents voiced concerns over the 240-megawatt power draw, thermal discharge, potential noise pollution, and proximity to wildlife in the adjacent Busse Woods forest preserve. Proponents, including local construction union representatives and city officials, pointed to $113 million in direct municipal revenue, $95 million for school districts 15 and 214, and an operational shift toward closed-loop cooling systems and Tier 4 diesel backup generators designed to minimize water draw and airborne hydrocarbon emissions.

A decisive factor in the council's decision was the property's boundary location bordering Schaumburg. Alderman Kevin O'Brien, who cast the lone dissenting vote on the rezoning ordinance after noting his ward opposed the project three to one, cautioned that rejecting the development could prompt Digital Realty to pursue deannexation. Under Illinois municipal law, the parcel could then annex into Schaumburg, leaving Rolling Meadows with the identical regional grid and environmental footprint but zero municipal revenue or regulatory oversight.

## Why it matters

The decision highlights how suburban municipalities are navigating the collision between commercial office vacancies and power-hungry computing infrastructure. Suburban office parks with high electrical interconnection feasibility are prime targets for hyperscale expansion, yet they frequently encounter intense neighborhood opposition over grid strain and utility rate impacts. The approval also contrasts sharply with pushback elsewhere in Cook County: in nearby Stickney Township, officials unanimously enacted a six-month data center moratorium through February 2027 to block Digital Realty's planned redevelopment of the 119-acre Hawthorne Race Course.

This tension mirrors a national pattern where local zoning boards become the primary gatekeepers of digital infrastructure. Just as [Vantage dropped its 500MW Trident campus](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/vantage-drops-500mw-trident-data-center-after-aurora-moratorium) after Aurora, Colorado instituted an emergency freeze, Midwest municipalities face competing pressures between windfall tax revenues and community pushback over water, noise, and electricity.

## Risks and open questions

The primary operational risk centers on the local distribution grid and substation timeline. While Digital Realty committed to a minimum $466 million build-out with completion slated no later than June 2031, ComEd must construct the 240-megawatt substation amid regional transformer supply backlogs. The site plan relies on closed-loop chillers to reduce municipal water consumption, but the facility will still rely on on-site Tier 4 diesel generation during grid emergencies, creating intermittent noise and emissions risks for adjacent residential neighborhoods and nearby forest preserves.

Furthermore, the Class 6B tax abatement shifts a significant share of the property tax burden away from the developer during its first 12 years of operation. While Assistant City Manager Glen Cole noted the property will still expand from generating $2 million annually to roughly 10% of the city's entire tax base, skeptical residents argued the tax incentive was an unnecessary concession for an $81.5 million land acquisition.

## What happens next

Digital Realty plans to begin site preparation and demolition of the 3800 Golf Road office building in 2027 following final administrative permitting and engineering sign-offs from the [City of Rolling Meadows](https://www.cityrm.org). Substation construction on the 4.6-acre northern parcel will proceed in coordination with ComEd, with the complete two-building campus scheduled to reach commercial operation by June 2031.
