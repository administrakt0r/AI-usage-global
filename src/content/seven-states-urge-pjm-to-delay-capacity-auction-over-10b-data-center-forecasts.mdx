Ratepayer advocates from seven states joined PJM Interconnection's independent market monitor on November 19, 2026, in demanding that the regional grid operator postpone its upcoming capacity auction. The coalition warned that baking unverified and canceled data center demand into forward procurement models threatens to push $5 billion to $10 billion in unnecessary capacity costs onto 67 million electricity consumers across 13 states and the District of Columbia.

The dispute targets PJM's Base Residual Auction for the 2029/2030 delivery year, scheduled for December 9–15, 2026. In a joint letter sent to PJM's Board of Managers, consumer advocates from Pennsylvania, Ohio, New Jersey, Delaware, West Virginia, Maryland, and Illinois urged the grid operator to remove speculative large loads before running the auction.

### The numbers and the mechanics

The demand follows a November 12 report from [Monitoring Analytics](https://www.monitoringanalytics.com), PJM's independent market monitor led by Joseph Bowring. The market monitor calculated that data center demand has already increased capacity costs by $29.4 billion across recent auctions. During the first nine months of 2026, data center demand added between $9.95 and $11.11 per megawatt-hour directly to wholesale capacity costs.

The core vulnerability lies in demand forecasting. Monitoring Analytics found that roughly 42% of data center load growth projected by PJM between the 2026/2027 and 2028/2029 delivery years was either canceled or delayed. Despite cancellations, paper projections remained embedded in PJM's forward curves. Under PJM's auction rules, artificial shifts in forecast demand drive clearing prices toward statutory caps, committing ratepayers to fund multi-year generation guarantees for facilities that never break ground.

The letter builds on a November 3 complaint filed at the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) by utility operator Exelon, which similarly argued that speculative large-load modeling distorts forward capacity prices.

### Why it matters to regional ratepayers

PJM coordinates the largest wholesale electricity market in North America. Unlike energy markets, where consumers pay for electricity consumed, capacity auctions pay generators years in advance simply to guarantee power is available during peak hours.

Under current PJM tariff rules, procurement costs are socialized across all distribution utilities and end-use customers rather than assigned to the industrial developers driving the demand. When a prospective 500-megawatt data center submits an interconnection request, grid planners adjust the regional demand curve upward. If that project stalls or withdraws, residential and small business customers remain locked into paying higher clearing prices. The state advocates cited a January agreement between PJM and 13 state governors requiring large-load forecasts to be verified before entering procurement models.

The dispute adds to grid friction across the region, where PJM previously filed [interconnection ride-through rules for 50MW data centers](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/pjm-mandates-grid-ride-through-rules-for-50mw-data-centers) to protect bulk power reliability.

### The grid operator's position

PJM management pushed back against pausing the December auction. PJM spokesman Jeffrey Shields stated that regional electricity demand is growing faster than new generation can be built, arguing that postponing procurement weeks before execution could destabilize wholesale markets and raise reliability risks during peak demand periods.

Shields noted that the December auction includes an established price cap to prevent runaway prices. PJM maintains that decisions regarding whether data centers receive local approval and utility connections rest with state regulators and distribution companies, requiring the transmission organization to plan for the demand those decisions create. PJM has floated a framework to encourage large loads to secure bilateral power agreements outside the primary auction, coupled with a backstop procurement mechanism for unhedged loads, but those revisions remain under regulatory review.

### What to watch next

The seven state consumer advocates gave PJM's Board of Managers a deadline of November 23, 2026, to provide a written response to their postponement request.

Because auction schedules and tariff parameters are governed by federal filings, PJM would need to file an emergency waiver with FERC to legally delay the December 9 auction window. If PJM's board declines to seek a delay, state advocates and utilities can petition FERC for emergency intervention to suspend the auction or order speculative loads stripped from the planning parameters before clearing.
