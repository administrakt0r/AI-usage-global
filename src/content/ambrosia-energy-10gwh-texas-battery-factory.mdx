## Ambrosia Energy Builds 10 GWh Texas Battery Plant for AI Data Centers

### Startup targets off-grid solar-plus-storage power plants at $100 per MWh

Clean energy startup Ambrosia Energy has begun construction on a 150,000-square-foot, 10 gigawatt-hour battery manufacturing facility near Austin, Texas. The factory aims to manufacture customized battery storage systems designed to power off-grid AI data centers continuously using solar energy.

## Key details

Ambrosia Energy announced plans to scale battery production to supply roughly 300 megawatts of off-grid power plant capacity annually, reaching full factory output within two to three years. The startup's power architecture pairs solar power arrays with utility-scale battery storage to supply continuous electricity at a target cost of $100 per megawatt-hour.

Later this year, Ambrosia will break ground on its first commercial site in West Texas, featuring 300 megawatts of solar panels paired with 660 megawatt-hours of battery storage. The facility is engineered to deliver 30 megawatts of continuous, 24/7 power to data center loads with a target uptime of 99.3%. To achieve its $100/MWh price target—competitive with natural gas peaker plants—the company redesigned stationary battery packs to reduce total component count by roughly a factor of 100, bringing finished pack costs down to 1.3 times bare cell pricing.

### Why this matters

As hyperscale data centers face multi-year grid interconnection delays and rising utility electricity rates, off-grid power generation has become a critical strategy for expanding AI compute capacity. By producing custom battery storage in-house and overbuilding solar capacity, Ambrosia provides a zero-emission alternative to gas turbines, enabling developers to bypass electric grid bottlenecks without increasing greenhouse gas emissions.

### Context

The announcement comes as electric utilities across the United States struggle to accommodate massive interconnection requests from AI data centers, which have triggered power grid rationing and legislative ratepayer protection bills in multiple states. With traditional power grid connections taking five to seven years in high-demand regions, AI developers are increasingly turning to behind-the-meter microgrids, islanded gas turbines, and co-located storage to bring computing infrastructure online faster.

## What happens next

Ambrosia Energy plans to begin constructing its 30 MW continuous commercial power plant in West Texas before the end of the year while ramping equipment installation at its Austin battery factory. Success at the initial West Texas site will determine whether off-grid solar and battery storage can reliably compete at scale against natural gas infrastructure for powering 24/7 AI compute workloads.
