## SpaceXAI Plans 6 GW AI Data Center in Arizona

### Musk's firm targets 747-acre Cochise County campus with 24 GWh battery backup

Elon Musk's artificial intelligence venture SpaceXAI has submitted a special development permit application for a massive 6-gigawatt data center campus in Cochise County, Arizona. The proposal calls for nine large compute facilities supported by $3.6 billion in dedicated power infrastructure and a 24-gigawatt-hour battery storage system. If fully developed, the site would represent the largest announced AI computing cluster in the world.

## Key details

According to filings submitted through SpaceXAI affiliate Tombstone Resources LLC, the project would span two parcels totaling 747 acres near Willcox Playa, roughly 80 miles east of Tucson. The site sits adjacent to the Apache Generating Station, a 626-megawatt facility operated by the Arizona Electric Power Cooperative (AEPCO) that generates power from natural gas, coal, and solar installations.

SpaceXAI plans to construct up to nine data center buildings across the campus, each enclosing approximately 493,000 square feet (45,801 square meters). The company intends to bring the campus online across four sequential 1.5-gigawatt stages, aiming to begin construction by March 2027 pending county zoning approvals.

To address grid stability concerns, SpaceXAI committed to self-funding approximately $3.6 billion in regional power infrastructure upgrades, starting with an initial $500 million allocation to support phase one. The campus design also incorporates a 24-gigawatt-hour battery energy storage system (BESS) intended to buffer fluctuating power demand and avoid destabilizing Arizona's electric transmission network. Cochise County officials scheduled a public hearing and zoning decision for December 9, 2026.

### Why this matters

A 6-gigawatt load requirement highlights the exponential electricity demands generated by next-generation frontier model clusters. For comparison, the entire state of Arizona maintained approximately 32 gigawatts of total net summer electric generating capacity in recent federal energy statistics. Connecting a single computing development requiring nearly 19 percent of the state's total capacity baseline underscores why utilities and regional grid planners face historic strain.

Moreover, placing gigawatt-scale infrastructure in high-desert regions presents critical questions regarding thermal cooling and water preservation. Even with on-site battery stabilization, running millions of high-density accelerators requires continuous baseline power and massive heat dissipation systems in arid ecosystems already sensitive to drought.

### Context

SpaceXAI currently concentrates its primary compute operations in the Memphis, Tennessee metro area, utilizing facilities across Tennessee and northern Mississippi to train and serve its Grok models. The company has aggressively expanded behind-the-meter generation, deploying industrial gas turbines and high-capacity interconnects to bypass multi-year utility interconnection queues.

The Arizona initiative signals a broader industry effort to find available land and transmission access away from saturated hubs like Northern Virginia and Texas. As major metropolitan grids implement interconnection moratoriums or stricter ratepayer protections, hyperscalers and frontier AI labs are pushing into rural counties with existing generation assets, even as equipment supply chains struggle to provide transformers, switchgear, and chips at scale.

## Risks and open questions

Securing 6 gigawatts of firm electrical supply poses severe logistical hurdles. While AEPCO's nearby Apache plant provides adjacent transmission access, its existing nameplate capacity of 626 megawatts falls far short of supplying even the initial 1.5-gigawatt phase without major new generation builds and transmission corridors. Furthermore, supplying enough AI accelerator hardware, specialized liquid cooling distribution units, and high-voltage transformers for nine hyperscale buildings presents substantial supply chain exposure. Community members in Cochise County have also raised concerns regarding local aquifer extraction and air emissions from potential backup generation during public informational sessions.

## What happens next

Cochise County supervisors will vote on the special development permit application on December 9, 2026. If approved, preliminary site engineering and transmission interconnection studies with regional utilities will advance through 2027 ahead of targeted groundbreaking. Independent transmission operators and environmental regulators will also scrutinize the necessary grid upgrade filings and water usage permits required for long-term cooling operations.
