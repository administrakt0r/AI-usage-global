## Texas Governor-Elect Hinojosa Pledges Data Center Moratorium

### Outgoing moratorium extended as new administration pushes binding resource legislation

Texas Governor-Elect Gina Hinojosa has announced plans to maintain an immediate moratorium on new data center developments across the state until lawmakers pass comprehensive legislation regulating water and electricity usage. The policy initiative responds to escalating strain on the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) grid and growing grassroots backlash against large-scale artificial intelligence infrastructure campuses.

## Key details

Under the policy framework dubbed the "Texans First Data Center Priorities," the incoming administration will seek to block new data center construction until the Texas Legislature convenes and enacts binding resource protection laws. The move follows outgoing Governor Greg Abbott's late-summer directives instructing the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT) and ERCOT to audit all data centers in the interconnection queue and pausing new environmental permits through December 2026. Hinojosa characterized temporary executive pauses as insufficient, calling for statutory safeguards that prevent industrial computing facilities from inflating local utility bills or draining municipal aquifers.

The proposed legislative agenda requires data center operators to demonstrate that their operations will not increase household water or power rates, eliminates state subsidies, bars developers from using nondisclosure agreements with municipal authorities, and renders water contracts unenforceable if they threaten local water supplies. The framework also proposes giving local communities binding voting rights to approve or reject proposed data center developments. The policy momentum follows high-profile local battles, including public opposition to Diode Ventures' proposed 900-megawatt campus in Red Oak and its subsequent withdrawal of a project in Henderson, Texas.

### Why this matters

Texas has emerged as one of the primary destinations for hyperscale artificial intelligence deployments due to available land and deregulated energy markets, but gigawatt-scale computing clusters have triggered severe resource bottlenecks. Facilities designed to host thousands of power-dense AI accelerators demand continuous baseload power and millions of gallons of cooling water, intensifying competition with residential consumers. Mandating proof of zero cost impact on residential ratepayers and granting local communities veto power introduces direct regulatory barriers that could delay or curtail hyperscale buildouts across ERCOT territory.

### Context

Public opposition to data center expansion has grown rapidly across North America, driven by concerns over grid reliability, transmission upgrade expenses, and regional water security. National exit polls revealed that 56 percent of voters considered AI data centers a crucial or major factor in their voting decisions, reflecting deep anxiety over utility bill increases. Texas has experienced several record peak electricity demand periods, and ERCOT has warned that large flexible loads could overwhelm regional generation if left uncoordinated.

## Risks and open questions

Major questions remain over whether the Texas Legislature will pass statutory bans or if industrial developers will challenge municipal voting requirements in court. If developers are blocked from grid-connected utility power, hyperscalers may accelerate behind-the-meter generation strategies, such as dedicated on-site natural gas turbines or private solar and battery microgrids, which pose localized air pollution and emissions challenges. Furthermore, developers that have already invested billions of dollars in site acquisitions and interconnect queue deposits face heightened regulatory uncertainty regarding project timelines.

## What happens next

The incoming administration will push for immediate legislative hearings when the Texas Legislature convenes in early 2027 to draft enforceable data center standards. Meanwhile, ERCOT is scheduled to conclude its comprehensive grid audit of data center interconnection requests in December 2026, which will reveal the full volume of power capacity sought by AI operators statewide. Technology companies and cloud infrastructure providers are expected to lobby heavily against local voter referendums and non-disclosure bans as legislative negotiations begin.
