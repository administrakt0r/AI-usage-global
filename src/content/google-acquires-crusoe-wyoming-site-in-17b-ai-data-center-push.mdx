## Google Acquires Crusoe Wyoming Site in $17B AI Data Center Push

### Tech giant takes direct control of Cheyenne campus with 702 MW fuel cell array

Google has acquired Crusoe Energy's unfinished data center project in southeastern Wyoming and committed $17 billion to develop the facility, stepping in directly after concerns arose over construction timelines and rising development costs. The acquisition transfers a project originally envisioned to scale up to 10 gigawatts into Google's direct portfolio, featuring 702 megawatts of dedicated Bloom Energy solid oxide fuel cells paired with carbon capture technology. The move reflects how hyperscalers are increasingly bypassing independent developers to secure power infrastructure and guarantee compute capacity for generative AI.

## Key details

The transaction transfers full ownership of the Laramie County data center campus from Denver-based developer Crusoe to Google, ending Crusoe's pre-development role encompassing land acquisition, zoning entitlements, and initial power design. Crusoe and infrastructure partner Tallgrass Energy originally unveiled the massive southeastern Wyoming campus with ambitions of scaling to 10 gigawatts of total capacity, but progress stalled earlier this year when Google paused negotiations over escalating capital estimates and delivery schedules.

Under Google's direct ownership, the company is committing $17 billion in capital expenditure to build out the site as its first operational data center campus in Wyoming. Power for the facility will be delivered through a specialized commercial-scale energy arrangement anchored by 702 megawatts of Bloom Energy fuel cells contracted with American Electric Power (AEP). The fuel cells run on natural gas to produce base-load electricity via electrochemical reaction, reducing direct freshwater consumption compared to conventional thermal generation.

Carbon emissions from the fuel cell systems and nearby gas generation operated by Tallgrass Energy and Black Hills Energy will be routed to Tallgrass's Eastern Wyoming Sequestration Hub for permanent deep underground geologic storage. Google is also conducting a front-end engineering and design study to evaluate retrofitting combined-cycle natural gas turbines with carbon capture, expanding Google's total contracted carbon capture capacity to nearly 1 gigawatt. To conserve scarce regional water resources, Google confirmed the facility will deploy an air-cooled thermal management system that eliminates evaporative cooling towers.

### Why this matters

The takeover highlights the immense pressure hyperscale cloud operators face to lock down reliable gigawatt-scale power supplies for artificial intelligence workloads. As AI model training and inference demand surge, access to high-voltage grid connections and large-capacity electrical generation has become the primary bottleneck slowing data center construction across North America.

By stepping into the developer seat with a $17 billion commitment, Google demonstrates that balance-sheet strength is becoming mandatory to push complex utility-scale energy projects through supply chain delays and utility negotiations. Independent developers face steep hurdles when financing multi-gigawatt substations, long-lead transformers, and specialized generation assets. Furthermore, Google's deployment of a 702 MW fuel cell installation coupled with carbon sequestration sets a precedent for how tech firms plan to supply continuous, behind-the-meter electricity without overwhelming municipal electrical utilities or running afoul of corporate carbon targets.

### Context

The Wyoming project, previously known during early planning stages as Project Jade, was temporarily put on pause in mid-2026 after initial developer-client negotiations hit friction regarding cost escalations and equipment delivery timetables. Despite the transfer of the Cheyenne site, Google maintains commercial ties with Crusoe, including the construction of a separate multi-hundred-megawatt AI data center campus in Armstrong County near Amarillo, Texas.

Cheyenne and southeastern Wyoming have emerged as a prominent corridor for data center growth due to favorable tax policies, high-altitude ambient cooling conditions, and proximity to regional interstate fiber routes and energy corridors. However, local utilities and community members across the Mountain West have voiced growing anxiety regarding water table depletion and transmission capacity strain. In response, Google emphasized that the campus will rely entirely on air cooling rather than local municipal water, while promising that the company will fund 100% of the auxiliary power infrastructure required to connect the facility to the regional grid.

## Risks and open questions

Deploying 702 megawatts of solid oxide fuel cells alongside large-scale carbon capture presents significant engineering and economic questions. While Bloom Energy fuel cells produce reliable electricity, capturing and sequestering high percentages of carbon dioxide from gas-fired electrochemical systems at this scale has never been demonstrated in commercial data center operations. The operational cost of carbon transportation and deep-well injection could increase running expenses substantially.

Additionally, the transaction reflects growing friction between independent data center operators and hyperscale tenants. If hyperscalers increasingly opt to buy out unfinished projects rather than leasing finished facilities, independent developers may struggle to secure early project financing without firm guarantees. The long-term impact on Cheyenne's power grid also remains closely watched, as local regulators assess whether dedicated behind-the-meter generation can fully insulate residential ratepayers from high-voltage transmission upgrade costs.

## What happens next

Google plans to initiate primary civil construction on the Laramie County campus, beginning site preparation and equipment staging for the Bloom Energy fuel cell arrays and transmission interconnects. Tallgrass Energy will advance permitting and engineering for the pipeline infrastructure needed to link the site's fuel systems and exhaust capture to the Eastern Wyoming Sequestration Hub.

Simultaneously, Google, Tallgrass, Black Hills Energy, and local officials are establishing a joint Community Council in Cheyenne to oversee infrastructure impacts, tax allocations, and workforce training initiatives. Observers will track whether Google's air-cooled, fuel-cell-plus-CCS model in Wyoming becomes a replicable design for future hyperscale AI deployments across regions facing constrained electrical grids and stringent water limits.
