## Botetourt County Creates Panel to Study Google Data Center Impacts

### Supervisors vote 3-1 for independent commission amid intense water concerns

The Botetourt County Board of Supervisors has voted 3-1 to establish an independent commission to conduct a comprehensive assessment of Google's proposed $3 billion data center campus. The decision came during a tense, packed meeting on Tuesday night, where hundreds of residents and protestors voiced strong concerns over the facility’s potential impact on the local water supply and public infrastructure.

## Key details

Under the approved measure, a five-member independent commission will be selected by the Board of Supervisors to evaluate the potential environmental, infrastructural, and utility impacts of the data center planned for a 312-acre parcel at the Botetourt Center at Greenfield. To ensure impartiality, commission members must serve without compensation, have no affiliation with Botetourt County government, and possess no personal or immediate family financial interest in Google or the data center industry. The board recorded three "yes" votes from Supervisors Walter Michael, Brandon Nicely, and Tim Snyder, with Linda Rottman voting "no," and Dr. Mac Scothorn listed as absent. The newly formed panel has been given a strict six-month timeline to deliver its final written report detailing its findings and expenditures.

### Why this matters

This development highlights the intensifying local opposition and regulatory friction facing high-density AI infrastructure projects. As tech giants like Google build out massive campuses to support advanced AI models, local water tables and municipal infrastructure are being pushed to their limits. This study will establish a formal, independent mechanism to evaluate those resource conflicts, serving as a potential blueprint for other rural communities grappling with rapid industrialization.

### Context

The vote follows months of rising tension in the Virginia county. On July 9, 2026, details emerged that Google’s Botetourt facility had contracted for up to 2 million gallons of water daily, making it one of the tech giant's most water-intensive complexes in the world. Although the Western Virginia Water Authority later noted Google plans to use air-cooling for its first building to reduce immediate demand, residents remain highly skeptical. The creation of the commission bypassed a Planning Commission proposal for a series of open public forums, leading to shouts of protest from attendees who demanded greater immediate transparency.

## What happens next

Once the Board of Supervisors selects and appoints the five commission members, the panel will begin its six-month investigative process. The commission is expected to audit water supply models, infrastructure capacity, and local environmental protections, ultimately delivering a public report. Meanwhile, residents and activist groups vow to keep organizing, warning local officials that their actions remain under close scrutiny.
