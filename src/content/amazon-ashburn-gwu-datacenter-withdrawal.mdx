## Amazon Cancels Ashburn Data Center Campus

### Cloud giant backs off Northern Virginia site following local pushback

Amazon has withdrawn its application to construct a major data center campus in Northern Virginia's Loudoun County following community resistance and official pushback over zoning restrictions.

## Key details

Amazon Data Services had acquired the 122-acre George Washington University (GWU) Science and Technology Campus in Ashburn for $427 million in March on a five-year sale-leaseback arrangement. The proposed Bridgefield Tech Center development envisioned a four-building campus totaling 800,000 square feet (74,320 square meters).

However, after meetings with Loudoun County officials and amidst strong opposition from local residents and students, Amazon agreed to withdraw its planning application and forgo legal action. Under a new agreement titled "Project Potomac Promise," Amazon will donate approximately 25 acres of land valued at $65 million to Loudoun County for a Community Arts Center and Indoor Sports Facility. The remainder of the site will be rezoned for mixed-use and residential housing, supported by a $5 million Amazon donation to the county's housing fund.

### Why this matters

Loudoun County is recognized as the world's largest data center hub, housing gigawatts of compute capacity. Amazon's decision to abandon its Ashburn campus underscores how community pushback and regulatory tightening in prime markets are halting cloud and AI infrastructure expansion, even for major market leaders.

### Context

This withdrawal follows recent decisions by Loudoun County officials to restrict data center development, including eliminating "by-right" zoning for data centers and considering countywide or statewide moratoriums. As power grid and land constraints mount across Northern Virginia, hyperscalers face growing hurdles when attempting to expand compute density in existing data center clusters.

## What happens next

Loudoun County and Amazon will collaborate on rezoning the former GWU campus for housing and community facilities rather than industrial compute. Meanwhile, Amazon will continue evaluating alternative sites across Northern Virginia to meet its growing infrastructure and compute demands.
