## Google's 2026 Report Reveals Record Surge in AI Resource Use

### Electricity demand jumps 37% as AI infrastructure buildout accelerates

Google's 11th annual Environmental Report, released June 30, 2026, reveals that the company's electricity consumption and water usage hit record highs in 2025. The surge is primarily driven by the rapid expansion of data centers required to support generative AI workloads, which is currently outpacing the company's ability to decarbonize its entire value chain.

## Key details

In its 2026 Environmental Report, Google disclosed that its total electricity demand in 2025 reached 43.6 TWh, a 37% increase from the previous year. Data centers accounted for approximately 97% of this load (42.4 TWh). The scale of Google's annual power consumption now rivals that of mid-sized nations like New Zealand.

While Google successfully reduced its operational emissions (Scope 1 and 2) by 2% through record clean energy procurement, its total greenhouse gas emissions rose by 18% to 14.47 million metric tons of CO2 equivalent. This spike was largely attributed to a 25% increase in supply chain emissions (Scope 3) related to AI infrastructure manufacturing.

Water usage also climbed significantly. Google's water stewardship projects replenished 7.7 billion gallons in 2025, which represents 78% of its total freshwater consumption. This indicates a total freshwater withdrawal of approximately 9.87 billion gallons, while total water consumption across all sources reached 10.869 billion gallons—a 34% increase from 2024.

### Why this matters

The report underscores the massive physical footprint of the AI revolution. Despite contracting a record 12 GW of clean energy in 2025—more than its total procurement from the previous two years combined—Google's absolute resource consumption is growing faster than the grid can decarbonize. The 37% jump in electricity demand is a significant acceleration from the 27% growth seen in 2024.

### Context

Google's experience mirrors the "hyper-growth" phase seen across the hyperscale industry. Microsoft and Amazon have also reported similar surges in power and water intensity as they deploy high-density AI clusters. Google maintains that its data centers are 83% more energy-efficient than the industry average for non-computing overhead, yet the sheer volume of IT equipment being added is driving total consumption to unprecedented levels.

## Risks and open questions

Google's "Net Zero" goal for 2030 is under increasing pressure. The company acknowledged that "the speed of our AI infrastructure development far outpaces the decarbonization of power grids," noting that regulatory bottlenecks and supply chain delays are slowing the deployment of new carbon-free energy. A key open question is whether future breakthroughs in fusion, enhanced geothermal, or nuclear power can scale fast enough to offset the exponential growth of AI compute demand.

## What happens next

Google is doubling down on "ambition-based" investments, including its largest-ever clean energy contracts and support for next-generation power technologies. However, the company expects continued resource pressure in the near term. Industry analysts suggest that tech giants may face tougher regulatory reporting requirements or community-led water use caps if the current trajectory of resource expansion continues unchecked.
