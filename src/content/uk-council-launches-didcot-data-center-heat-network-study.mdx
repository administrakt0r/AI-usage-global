## UK Council Studies Data Center Heat Reuse in Oxfordshire

### Vale of White Horse District Council launches feasibility study for 4.3 million sq ft data center cluster

The Vale of White Horse District Council in Abingdon, UK, has launched a heat network feasibility study for proposed data center projects in Oxfordshire. The study will evaluate using waste heat from 400,000 square meters (4.3 million square feet) of data center space planned adjacent to the former Didcot Power Station.

## Key details

The study, commissioned by the council and undertaken by energy consultancy Nordic Energy, will evaluate whether thermal energy generated by high-density data centers can be captured and distributed to nearby buildings. The site includes land acquired by Amazon Web Services (AWS) from German energy provider RWE in July 2025 for hyperscale development.

The assessment aims to review local heat network zone opportunities under the UK's National Zoning Model (NZM) and determine technical and economic viability. Nordic Energy will map major thermal demand sources, assess delivery models, and outline regulatory alignment under the UK Energy Act 2023. Final findings and recommendations are scheduled for publication on March 31, 2027.

### Why this matters

High-density AI data centers produce massive amounts of low-grade thermal waste during liquid and air cooling operations. Capturing and repurposing waste heat into local district heating networks transforms an environmental byproduct into useful thermal energy, reducing primary energy consumption for local space heating and easing community resistance to massive infrastructure buildouts.

### Context

While data center heat reuse is widely deployed in Nordic countries—such as Microsoft's partnership with Fortum in Finland supplying up to 40% of district heating needs for Espoo, Kauniainen, and Kirkkonummi—the UK has historically lacked the necessary district heating infrastructure. Legislative shifts under the Energy Act 2023 established legal frameworks for heat network zoning across England, encouraging local councils to mandate thermal recovery from large industrial and computing facilities.

## Risks and open questions

Key questions remain regarding technical integration and economic delivery models. Recovering waste heat from low-temperature cooling loops requires heat pump elevation, which consumes additional electricity. Furthermore, aligning the multi-year construction timelines of hyperscale AI data centers with municipal heat network developments requires binding long-term commitments between private tech operators and public authorities.

## What happens next

Nordic Energy will conduct technical mapping and economic modeling through late 2026 before delivering its final feasibility report to the council by March 31, 2027. If deemed viable, the Vale of White Horse District Council will establish official heat network zones around Didcot, creating a model for mandatory waste heat recovery across future UK hyperscale developments.
