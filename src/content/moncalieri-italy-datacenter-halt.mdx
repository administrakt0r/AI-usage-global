## Italy Municipality Halts 576 MW AI Data Center Project

### Local authorities in Moncalieri freeze planning over unanswered power and environmental questions

Municipal authorities in Moncalieri, northern Italy, have halted the planning application for a proposed 576-megawatt data center project. Mayor Lorenzo Mauro and governing coalition council members cited unresolved concerns regarding environmental impacts, energy consumption, and public health requirements.

## Key details

The proposed project in Moncalieri, located in Italy's northern Piedmont region, was put forward by real estate developer Moncalieri Logistics and backed by Logistics Capital Partners (LCP). Billed as Italy's largest data center application, the plans call for a 576-megawatt electrical capacity facility spanning 2.9 million square feet (270,000 square meters) of floor space on the former Industria Libraria Tipografica Editrice (ILTE) industrial site, which LCP acquired in 2022 for €9 million ($10.1 million).

Although the project promised to create 320 permanent jobs upon completion, local council members froze its progress through the municipal planning pipeline. Mayor Lorenzo Mauro stated that critical disclosures regarding energy draw, local environmental safeguards, and public health protections were absent from the developer's filing. The municipality indicated that the project will remain blocked until comprehensive assurances and modifications are submitted.

### Why this matters

The decision in Moncalieri reflects escalating regulatory and municipal friction surrounding hyperscale AI infrastructure in Southern Europe. A single 576-megawatt facility represents an enormous draw on regional power grids, equivalent to the electricity demand of hundreds of thousands of households. Municipalities are increasingly unwilling to approve gigawatt-scale infrastructure without detailed commitments on energy sourcing, local grid stability, and environmental mitigation.

### Context

Italy's Piedmont region has emerged as a key destination for European data center expansion. According to national transmission system operator Terna, grid connection requests in Piedmont surged in 2026, reaching 64 applications totaling 13.13 gigawatts of requested power. This volume ranks Piedmont second in Italy behind Lombardy for data center grid power requests. In response to this influx, Piedmont's regional government has proposed draft legislation to strictly regulate new data center developments, introducing requirements more stringent than neighboring Lombardy's Regional Law No. 11/2026.

## What happens next

The Moncalieri data center application remains suspended while municipal leaders wait to see if Moncalieri Logistics and LCP submit revised plans addressing power consumption and environmental protections. Meanwhile, regional lawmakers in Piedmont are expected to finalize new regulatory frameworks for large-scale data centers, which will establish strict mandatory standards for future grid interconnections and site approvals.
