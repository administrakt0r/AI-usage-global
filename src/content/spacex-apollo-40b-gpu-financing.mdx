## SpaceX Secures $40 Billion GPU Lease Deal With Apollo

### Private equity structure finances Nvidia Vera Rubin chips for terrestrial AI infrastructure

SpaceX has entered into a binding agreement for approximately $40 billion in GPU financing with Apollo Global Management to fund its expanding AI compute infrastructure. Under the structure, Apollo will purchase Nvidia hardware and lease it back to SpaceX over five-year terms starting in early 2027.

## Key details

According to regulatory filings submitted on October 26, 2026, Apollo Global Management will buy $40 billion worth of Nvidia next-generation Vera Rubin GPUs and specialized server hardware. Apollo will then lease the hardware back to SpaceX for terms lasting 5 to 5.25 years per equipment tranche.

The arrangement allows SpaceX to scale its compute footprint without drawing heavily on traditional debt markets. SpaceX reported capital expenditures of nearly $20 billion in Q3 2026—up from $6.9 billion in the same period last year—and expects Q4 capital spending to reach between $35 billion and $40 billion. Company executives noted that its AI segment, which powers Grok, Cursor, and cloud compute hosting, generated $7.6 billion of its $14.6 billion total Q3 revenue.

### Why this matters

The $40 billion deal highlights the staggering capital intensity required to acquire frontier AI compute hardware. By utilizing sale-and-leaseback structures, major AI operators are creating specialized financing mechanisms to acquire hundreds of thousands of high-density GPUs while avoiding direct balance sheet leverage.

### Context

As hyperscalers and neoclouds compete for GPU capacity, traditional debt financing has become tighter for entities without diversified revenue streams. SpaceX's cash flow from Starlink and launch operations enabled it to secure investment-grade terms, reflecting a widening divide between cash-generative conglomerates and pure-play AI compute providers.

## What happens next

The transaction is expected to close by the end of 2026, with initial GPU equipment deliveries and lease terms commencing in the first half of 2027. SpaceX plans to deploy the Vera Rubin platform across its expanding data center facilities to support growing compute demands for model training and cloud inference.
