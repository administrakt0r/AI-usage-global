## Kodiak Gas Services Secures 76MW Deal for Texas AI Data Center

### Texas gas firm deploys 40 reciprocating units to supply behind-the-meter primary power for hyperscale AI workloads

Kodiak Gas Services has signed a six-year contract to supply 76 MW of behind-the-meter baseload natural gas power to a West Texas data center facility. The deployment highlights how AI operators are bypassing grid interconnection delays by turning to dedicated, on-site natural gas generation to meet urgent compute demands.

## Key details

Under the six-year agreement, Houston-based Kodiak Gas Services will deploy approximately 40 natural gas-fueled reciprocating generation units along with balance-of-plant infrastructure. Deployment is scheduled to begin in the fourth quarter of 2026 and scale into full operation during the first quarter of 2027.

The data center facility operator has secured a contract with an investment-grade-rated hyperscaler, while a major GPU designer serves as the guarantor for the data center lease. Kodiak noted that this represents its second long-term contract to supply primary, behind-the-meter power directly to a data center site. Kodiak targets 2 GW of total power generation capacity by 2030, leveraging its established operational footprint in the Permian Basin of West Texas.

### Why this matters

Regional electrical grids across the United States, including Texas's ERCOT system, face unprecedented capacity queues and multi-year delays for new high-voltage connections. By installing 76 MW of on-site gas engines, the facility avoids grid queue bottlenecks and secures immediate baseload electricity required for continuous AI model training and inference. However, this shift toward islanded gas generation increases local carbon emissions and reliance on fossil fuels to sustain computing growth.

### Context

The deal reflects a broader industry trend where hyperscalers and data center developers increasingly rely on behind-the-meter fossil generation to circumvent power grid constraints. Across Texas and the broader Sunbelt region, developers are contracting natural gas reciprocating engines and gas turbines directly at the site boundary to supply reliable power. While this approach solves immediate speed-to-power challenges for AI companies, it presents ongoing environmental and regulatory questions regarding industrial emissions and local air quality.

## Risks and open questions

Deploying 40 natural gas reciprocating engines raises concerns about localized air pollution, greenhouse gas emissions, and long-term fuel price exposure in volatile energy markets. Additionally, environmental regulators and local communities are increasingly scrutinizing unpermitted or fast-tracked off-grid generation projects as hyperscalers expand behind-the-meter operations.

## What happens next

Kodiak will begin delivering generation equipment to the West Texas site in the fourth quarter of 2026, targeting full 76 MW operational capability by early 2027. As grid congestion persists, similar behind-the-meter natural gas power deals are expected to proliferate across energy-producing regions in North America.
