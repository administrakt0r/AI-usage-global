## Spain Revises Renewable Energy Mandate for Data Centers

### Spanish regulators ease clean power rules as data center grid requests top 10 GW

The Spanish government has revised its proposed clean energy mandates for data centers, easing compliance requirements for operators while keeping strict hourly renewable targets in place. The policy adjustment comes as data center projects seeking grid connections in Spain exceed 10 GW, driven by European AI infrastructure expansion.

## Key details

Under the updated framework, Spain raised the minimum capacity threshold for data centers subject to renewable energy rules from 1 MW to 10 MW. The government also extended the pre-operation window for developers to match new compute capacity with new renewable installations from 18 months to 36 months, while lifting previous restrictions on solar power additions.

Despite these relaxations, the regulation maintains the core requirement that data centers cover at least 80% of their hourly power supply with renewable energy. To ease the transition, regulators added a five-year grace period for operators to reach full hourly compliance.

### Why this matters

Spain has become one of Europe's primary hubs for AI data center buildouts due to its vast land availability and strong solar and wind generation potential. Easing the entry threshold to 10 MW provides flexibility for smaller edge facilities, while the 80% hourly matching requirement ensures that hyperscale AI campuses must actively support and finance regional grid decarbonization.

### Context

Europe faces growing grid congestion as hyperscalers and AI clouds compete for clean electricity. Similar to grid queue reforms in Denmark and the United Kingdom, Spain's mandate aims to balance ambitious climate targets with the rapid power demands of AI compute clusters without overwhelming national energy infrastructure.

## Risks and open questions

It remains uncertain whether local renewable generation can scale fast enough to meet 80% hourly matching during peak demand hours without forcing operators to rely on expensive battery storage or behind-the-meter generation. Additionally, grid operators must manage whether the 10 GW pipeline of pending requests will result in actual project completion or speculative backlog.

## What happens next

Data center operators planning deployments in Spain will prepare compliance plans under the five-year transition timeline. Government agencies will monitor grid interconnection requests and assess whether the 36-month renewable matching window effectively spurs new clean energy development across the Iberian peninsula.
