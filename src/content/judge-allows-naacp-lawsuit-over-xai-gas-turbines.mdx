## Judge Allows NAACP Lawsuit Over xAI Data Center Gas Turbines

### Federal court rules Clean Air Act lawsuit can proceed against Colossus

A federal judge has denied a motion by xAI seeking to dismiss a lawsuit by the NAACP over unpermitted gas turbines powering its Colossus data center outside Memphis.

The ruling establishes that local community groups have standing to challenge unauthorized fossil fuel generation deployed to meet surging artificial intelligence energy demands.

## Key details

U.S. District Judge Debra M. Brown ruled against xAI's motion to dismiss the lawsuit filed by the Southern Environmental Law Center on behalf of the NAACP. The lawsuit alleges that xAI installed and operated at least 35 combustion turbines and associated air pollution sources at the Colossus site without securing required preconstruction or operating air permits under the Clean Air Act.

xAI argued that the turbines should be exempt from permitting requirements by classifying them as "mobile sources" and claimed the plaintiffs lacked constitutional standing. Judge Brown rejected both arguments, determining that combustion turbines are not mobile sources under Mississippi's State Implementation Plan and confirming that the citizen suit provision of the Clean Air Act is constitutional.

### Why this matters

The decision limits the ability of artificial intelligence developers to bypass environmental review by deploying behind-the-meter fossil generation as temporary or mobile hardware.

As AI compute clusters require hundreds of megawatts faster than regional utilities can construct grid connections, developers have turned to unpermitted gas turbines, shifting pollution burdens onto nearby residential communities.

### Context

xAI's Colossus cluster in the greater Memphis region has faced ongoing community scrutiny over local air quality and utility demands.

The Department of Justice had previously attempted to dismiss the lawsuit earlier in the month, an effort that was also rejected by the court. The ruling comes as jurisdictions nationwide implement moratoriums and restrictions on rapid data center power buildouts.

## Risks and open questions

The case will now proceed to the merits, which could force xAI to halt turbine operations or install costly emissions control equipment.

It remains uncertain whether regulatory authorities will impose retroactive operational curtailments or if xAI will need to secure alternative grid-supplied power while emissions permits are reviewed.

## What happens next

The Southern Environmental Law Center and NAACP will advance the lawsuit in federal district court seeking enforcement of Clean Air Act standards and penalties.

The ruling sets a legal precedent that other communities may cite against behind-the-meter generation projects deployed for rapid AI cluster expansions across the United States.
