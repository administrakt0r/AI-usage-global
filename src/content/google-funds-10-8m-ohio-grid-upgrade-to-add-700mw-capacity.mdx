## Google Funds $10.8M Ohio Grid Upgrade to Add 700MW Capacity

### A proposed transmission participation agreement with Ohio Power would deliver 24 MW two years early in Columbus while shielding retail ratepayers from the upgrade cost.

Google and Ohio Power Company asked the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio on November 19 to approve a $10.8 million voluntary upgrade using advanced conductors on 15.5 miles of aging transmission lines in Columbus. According to [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/googles-grid-deal-in-ohio-tests-a-faster-path-to-data-center-power/), the filing would deliver 24 megawatts of contracted capacity for Google's expanding local data center about two years early and add more than 700 MW of corridor headroom by 2031 without passing the incremental cost onto everyday electricity customers.

## Key details

Under the proposed Transmission Participation Agreement, Google would pay $10.8 million upfront to cover the incremental cost of replacing 50-year-old wires with higher-capacity advanced conductors on lines Ohio Power already planned to rebuild. Ohio Power, an American Electric Power unit serving 1.5 million customers across 61 Ohio counties, expects the upgraded Columbus lines to enter service by late 2028 if state regulators approve the filing.

Advanced conductors carry 40% to 70% more electricity than conventional wires along existing rights-of-way. Once Ohio Power and its partners complete a separate 300-mile, 765-kilovolt transmission buildout in central Ohio by 2031, the reconductored Columbus lines are projected to open more than 700 MW of extra grid headroom compared with a standard rebuild.

Google also committed $1 million across three workforce and supply-chain programs: Ohio lineman training, a national advanced-conductor installation curriculum with hardware vendors, and manufacturing apprenticeships in the state. Amanda Peterson Corio, Google's global head of energy and power, said the agreement gives data centers "a new way" to expand transmission capacity years faster while lowering consumer costs and strengthening reliability.

### By the numbers

- **$10.8 million**: One-time incremental capital funded by Google for advanced conductors.
- **15.5 miles**: Length of 50-year-old Columbus transmission lines slated for reconductoring.
- **24 MW**: Contracted Google data center capacity moved up by roughly two years.
- **700+ MW**: Extra corridor capacity projected by 2031 once planned 765-kV lines arrive.
- **40%–70%**: Additional electricity advanced conductors carry over conventional wires.
- **$1 million**: Separate Google funding for lineman training and manufacturing apprenticeships.

### Why this matters

Grid congestion has forced hyperscalers to wait years for new power lines or build fossil generation on site. By funding the premium to upgrade existing corridors, Google gains earlier access to 24 MW while cutting line losses and slightly reducing transmission charges for other Ohio Power customers.

Marc Reitter, Ohio Power's president and chief operating officer, said pairing Google-funded transmission hardware with planned utility investments expands future corridor capacity for central Ohio while "passing the savings directly on to our customers." [T&D World](https://www.tdworld.com/utility-business/news/55420738/google-ohio-power-propose-108m-voluntary-transmission-upgrade-initiative-to-add-capacity-while-shielding-ratepayers) noted that the voluntary model contrasts with mandatory large-load tariffs utilities in states such as Texas have proposed to keep infrastructure costs off retail bills.

### Context

Google has invested over $20 billion in central Ohio data center campuses across New Albany, Columbus, and Lancaster since 2019, and partnered with conductor maker CTC Global in June 2025 to identify U.S. grid-enhancing projects. Elsewhere in the state, interconnection backlogs have pushed competing developers toward captive fossil power; on November 19, the [Ohio Power Siting Board approved an 800 MW captive natural gas plant](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/ohio-approves-800mw-data-center-gas-plant-despite-voter-rejection) for an EdgeConneX campus south of Columbus weeks after local voters rejected it.

Studies from the U.S. Department of Energy, GridLab, and UC Berkeley cited by Latitude Media estimate that advanced conductors cost 33% to 75% less than building new transmission lines while doubling capacity. Policy momentum is growing as well, with $1.9 billion in DOE SPARK grants for grid-enhancing technologies, FERC orders requiring utilities to evaluate advanced conductors in connection studies, and a 2025 Ohio law directing regulators to map out corporate utility partnerships.

## Risks and open questions

Both the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio and the Ohio Power Siting Board must approve the proposal before construction starts, and the full 700 MW corridor gain depends on the separate 300-mile central Ohio transmission buildout finishing by 2031. Utility ratemaking also remains a barrier because state rules generally reward capital spending on new lines over efficiency upgrades, while limited crew familiarity with advanced conductors has slowed U.S. adoption compared with markets such as Indonesia and Bangladesh.

## What happens next

PUCO and the Ohio Power Siting Board must both rule on the November 19 joint filing before construction can move forward. If regulators approve the agreement, Ohio Power expects the 15.5-mile Columbus upgrade to enter service by late 2028, while Google's Ellen Zuckerman and CTC Global's Theodore Paradise pointed to the deal as a template for Amazon, Microsoft, and Anthropic in other utility territories.
