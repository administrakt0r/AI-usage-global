## Sanders and AOC Propose Federal AI Data Center Moratorium Act

### New legislation seeks to pause US data center construction pending environmental and resource safeguards

Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez have introduced the "Artificial Intelligence Data Center Moratorium Act," a landmark bill that would impose a federal freeze on the construction of new AI data centers across the United States. The proposal responds to growing concerns over the massive electricity and water requirements of AI infrastructure and its impact on local communities.

## Key details

The proposed legislation would halt all new data center development until national safeguards are established to address the sector's environmental footprint. Key provisions of the act focus on several critical resource and social dimensions:
- **Resource Consumption:** The bill requires comprehensive reporting on energy and water usage, citing the strain on local utilities and dwindling water tables.
- **Environmental Impact:** New projects would be required to undergo rigorous environmental reviews to assess their long-term impact on carbon emissions and land use.
- **Economic Oversight:** The act calls for an investigation into the role of private equity firms, such as Blackstone and BlackRock, which have invested nearly $200 billion in data center-related deals since 2022.
- **Regulatory Framework:** The moratorium would remain in place until the enactment of federal legislation that "safeguards the public from the dangers of artificial intelligence," including its physical infrastructure requirements.

### Why this matters

The "AI Data Center Moratorium Act" represents the first significant federal attempt to regulate the physical expansion of AI. As data centers increasingly compete with residential consumers for grid capacity and water rights, the bill highlights the growing tension between rapid technological scaling and regional resource sustainability.

### Context

This federal move follows a wave of local and state-level opposition. While Maine's governor recently vetoed a state-wide moratorium, at least 12 other states are currently considering similar legislation. The rapid buildout has seen data center operators acquiring gas-fired power plants and stakes in utilities to fuel a boom that many experts warn is outpacing current grid infrastructure.

## What happens next

The bill faces significant hurdles in Congress but serves as a focal point for the escalating debate over AI’s resource costs. Lawmakers are expected to hold committee hearings to discuss the implications of the moratorium on U.S. technological competitiveness versus the immediate needs of environmental protection and utility affordability.
