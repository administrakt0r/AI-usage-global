## Meta Signs 1.6 GW AI Capacity Deal with Crusoe Energy

### Massive agreement covers new hyperscale data center capacity in Texas and Missouri

Meta has reportedly signed a landmark 1.6-gigawatt capacity agreement with Crusoe Energy, marking one of the largest single infrastructure deals in the history of the AI industry. The agreement secures critical power and space for Meta's next-generation AI workloads across two massive new data center campuses in the United States.

## Key details

The multi-year agreement provides Meta with 1,600 megawatts (1.6 GW) of dedicated data center capacity. The infrastructure will be distributed across two primary locations: a new campus in Childress, Texas, and another in Warrenton, Missouri. These facilities are designed specifically to handle the extreme power densities required by modern AI training and inference clusters.

While financial terms were not officially disclosed, industry analysts estimate the deal could represent billions of dollars in long-term infrastructure commitments. Crusoe Energy, known for its focus on water-efficient cooling and "behind-the-meter" power solutions, will lead the development of the sites to meet Meta's aggressive deployment timelines for 2026 and 2027.

### Why this matters

A 1.6 GW commitment represents a staggering amount of electricity—enough to power over one million homes—allocated to a single company's AI operations. This deal underscores the intensifying "arms race" for power-ready data center space, where the ability to secure massive blocks of electricity has become a primary competitive advantage for leading AI labs and hyperscalers.

### Context

The deal comes as traditional data center hubs like Northern Virginia face severe grid constraints and multi-year backlogs for new power connections. By pivoting to Texas and Missouri, Meta and Crusoe are targeting regions with more flexible grid capacity and favorable environments for large-scale industrial infrastructure. This follows Meta's broader strategy of investing in specialized AI hardware and the massive physical footprint required to support it.

## What happens next

Construction is expected to accelerate at both the Texas and Missouri sites to meet Meta's operational targets. As these gigawatt-scale facilities come online, they will likely drive significant local utility upgrades and increase pressure on regional water resources for cooling, despite Crusoe's efficiency targets. Observers will be watching for how these massive loads impact local residential energy prices and grid stability.
