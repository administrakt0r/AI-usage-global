## Stranded Natural Gas to Power Offshore AI Data Centers

### Houston energy firms partner to build floating compute platforms powered by unmarketed gas and cooled by seawater

Houston-based energy infrastructure companies Riverway Group and Offshore Data Center have announced a joint partnership to deploy floating offshore data centers powered directly by stranded natural gas. The ocean-based compute platforms aim to bypass terrestrial electrical grid backlogs while cutting cooling energy expenses through seawater heat exchange.

## Key details

The new initiative seeks to commercialize offshore gas deposits that are currently unutilized or reinjected into reservoirs due to the high capital costs of constructing undersea pipelines to shore. By placing AI compute hardware directly adjacent to offshore production wells, the operators eliminate traditional pipeline transmission infrastructure and bypass congested terrestrial power grids.

The proposed floating platform design is reported to be 50 percent lighter than conventional offshore structures while supporting topside industrial loads, including high-density server clusters, ammonia or methanol processing facilities, and standard gas production equipment. To handle the thermal output of high-density AI hardware, the platform incorporates a patented closed-loop cooling system that utilizes surrounding seawater to dissipate heat.

While the companies have not yet released specific timelines, buildout capital expenditure figures, or total megawatt compute capacity limits, the venture represents the latest attempt by energy developers to monetise unmarketed energy reserves specifically for artificial intelligence workloads.

### Why this matters

As terrestrial grid interconnect queues stretch to five years or longer in major computing hubs, AI operators are increasingly turning to off-grid generation strategies. Utilizing stranded natural gas at sea provides a dedicated power source without competing with municipal or residential utility demands on land. Furthermore, utilizing seawater heat exchangers addresses one of AI's most critical resource constraints: operational freshwater consumption.

### Context

The partnership reflects a broader industry movement toward marine computing solutions and non-traditional energy sourcing for AI clusters. With terrestrial hyperscale deployments facing escalating opposition from communities concerned about groundwater depletion and rising utility rates, offshore and subsea data center concepts offer an alternative deployment model. Similar ocean-based and floating solar initiatives are currently being tested across California, Europe, and Asia to address energy access and cooling bottlenecks.

## What happens next

Riverway Group and Offshore Data Center will market the platform to oil and gas producers seeking new revenue streams for stranded offshore assets. Initial engineering validations, environmental permitting for thermal seawater discharge, and modular construction schedules in U.S. shipyards are expected to be outlined in subsequent corporate disclosures.
