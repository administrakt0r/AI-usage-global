## CyrusOne KEP Tops Out 48MW Data Center in Osaka, Japan

### Kansai Electric Power joint venture completes structural frame on 70 MVA campus under 1 trillion yen expansion plan

CyrusOne KEP has completed the structural steel topping out of OSK1, a 48 MW hyperscale data center facility in Kansai, Japan. Developed through a 50:50 joint venture between Dallas-headquartered CyrusOne and Kansai Electric Power Company (KEPCO), the campus represents the first deployment under a planned 1 trillion yen ($7 billion) digital infrastructure pipeline. The project demonstrates an emerging development model where global AI infrastructure operators partner directly with regional power utilities to secure high-voltage electrical capacity amidst intensifying grid interconnection queues across Asia.

## Key details

The OSK1 development sits on a 19,693-square-meter parcel in the Keihanna Availability Zone within the Kansai region near Osaka and Kyoto. Designed as a single four-story building spanning 11,562 square meters (124,452 square feet) of technical space, the facility will accommodate six 8 MW data halls engineered for high-density computing and artificial intelligence workloads.

Structural completion was celebrated with a traditional Japanese *Jōtōshiki* ceremony, where golden and silver bolts were driven into the final structural steel beam. Construction on the site broke ground in September 2024, managed by a construction joint venture comprising Kajima Corporation and Sekisui House, Ltd., alongside architectural firm Nikken Sekkei, as reported by [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/cyrusone-and-kep-top-out-48mw-data-center-in-osaka-japan/). The campus is backed by a dedicated 70 MVA incoming electrical utility power supply delivered by KEPCO, with commercial delivery planned in three phases of 16 MW each, according to [CyrusOne project documentation](https://www.cyrusone.com/media-coverage-press-releases/cyrusone-kep-breaks-ground-on-first-data-center-in-japan).

### By the numbers

- **48 MW**: Total IT capacity delivered across six 8 MW data halls within the four-story facility.
- **70 MVA**: Dedicated incoming utility power supply delivered by Kansai Electric Power Company.
- **1 trillion yen ($7 billion)**: Minimum ten-year capital expenditure committed by CyrusOne KEP across Japan.
- **900 MW**: Long-term commercial target for total incoming power capacity (600 MW IT capacity) over the next decade.
- **16 MW**: Initial IT capacity scheduled to enter commercial operation in Phase 1 by the first quarter of 2028.
- **35 GW**: Power generation fleet operated by utility partner KEPCO across nuclear, hydro, and thermal assets.

### Why this matters

Securing high-voltage transmission interconnects has emerged as the defining barrier to hyperscale AI buildouts worldwide. By structuring an equity joint venture with KEPCO—Japan's second-largest electric power company—CyrusOne avoids the multi-year interconnection delays that increasingly obstruct independent developers. The direct utility partnership allows coordinated substation engineering and power delivery backed by KEPCO's regional generation base of more than 35 GW.

The facility also addresses community and environmental friction common to urban data center siting. To comply with local residential and commercial planning codes, CyrusOne KEP designed specialized acoustic enclosures to dampen noise from emergency backup diesel generators, chillers, and air handling equipment. External architectural facades conceal exhaust flues and mechanical equipment to preserve neighborhood visual aesthetics.

### Context

Japan is experiencing an acceleration in digital infrastructure construction, stimulated by enterprise generative AI adoption and government digital sovereignty programs. As power and land bottlenecks limit new development in Greater Tokyo, operators are shifting capital toward the Kansai metropolitan hub surrounding Osaka. This mirrors wider industry moves toward utility co-investment, such as [JERA, Dell Technologies, and Rhaelm partnering on behind-the-meter AI compute](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/jera-dell-rhaelm-japan-btm-ai-datacenter). On a global scale, the International Energy Agency projects data center electricity consumption will reach approximately 945 TWh by 2030, intensifying the competition for firm utility power.

## Risks and open questions

While KEPCO's utility sponsorship guarantees immediate grid integration, the long-term carbon footprint of OSK1 depends on the regional generation mix supplying Kansai. Although KEPCO operates multiple restarted nuclear reactors alongside extensive pumped-storage hydro facilities, fossil natural gas and coal plants still provide baseload power during seasonal grid peaks.

Furthermore, the four-year construction horizon from 2024 groundbreaking to early 2028 initial operations spans multiple hardware cycles. With next-generation AI accelerators driving rack power densities past 100 kW and requiring direct-to-chip liquid cooling loops, the developers have not yet detailed how mechanical systems in subsequent 16 MW phases will adapt to evolving thermal demands.

## What happens next

Following the structural frame topping out, general contractors Kajima and Sekisui House will proceed with exterior facade installation, roof weatherproofing, and interior architectural fit-out through 2027. Electrical switchgear assembly and 70 MVA substation commissioning will occur ahead of Phase 1 energization.

The initial 16 MW phase remains on schedule to begin commercial operations in the first quarter of 2028. Simultaneously, CyrusOne KEP is actively evaluating development sites in Tokyo and other Japanese markets to expand toward its 900 MW commercial target.
