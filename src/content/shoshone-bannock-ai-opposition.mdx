## Shoshone-Bannock Tribes Oppose Pocatello AI Data Center

### Tribes cite "speculative" water requirements and energy rate risks

The Shoshone-Bannock Tribes have reaffirmed their opposition to a proposed artificial intelligence data center at the former Hoku site in Pocatello, Idaho. The Tribes argue that the developer has failed to provide sufficient information regarding the project's impact on local electricity rates and water resources.

## Key details

In a formal statement released ahead of a July 16 appeal hearing, the Shoshone-Bannock Tribes expressed deep concern over the lack of transparency regarding the resource demands of the proposed AI facility. The Tribes highlighted that while House Bill (HB) 395 (2025) and HB 911 (2026) mandate that developers pay for power infrastructure, the limited supply and high demand for electricity are still likely to drive up rates for existing customers.

Critical quantitative concerns include:
- **Water Consumption:** The developer claimed the facility would only use as much water as "four to five houses," a figure the Tribes labeled as speculative given the known high-intensity cooling needs of AI infrastructure.
- **Cooling Hazards:** The Tribes raised alarms over the lack of detail regarding chemicals or additives used in the liquid cooling system, noting that the Portneuf River sits immediately west of the site.
- **Legislative Gaps:** Although HB 895 (2026) prohibits new data centers from using water for cooling as a consumptive use unless sourced from a municipality, the Tribes argue that the on-site power generation and cooling system leaks still pose significant risks.

### Why this matters

This opposition highlights the growing friction between rapid AI infrastructure expansion and the protection of Tribal Treaty Rights and local resources. The Shoshone-Bannock Tribes' stance emphasizes that the true cost of AI—measured in water table impacts and grid strain—is often obscured by insufficient data during the permitting process.

### Context

The Pocatello project is part of a broader trend where developers seek to repurpose industrial sites for high-density AI compute. However, as the Tribes noted, the grid is already under pressure, with Idaho Power's 2025 General Rate Case citing rapid growth as a justification for rate increases. This situation mirrors similar conflicts in water-stressed regions across the American West where AI's physical footprint is colliding with long-standing environmental protections.

## Risks and open questions

The primary risk remains the potential for contamination of the Portneuf River in the event of a cooling system leak. Additionally, there is significant uncertainty regarding whether current state statutes, like HB 911, are truly enforceable in preventing cost-shifting to residential ratepayers. The Tribes also pointed out that AI demand is delaying the decommissioning of fossil fuel power plants, further increasing the environmental footprint of the technology.

## What happens next

The Pocatello City Council is scheduled to hold an appeal hearing on July 16, 2026. The Shoshone-Bannock Tribes have called for a deliberate environmental review and for the city to ensure that all federal and state standards are met before any Conditional Use Permit (CUP) is granted for the former Hoku site.
