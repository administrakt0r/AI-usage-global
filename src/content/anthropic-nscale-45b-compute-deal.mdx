## Anthropic Signs $45 Billion Compute Deal with Nscale

### British infrastructure firm Nscale to supply six-year compute capacity from West Virginia data center

Artificial intelligence lab Anthropic has agreed to rent roughly $45 billion worth of AI computing capacity from British infrastructure company Nscale. The six-year agreement will draw high-density compute power from Nscale's flagship data center campus in West Virginia to support Anthropic's rapidly expanding model development and inference demands.

## Key details

Under the terms of the deal, Nscale will furnish Anthropic with cloud compute powered by Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin chip architecture. The Vera Rubin system integrates six distinct chips into a single unified architecture designed to optimize energy efficiency and throughput for large-scale AI workloads. Delivery of the computing capacity is scheduled to begin in late 2027.

The $45 billion agreement represents one of the largest single infrastructure commitments in Anthropic's history. It follows a multi-billion-dollar compute expansion strategy carried out over the past eight months. Earlier in August 2026, Anthropic signed a [$10 billion compute agreement](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/04/anthropic-signs-10-billion-deal-with-ai-cloud-startup-volta/) with Norwegian AI cloud provider Volta. The company also executed a $5 billion chip agreement with AMD in July 2026, an estimated $1.25 billion monthly compute lease with SpaceX, and a 5-gigawatt compute expansion agreement with Amazon earlier in the year.

### Why this matters

The multi-billion-dollar contract underscores the escalating financial and physical resource commitments required to sustain front-line AI competition. As cloud providers and frontier AI labs race to secure gigawatt-scale power allocations and advanced hardware, long-term multi-year leases are becoming essential to justify data center construction costs. By locking in compute capacity years in advance, AI companies are driving unprecedented capital expenditure into regional energy grids and specialized data center facilities.

### Context

Hyperscalers and frontier model developers are increasingly entering long-term off-take agreements to guarantee access to upcoming server architectures. With energy grid backlogs and hardware lead times stretching across multiple years, infrastructure operators like Nscale are securing long-term customer commitments before completing facility buildouts. This transaction mirrors broader industry trends, including gigawatt-scale power deals signed by Microsoft, Google, and Meta across North America and Europe.

## What happens next

Nscale will continue development of its West Virginia data center facility ahead of the planned late 2027 compute deployment. Meanwhile, industry observers and utility regulators will monitor the project's energy grid interconnection requirements and regional infrastructure impact as West Virginia prepares to host another large-scale compute hub.
