## Warsaw Suburb Data Center Plan Sparks Protests Over Water Supply

### Residents in Piaseczno and Józefosław fear cooling demands will strain municipal water

Plans to construct a new data center facility on the border of Piaseczno and Józefosław near Warsaw, Poland, have sparked public protests from local residents. Community members fear the energy-intensive project will strain local utilities and trigger severe drinking water shortages.

## Key details

Residents living on the border of the suburban towns of Piaseczno and Józefosław, located just south of Warsaw, have organized public demonstrations against a proposed data center facility. The project has raised immediate community concerns regarding local resource allocation, particularly surrounding the large volumes of municipal water needed for facility cooling.

Local opposition centers on the fear that high-density computing infrastructure will compete directly with residential neighborhoods for water supplies. Community members worry that during peak operating periods and warmer summer months, industrial water draw from the facility could lower water pressure or deplete local reserves.

### Why this matters

As data center operators expand across Eastern Europe to support growing compute demand, infrastructure projects are increasingly colliding with local municipal utilities. High-density server facilities require substantial cooling capacity, often relying on evaporative cooling or municipal water systems.

When large compute facilities are sited near residential suburbs, the sudden increase in industrial water and power demand can threaten local utility capacity. Community resistance in Warsaw highlights how resource constraints are becoming a major friction point for infrastructure expansion across European markets.

### Context

The protests in Poland reflect a growing international trend of community resistance against data center developments. Across Europe and North and South America, municipalities are facing heightened scrutiny from residents concerned about the impact of hyperscale and enterprise data centers on local water tables and electricity grids.

Similar concerns over municipal water draw and utility strain have led to local moratoria and regulatory reviews in several European and American jurisdictions. As data center buildouts continue, securing sustainable water management solutions and community approval is becoming essential for project viability.

## Risks and open questions

Key questions remain regarding the specific cooling architecture proposed for the facility and whether closed-loop or waterless cooling technologies will be implemented to mitigate municipal water draw. It is also uncertain whether local municipal authorities will impose resource conditions or environmental impact assessments before granting final planning permissions.

## What happens next

Local municipal councils in Piaseczno and Józefosław are expected to review public objections and assess the facility's projected utility impact. Further public discussions and environmental evaluations will determine whether the developer must modify cooling specifications or offer utility infrastructure guarantees to proceed.
