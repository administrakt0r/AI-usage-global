## New Jersey Towns Ban Hyperscale AI Data Centers

### Grassroots municipal resolutions pass in over 70 towns as residents reject resource-heavy facilities

Over 70 municipalities across New Jersey have adopted resolutions banning hyperscale AI data centers, driven by environmental concerns over water consumption, chemical use, and electric grid strain. The surge in local grassroots opposition follows the passage of New Jersey's Data Center Fair Share bill (A796/S731), which requires facilities larger than 50 megawatts to construct and fund their own power generation infrastructure.

## Key details

According to an August 14, 2026 report by Clean Water Action, 81% of New Jersey voters express concern regarding the environmental footprint of large-scale computing facilities. In response, more than 70 towns have enacted local ban resolutions, while 30 additional municipalities have pending ordinances to block hyperscale developments.

Hyperscale AI data centers typically consume between 1.0 million and 2.5 million gallons of water daily for cooling—an amount equivalent to the daily water demand of 3,300 to 8,300 households. In addition to direct water extraction, environmental advocates warn that alternative closed-loop cooling systems rely on large volumes of per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS).

At the state level, Governor Mikie Sherrill recently signed the Data Center Fair Share bill (A796/S731). The legislation protects residential ratepayers from subsidizing energy infrastructure by requiring data centers exceeding 50 MW to build and pay for their own on-site or dedicated power generation. However, over 60 environmental and community groups are calling for a comprehensive statewide moratorium on facilities larger than 20 MW, citing measures taken by neighboring New York.

### Why this matters

The rapid adoption of municipal bans highlights growing local resistance to the physical footprint of AI compute expansion. While state legislation addresses electricity cost-shifting onto ratepayers, local communities remain vulnerable to watershed depletion and toxic chemical contamination from cooling systems.

### Context

Across North America, municipalities and state governments are increasingly turning to moratoriums and strict zoning mandates to manage AI infrastructure growth. As public utility grids face capacity limits and local aquifers experience drought, community governance has emerged as a primary check on hyperscale data center development.

## What happens next

Clean Water Action and allied environmental organizations continue to push Governor Sherrill for an executive order establishing a statewide moratorium on new AI data centers over 20 MW. Meanwhile, dozens of additional New Jersey municipalities are slated to vote on local ban ordinances in the coming months.
