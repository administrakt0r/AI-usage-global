## West Virginia Approves FirstEnergy $2.48B Gas Plant for 1,012 MW Data Center Load

### Regulators granted Mon Power and Potomac Edison a construction surcharge to build a 1,200 MW natural gas facility and 70 MW of solar after a single data center customer requested power scaling past 1 GW.

The West Virginia Public Service Commission has approved a $2.48 billion plan by FirstEnergy utilities Mon Power and Potomac Edison to build the 1,200 MW Maidsville Energy Center combined-cycle gas plant alongside 70 MW of utility-scale solar, backed by a construction surcharge paid by retail ratepayers. According to [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/firstenergy-utilities-get-approval-for-25b-gas-plant-in-west-virginia/875646/), commission staff noted that the buildout was prompted by an unnamed data center customer seeking 100 MW in 2028 and ramping incrementally to 1,012 MW by 2033.

## Key details

In approving the certificate of public convenience and necessity in Monongalia County next to FirstEnergy's existing Fort Martin Power Station, regulators accepted the utilities' warning of a looming supply crunch. Commission staff analysis cited by [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/firstenergy-utilities-get-approval-for-25b-gas-plant-in-west-virginia/875646/) showed Mon Power projecting a 2,997 MW summer peak in 2026, shifting to a 60 MW capacity shortfall by 2028 and a 2,194 MW deficit by 2034 once large loads such as data centers connect.

FirstEnergy expects construction to begin in 2027 and finish by 2031, with peak site employment exceeding 2,000 union construction workers before dropping to about 30 permanent full-time roles, FirstEnergy West Virginia and Maryland President Chris Beam told [WV MetroNews](https://wvmetronews.com/2026/11/19/construction-on-firstenergys-2-48-billion-natural-gas-plant-solar-projects-expected-to-start-next-year/). Beam confirmed the company has already locked in gas turbine delivery dates through cash reservation agreements amid global manufacturing backlogs.

The PSC also granted a construction-work-in-progress surcharge so Mon Power and Potomac Edison can collect financing costs from customers during the four-year build rather than waiting until the plant enters service. Regulators cited precedent from Pepco Holdings' canceled Mid Atlantic Power Pathway, which recovered more than $80 million from ratepayers after the transmission line was abandoned. If Maidsville stalls or stops, the PSC order states it will open a separate proceeding to set surcharge or base-rate recovery terms.

### By the numbers

- **1,200 MW**: Nameplate capacity of the Maidsville Energy Center combined-cycle natural gas plant, paired with **70 MW** of solar across Tucker, Preston, and Monongalia counties, per [WV MetroNews](https://wvmetronews.com/2026/11/19/construction-on-firstenergys-2-48-billion-natural-gas-plant-solar-projects-expected-to-start-next-year/).
- **$2.48 billion**: Estimated capital cost of the generation package.
- **100 MW to 1,012 MW**: Projected demand ramp from the unnamed data center customer between 2028 and 2033, according to PSC staff.
- **2,194 MW**: Projected Mon Power capacity shortfall by 2034 under new large-load additions, up from **60 MW** in 2028.
- **67%**: Estimated jump in Mon Power's roughly **$4 billion** rate base from adding the project, according to Sierra Club testimony cited by [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/firstenergy-utilities-get-approval-for-25b-gas-plant-in-west-virginia/875646/).
- **$80 million+**: Precedent cited by the PSC from Pepco Holdings' uncompleted Mid Atlantic Power Pathway recovery.

### Why this matters

Unlike tech hyperscalers signing private power purchase agreements, FirstEnergy's approval puts a multi-billion-dollar fossil gas asset directly into regulated utility rate base. Consumer advocates, environmental groups, and the United Mine Workers Union objected during proceedings, arguing that residential and small business customers are being forced to backstop capacity built for hyperscale tech tenants without dedicated large-load tariffs.

Anna Sommer of Energy Futures Group, testifying for the West Virginia Citizen Action Group, Solar United Neighbors, and Energy Efficient West Virginia, warned that an underdeveloped plant proposal raises the risk of cost overruns and schedule slips. Energy Efficient West Virginia Policy Director Emmett Pepper went further in remarks to [WV MetroNews](https://wvmetronews.com/2026/11/19/construction-on-firstenergys-2-48-billion-natural-gas-plant-solar-projects-expected-to-start-next-year/), arguing customers should pay nothing for generation built for data centers and urging Governor Patrick Morrisey to recall lawmakers to strengthen ratepayer protections.

### Context

FirstEnergy's [West Virginia generation project overview](https://www.firstenergycorp.com/about/generation_system/wv_generation.html) frames the gas and solar buildout around long-term reliability and avoiding capacity market volatility. Across FirstEnergy's multi-state footprint, [its data center pipeline has climbed to 26 GW](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/firstenergy-datacenter-pipeline-expansion-2026), mirroring surging capacity auction prices across the PJM Interconnection.

Earlier this year, [the Department of Energy pressed PJM to shield ratepayers](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/doe-presses-pjm-to-shield-ratepayers-from-ai-data-center-grid-costs) from AI grid buildout costs after [data center demand pushed PJM capacity auctions to the price cap](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/pjm-capacity-auction-price-cap-ai). West Virginia's order highlights a sharp split among US states: while several legislatures are forcing tech buyers to pay upfront via large-load tariffs, West Virginia regulators approved expedited recovery from existing utility customers.

## Risks and open questions

- **Stranded-asset exposure if the 1,012 MW tenant walks**: Because the PSC authorized a construction surcharge without requiring a dedicated large-load tariff or binding customer collateral in the order reported by Utility Dive, households could keep paying if projected data center load fails to materialize.
- **Gas turbine and EPC inflation**: Global turbine lead times have already forced FirstEnergy into reservation payments; supply chain bottlenecks or labor shortages during the 2027 to 2031 build could push final capital costs past $2.48 billion.
- **Climate and emissions lock-in**: Adding 1,200 MW of baseload natural gas capacity locks in decades of fossil generation inside PJM to serve digital infrastructure loads that could otherwise be paired with firm clean energy or co-located storage.

## What happens next

FirstEnergy subsidiaries Mon Power and Potomac Edison plan to begin site construction in 2027 and bring the 1,200 MW Maidsville Energy Center online in 2031. Consumer and environmental intervenors, along with legislative critics, are pushing for new state code guardrails and dedicated large-load tariffs before additional West Virginia generation projects seek PSC approval.
