## DTE Energy Ups Five-Year Capital Plan to $44B on Data Center Load

### Michigan Utility Expands Infrastructure Budget by Nearly $10B

Michigan utility DTE Energy has expanded its five-year infrastructure investment plan by nearly $10 billion to approximately $44 billion, citing massive power demand from artificial intelligence and cloud data centers as the primary driver. The revised capital strategy underscores how rapidly escalating compute workloads are reshaping long-term utility generation, transmission, and rate structures.

## Key details

During its third-quarter earnings call, DTE Energy announced that its five-year utility investment plan increased from previous projections to $44 billion. The electric division, DTE Electric, accounts for $39 billion of that total, which includes approximately $23 billion dedicated to generation infrastructure and $12 billion allocated for upgrading the electric distribution grid. The capital expenditure program includes deploying 7.6 gigawatts (GW) of new solar and wind generation, 2.1 GW of combined-cycle natural gas capacity to replace the retiring Monroe coal plant, and 2.7 GW of battery energy storage systems.

The utility disclosed that it has already fully secured 2.4 GW of contracted data center power demand across its service territory. This secured load comprises a 1.4 GW facility in Saline Township, Michigan, developed by Related Digital in partnership with Oracle and OpenAI, and a 1 GW computing campus in Van Buren Township developed by Google. Under an approved 20-year agreement, Google committed to funding 480 megawatts (MW) of energy storage and bringing 2.7 GW of clean energy resources to the regional grid, with complete energization slated for late 2028. Beyond contracted customers, DTE reported an active pipeline of 5 GW to 6 GW in potential data center load requests, including 2 GW currently under advanced negotiations.

### Why this matters

The multi-billion-dollar budget expansion demonstrates the physical and financial scale required to integrate hyperscale artificial intelligence facilities into existing regional grids. Serving gigawatt-scale computing campuses necessitates massive capital outlays for new transmission lines, substations, and firm generation capacity that far outpace historical utility load forecasting.

To shield existing residential and business customers from stranded asset risks, DTE established specialized large-load tariff structures and power purchase agreements. The contracts enforce minimum monthly billing charges, strict collateral and credit requirements, and substantial termination fees. DTE leadership maintains that spreading fixed system investments across a significantly larger volume of industrial electric sales will ultimately lower baseline distribution charges for the utility's 2.3 million electric customers.

### Context

Utilities across the United States are aggressively updating long-term integrated resource plans as technology companies seek direct grid access for power-dense AI model training and inference clusters. Similar multi-gigawatt utility agreements have emerged across PJM, MISO, and ERCOT service territories, where regional transmission operators face tightening reserve margins and delayed fossil-fuel retirements.

DTE's parallel natural gas business, which serves 1.3 million customers in Michigan, also reflects the sector's reliance on dual-fuel strategies to maintain baseload reliability while intermittent renewable assets are constructed. The combination of behind-the-meter storage mandates and utility-scale solar installations highlights how tech operators are forced to co-fund grid expansion to bypass multi-year interconnection queues.

## Risks and open questions

The reliance on massive utility capital expenditures raises regulatory questions regarding cost allocation and grid resilience. While DTE's tariff framework includes protective credit covenants, unexpected cancellations or technological shifts in AI compute efficiency could leave utility shareholders and rate commissions managing oversized generation portfolios.

Environmental stakeholders also question the utility's plan to deploy 2.1 GW of new combined-cycle natural gas capacity to replace retiring coal units. Although gas turbines provide firm dispatchable power to stabilize hyperscale loads, their continued deployment complicates state and corporate net-zero carbon goals.

## What happens next

DTE Energy will continue construction milestones on the secured 1.4 GW Saline Township campus and Google's 1 GW Van Buren Township site, with power demand ramping up progressively between 2027 and 2028. Utility management plans to finalize negotiations on an additional large-load contract from its 2 GW advanced pipeline before the end of the year.

State utility regulators at the Michigan Public Service Commission will oversee the prudency of ongoing grid distribution investments and verify that large-load tariffs successfully insulate residential ratepayers from system upgrade costs.
