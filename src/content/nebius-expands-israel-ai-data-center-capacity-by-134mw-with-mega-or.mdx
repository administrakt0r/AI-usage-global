## Nebius Expands Israel AI Data Center Capacity by 134MW with Mega Or

### Neocloud commits to $1.6B campus expansion across Beit Shemesh and Masmiya, raising total Israeli lease to 222MW as regulator freezes grid access

Amsterdam-based AI cloud infrastructure provider Nebius has contracted an additional 134 megawatts of dedicated data center capacity from Israeli developer Mega Or across two new campuses. The expansion raises Nebius's total contracted capacity with Mega Or to 222 megawatts, representing an estimated $1.6 billion in facility construction costs. The agreement arrives as regional grid operators struggle to supply large compute clusters, with Israel's Electricity Authority freezing grid connection applications at one of the planned sites.

## Key details

Under the seven-year lease agreement, first reported by [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/nebius-expands-israel-data-center-lease-with-mega-or-by-134mw/) and [CalcalisTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/25ue6vi49), Nebius secured additional capacity at Mega Or's Beit Shemesh development and a planned campus in Masmiya. The deal includes an option for Nebius to extend the lease by five years upon delivery, targeted for the end of the second quarter of 2028.

Mega Or estimates construction capital expenditures for the two facilities at approximately $12 million per megawatt of IT capacity. Once fully operational, the 134-megawatt expansion is projected to generate roughly 550 million Israeli shekels ($164.7 million) in annual net operating income for Mega Or, building upon 740 million shekels ($221.7 million) from prior contracts between the companies.

The transaction marks the third capacity deal between the companies. Nebius previously secured 8 megawatts at Modi'in in May 2025 and 80 megawatts split between Beit Shemesh (58 megawatts) and Masmiya (22 megawatts) earlier in 2026. At Beit Shemesh, where the total campus power envelope is planned for 135 megawatts, Nebius will occupy half of the site.

### Why this matters

The deal reflects how independent GPU cloud providers are locking up massive multi-megawatt blocks of power years before servers can be installed. Drawing 222 megawatts of continuous baseload electricity requires roughly 1.94 terawatt-hours of power each year. For context, that equals approximately 2.5% of Israel's entire annual electricity generation, concentrated into three suburban facilities.

Supplying high-density AI clusters puts intense pressure on localized transmission infrastructure. The Masmiya site currently lacks an approved grid connection because Israel's Electricity Authority halted processing new high-voltage interconnections to audit transmission headroom. Securing formal transmission rights is a mandatory condition precedent for closing the Masmiya portion of the agreement.

### Context

Nebius, spun out of Russian tech conglomerate Yandex following its corporate restructuring and led by CEO Arkady Volozh, has emerged as a major alternative cloud builder competing with CoreWeave and Lambda Labs for Nvidia accelerators. In its third-quarter 2026 earnings report, Nebius posted group revenue of $980.8 million and deployed $5.7 billion in quarterly capital expenditures, primarily for compute clusters and data center leases.

Grid interconnection delays and regulatory scrutiny are spreading beyond North America. Transmission bottlenecks have prompted emergency reviews worldwide, paralleling legislative efforts like the [US Senate BAAJA bill](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/us-senate-passes-baaja-bill-requiring-20mw-data-centers-to-fund-grid) to mandate data center funding for transmission expansions, as well as rapid capacity scaling across Asia such as [CyrusOne's 48MW buildout in Osaka](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/cyrusone-kep-tops-out-48mw-data-center-in-osaka-japan).

## Risks and open questions

The most immediate operational hurdle is the regulatory freeze on the Masmiya substation connection. If Israel's Electricity Authority declines to expand substation capacity or demands that the developer fund utility-scale grid upgrades, delivery timelines could slip past mid-2028.

Neither Nebius nor Mega Or disclosed water consumption figures or the cooling architecture planned for the 134 megawatts. With Israel facing chronic freshwater constraints, local zoning boards typically push operators toward closed-loop dry cooling or treated effluent rather than evaporative towers.

## What happens next

Mega Or plans to deliver the first 58-megawatt phase of Beit Shemesh in stages through early 2027, followed by initial fit-out of the expanded halls. In Masmiya, developers must submit formal interconnection proposals once the Electricity Authority concludes its grid assessment. Nebius is scheduled to begin server installation across the new 134-megawatt space as construction wraps up in mid-2028.
