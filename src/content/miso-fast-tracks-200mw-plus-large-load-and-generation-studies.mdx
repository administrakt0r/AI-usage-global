## MISO Proposes Fast-Track Interconnection for 200 MW+ AI Loads

### Regional grid operator submits LARS proposal to FERC to pair massive AI demand with dedicated power generation in 120 days.

The Midcontinent Independent System Operator (MISO) has submitted a proposal to federal regulators to establish a fast-track 120-day interconnection study process for large electric loads exceeding 200 MW. The proposed Large Load and Associated Resource Study (LARS) process aims to streamline grid integration by pairing hyperscale AI data centers directly with new co-located power generation facilities across 15 states.

## Key details

MISO's filing with the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) introduces a parallel study track designed for large-scale energy consumers and dedicated generating facilities located within the same local resource zone. To prevent speculative queue inflation, the LARS framework enforces strict entry requirements, including non-refundable deposits and mandatory commercial operation dates within three years of application.

The proposal caps participation at 10 LARS applications per study cycle across three annual review periods. Crucially, the rule mandates that the co-located generation facility and the large AI load remain paired for a minimum of 15 years, ensuring that long-term grid stability and network upgrade costs are shared predictably between project developers.

MISO, which manages electric grid reliability across 15 central U.S. states from Louisiana to Minnesota, projects annual load growth of 1% to 3% through 2044. Grid officials noted that the LARS initiative directly addresses FERC's June finding that existing interconnection rules are inadequate for handling proximate, high-density industrial loads like AI compute facilities.

### Why this matters

As hyperscale AI data centers demand hundreds of megawatts to gigawatt-level capacity, standard interconnection queues in regional transmission organizations have become severely bottlenecked, often taking five to seven years to complete. MISO's 120-day accelerated pathway allows developers to co-locate new generation with AI facilities, bypassing long queue delays without compromising grid reliability standards.

### Context

This filing follows similar fast-track and large-load initiatives across major U.S. grid operators, including PJM Interconnection and CAISO, as utilities struggle to accommodate explosive AI compute expansion. By requiring paired generation and mandatory 15-year commitments, grid operators aim to prevent unbacked "phantom" load requests from triggering multi-billion-dollar grid upgrades that could otherwise burden residential electricity ratepayers.

## What happens next

FERC will review MISO's LARS application to determine whether the proposed 120-day study process and co-location criteria meet federal non-discrimination and reliability standards. If approved, MISO plans to launch its first annual LARS study period, offering a model for other regional transmission organizations seeking to integrate massive AI energy loads while protecting grid stability.
