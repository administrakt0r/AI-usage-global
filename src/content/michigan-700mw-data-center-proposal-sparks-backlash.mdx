## Proposed 700 MW Michigan AI Data Center Sparks Immediate Local Resistance

### Delhi Township officials terminate economic partnership after being blindsided by $10 billion proposal

The Lansing Economic Area Partnership and developer Lighthouse Sustainability have proposed a $10 billion, 700 megawatt data center campus in Holt, Michigan. The announcement triggered swift condemnation from Delhi Township board members, who had enacted a local moratorium on data centers and were not consulted prior to the public announcement.

## Key details

The proposed project spans a 423-acre site known as the Holt Economic Opportunity Park located on College Road in Delhi Township, south of Lansing in Ingham County. Designed to accommodate up to 700 MW of power capacity, the development carries an estimated construction cost of $10 billion. The property was previously used in part for gravel mining, and site readiness work is supported by a $6.5 million state grant secured by the Lansing Economic Area Partnership (LEAP).

LEAP partnered with Lighthouse Sustainability, a newly formed data center developer founded by Gary Dorris, former founder of energy analytics software firm Ascend Analytics. While LEAP holds an option to purchase the property, no land use applications or formal building plans have been submitted, and no anchor hyperscale tenant has been named.

Despite LEAP and Lighthouse stating they intend to respect the local 12-month moratorium on data centers enacted by Delhi Township in April 2026, township officials expressed dismay at learning about the project through a public press release. In response, the Delhi Township Board immediately ended its municipal membership with LEAP and scheduled a special meeting to adopt a resolution opposing any data center development on the site.

### Why this matters

A 700 MW facility represents a massive addition to regional electricity demand, equivalent to the power consumption of hundreds of thousands of homes. The dispute underscores growing friction between regional economic development organizations seeking large-scale capital investments and municipal authorities attempting to protect local infrastructure, water tables, and electrical grid capacity.

### Context

Across Michigan and the broader Midwest, developers are increasingly targeting suburban and semi-rural industrial sites with existing transmission access for gigawatt-scale AI infrastructure. However, community pushback has accelerated throughout 2026 as residents and local governments raise concerns over noise, utility rate increases, and heavy water and power consumption. Local moratoriums have become a primary tool for municipalities seeking time to evaluate environmental and economic risks before long-term commitments are made.

## What happens next

The Delhi Township Board is expected to pass its formal resolution opposing the data center project and calling on Lighthouse Sustainability to honor its pledge to withdraw if local opposition persists. Meanwhile, LEAP and Lighthouse plan to conduct community outreach during the remainder of the township's moratorium to present environmental and energy studies to local residents.
