## AEP Plans $4B Fuel Cell Deal for Google AI Data Center

### Utility pairs 702 MW Bloom microgrid with 1.6 GW linear generator order

American Electric Power has announced a $4 billion plan to build a 702 MW fuel cell power project near Cheyenne, Wyoming, under a 20-year agreement with Google. The utility is also procuring up to 1.6 GW of linear generators from Mainspring Energy to accelerate off-grid power deployment for energy-intensive compute facilities.

## Key details

Under the $4 billion project, Google will purchase power under a 20-year power purchase agreement from Cheyenne Fuel Cell, an entity 92% owned by AEP and 8% by funds advised by Basalt Infrastructure Partners. The project will deploy 702 MW of Bloom Energy fuel cells near Cheyenne, Wyoming, starting construction in 2026 with full commercial operation expected in 2028. The fuel cell installation will incorporate carbon capture technology to deliver near-zero-emissions electricity.

In a parallel transaction valued at up to $4 billion over eight years, AEP signed a purchase agreement for up to 1.6 GW of Mainspring Energy linear generators. AEP will take initial delivery of 190 MW in 2027 and purchase approximately 200 MW annually thereafter. Mainspring's 2.5 MW modular units generate electricity via a flameless, low-temperature reaction that produces near-zero nitrogen oxide emissions and can operate on natural gas, biogas, or hydrogen.

### Why this matters

The deals highlight how electric utilities are bypassing traditional transmission grid interconnection queues by deploying massive behind-the-meter and fuel cell microgrids directly for hyperscale AI operators. With grid capacity backlogs stretching multi-year delays across major US regions, major utilities are stepping outside their traditional service territories to provide rapid, dedicated power solutions financed by corporate tech customers.

### Context

As artificial intelligence workloads drive unprecedented electricity demand growth across North America, hyperscalers like Google are seeking rapid power delivery without relying on congested public grids. The 702 MW Wyoming fuel cell campus represents one of the largest corporate off-grid power commitments to date, following previous fuel cell and microgrid announcements by Oracle, Microsoft, and Nebius. The $4 billion commitment to Mainspring linear generators further reflects utility strategies to secure modular, rapid-deploy generation hardware amid tight global gas turbine supply chains.

## What happens next

AEP plans to break ground on the 702 MW Cheyenne fuel cell facility before the end of 2026, targeting commercial operation in 2028. Meanwhile, initial shipments of Mainspring's 2.5 MW linear generator units will begin arriving in 2027 as AEP integrates modular dispatchable power across its regional service footprint.
