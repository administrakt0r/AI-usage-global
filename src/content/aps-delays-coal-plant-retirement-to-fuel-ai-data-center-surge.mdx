## APS Delays Coal Plant Retirement to Fuel AI Data Center Surge

### Utility pushes Four Corners exit to 2038 as peak power demand jumps 60%

Arizona Public Service has proposed extending the operational life of its Four Corners coal-fired power plant by seven years to 2038. The decision forms a central pillar of the utility's updated integrated resource plan designed to meet surging electricity demand from artificial intelligence data centers and industrial manufacturing across the Desert Southwest.

## Key details

- In an Integrated Resource Plan filed with the Arizona Corporation Commission, Arizona Public Service (APS) revealed that peak grid demand is projected to climb 3.4% annually over the next 15 years, reaching more than 14.5 GW by 2041 compared to its August record peak of 9.2 GW.
- Annual retail electricity sales across APS's service territory are forecast to more than double from 34,200 GWh in 2025 to approximately 80,600 GWh by 2041, driven primarily by high-density data centers and semiconductor manufacturing facilities.
- To maintain grid reliability amid unprecedented load growth, APS plans to postpone the planned 2031 retirement of the coal-fired Four Corners Power Plant in northern New Mexico to as late as 2038, which the utility estimates could save customers nearly $1 billion compared to immediate replacement costs.
- Alongside the coal extension, APS outlines plans to add 4,000 MW of new natural gas generation through 2036, including repowering the Cholla facility into a 387 MW gas plant by 2030 and constructing the 1,238 MW Desert Sun combined-cycle plant by 2032 dedicated to large-customer subscription loads.
- The utility's resource additions also incorporate 2,000 MW of utility-scale solar and 2,500 MW of battery energy storage online by 2029, scaling total battery storage to 6 GW by 2030 to complement fossil fuel baseload generation.

### Why this matters

The decision to postpone coal plant retirements illustrates how the explosive compute requirements of generative AI are colliding directly with decarbonization timetables. Utilities facing massive large-load interconnection requests from hyperscale operators are choosing fossil fuel longevity over retirement targets to ensure round-the-clock reliability. Extending coal generation preserves firm baseload power, but it threatens regional emission targets and lock in higher greenhouse gas footprints for cloud computing infrastructure.

### Context

Arizona has become a premier national hub for hyperscale data center development due to its low seismic risk, favorable tax structures, and access to regional fiber backbones. However, rapid data center expansion has strained the Desert Southwest's electrical grid, prompting utilities to reassess long-term capacity plans. Similar moves have unfolded across the PJM Interconnection, Duke Energy's service territory in the Carolinas, and Georgia Power, where utilities have repeatedly raised load forecasts and slowed fossil fuel phaseouts to accommodate AI compute clusters.

## Risks and open questions

Clean energy and ratepayer advocates have sharply criticized the plan, arguing that prolonging coal operations increases environmental harms and air pollution while passing volatile fuel and maintenance costs to households. There is also substantial financial risk regarding whether proposed data center loads materialize as contracted; to mitigate potential stranded asset risks, APS has instituted minimum take-or-pay energy commitments, mandatory collateral, and early exit fees for hyperscale customers.

## What happens next

The Arizona Corporation Commission will review the 2026 Integrated Resource Plan through public hearings, evidentiary proceedings, and stakeholder comments over the coming months. Regulators will evaluate whether to approve the Four Corners life extension and proposed gas plant construction, or require APS to procure more aggressive deployments of distributed clean generation, geothermal power, and long-duration storage.
