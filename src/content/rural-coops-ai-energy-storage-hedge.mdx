## Not-for-profit Utilities Turn to Energy Storage to Hedge AI Costs

### Rural cooperatives deploy battery storage to mitigate wholesale power price spikes driven by data center demand.

Distribution cooperatives are increasingly turning to battery energy storage and behind-the-meter demand response to shield members from rising wholesale electricity prices. As hyperscale AI data centers drive record demand for grid capacity, smaller non-profit utilities are using storage as a financial and operational hedge against infrastructure-driven cost spikes.

## Key details

According to the National Rural Electric Cooperative Association (NRECA), rural electric cooperatives already had 439 MW/1,047 MWh of operating battery energy storage projects online as of last summer. These systems allow cooperatives to "peak shave"—discharging batteries when demand is highest—to reduce the expensive wholesale demand charges they pay to larger power providers.

Meeker Energy, a Minnesota-based cooperative serving approximately 10,000 members, represents a growing trend among non-profit utilities. With 60% of its members already participating in at least one load management program, the cooperative is now testing behind-the-meter residential batteries. Unlike investor-owned utilities that profit from building new infrastructure, cooperatives are focused on price hedging and avoiding the massive infrastructure investments required to support the concentrated load growth of AI infrastructure.

### Why this matters

The AI boom is fundamentally changing the economics of wholesale power. While large-scale developers and utilities may benefit from the surge in demand, smaller distribution cooperatives often face the downside of higher wholesale prices without the direct revenue from data center contracts. By deploying storage, these utilities can decouple their members' energy bills from the volatile costs associated with the national AI buildout.

### Context

The utility industry is currently projected to spend $1.4 trillion over the next five years to upgrade the grid for AI and industrial electrification. This capital-intensive cycle is already leading to double-digit increases in residential energy bills in several states. For rural and member-owned utilities, which often have less capital than investor-owned giants, energy storage offers a way to maintain reliability and affordability without waiting for large-scale grid expansions that may take a decade to complete.

## What happens next

Expect a significant increase in "distributed" energy storage deployments as cooperatives seek to bypass grid congestion. The success of programs like Meeker's will likely lead to more aggressive federal and state incentives for community-scale storage, specifically designed to protect rural ratepayers from the infrastructure-driven costs of the global AI race.
