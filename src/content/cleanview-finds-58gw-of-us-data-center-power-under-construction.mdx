## Cleanview Finds 58GW of US Data Center Power Under Construction

### Active builds would nearly double the nation's 65GW operational fleet despite a summer slowdown in new starts

Data centre developers in the United States currently have 58 gigawatts of power capacity under active construction, according to new research published by infrastructure tracker Cleanview. If completed, the ongoing buildout will nearly double the 65 gigawatts of data centre capacity currently operating across the country. While new construction starts dipped 25 percent in the third quarter of 2026 to 8.4 gigawatts following a record-breaking spring, overall construction activity in 2026 has already reached 38 gigawatts—twice the volume recorded during the same period in 2025.

## Key details

The quarterly market analysis, published by [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/cleanview-us-data-center-construction-slows-in-q3-2026-following-record-breaking-q2/), details how the rapid expansion of artificial intelligence infrastructure is pushing physical buildout volumes to unprecedented levels. Cleanview, which monitors energy infrastructure and data centre construction using permit filings, regulatory dockets, and satellite imagery via [Cleanview](https://cleanview.co), tracked 8.4 gigawatts across 65 new facilities in the third quarter of 2026, down from 11.2 gigawatts in the second quarter.

The third-quarter moderation followed the hottest construction quarter in industry history, marking the first sequential quarterly decline since early 2025. However, early data from the fourth quarter indicates the slowdown was brief. Developers initiated 4.6 gigawatts of new capacity in October alone, representing the highest monthly total since June and nearly doubling October 2025 activity. Cleanview projects that announced data centre projects initiated between January and October could require more than $1 trillion in total capital expenditure upon completion.

At the state level, Texas dominates national development with 9.8 gigawatts of capacity initiated over the past 12 months, followed by Virginia at 4.5 gigawatts and Louisiana at 2.8 gigawatts. Hyperscale cloud providers account for the largest individual footprints: Amazon leads with 4.6 gigawatts of new starts, followed closely by Google with 4.1 gigawatts and Meta with 3.9 gigawatts. Among colocation and wholesale developers, Vantage Data Centers initiated 2.8 gigawatts, Stack Infrastructure started 2.5 gigawatts, QTS added 2.1 gigawatts, Crusoe Energy broke ground on 1.8 gigawatts, and Related Digital commenced 1.6 gigawatts.

### Why this matters

The sheer scale of 58 gigawatts under active construction illustrates why regional power grids are facing severe strain. To put this in perspective, 58 gigawatts is roughly equal to the entire peak electric demand of the state of New York, or the output of roughly 58 standard commercial nuclear reactors.

Because transmission interconnection queues face multi-year delays across major regional transmission organizations like PJM and ERCOT, adding 58 gigawatts of high-density load threatens to drive up wholesale capacity prices and increase regional competition for electricity. Furthermore, the high concentration of new capacity in Texas (9.8 GW) and Virginia (4.5 GW) concentrates intense cooling water withdrawals and transmission upgrade costs on local ratepayers and watersheds.

## Risks and open questions

It remains uncertain how much of the 58 gigawatts under construction can successfully energize on schedule. Severe supply-chain lead times for step-up transformers, switchgear, and high-voltage circuit breakers now stretch from three to four years, meaning many facilities may complete physical shells long before utilities can provide energized substation hookups.

Additionally, rising community pushback over electricity bills and water consumption poses legal and permitting risks for projects currently in progress. As state regulators and grid authorities scrutinize large-load tariffs, developers may face costly mandatory grid upgrade contributions or curtailment clauses before achieving commercial operations.

## What happens next

Market observers will monitor November and December construction filings to determine whether the fourth quarter surpasses the second quarter's record of 11.2 gigawatts. Cleanview will release full-year 2026 deployment figures in January 2027.

State utility commissions in Texas, Virginia, and Louisiana are expected to issue final rulings on dedicated large-load tariff schedules throughout the first half of 2027, establishing how much capital developers must commit for long-term transmission and generation upgrades.
