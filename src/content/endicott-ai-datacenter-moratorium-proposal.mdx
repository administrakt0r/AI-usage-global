## New York Village Proposes Total Moratorium on AI Data Centers

### Endicott leaders cite long-standing pollution history and resource strain in call for a 12-month pause.

The Village of Endicott, New York, is preparing to vote on a 12-month moratorium on all AI data center development. Local officials aim to protect community water and energy resources from the rapid expansion of high-density computing infrastructure.

## Key details

Mayor Nick Burlingame announced that the village attorney is drafting legislation for a total pause on data center construction, with a board vote scheduled for Monday, July 20, 2026. If approved, the moratorium will create a one-year window for a newly formed committee of residents and businesses to study the potential impacts of these facilities.

The proposed local measure is notably stricter than New York’s statewide moratorium passed earlier this year. While the state's freeze only applies to "large" facilities, Endicott’s proposal would halt all data center development regardless of scale. The village's caution is rooted in its environmental history, with leaders emphasizing the need to be "good stewards" of local resources after decades of dealing with industrial pollution.

### Why this matters

The Endicott proposal highlights a growing trend of municipal-level resistance to AI infrastructure. Even in states where broad regulations exist, local governments are finding that regional standards may not be sufficient to protect specific watersheds or local grid capacity. This reflects a shift toward total localized oversight for AI resource footprints.

### Context

This move follows several other local and state-level moratoriums across the United States in 2026, including in Seattle, Spokane, and Reno. The village of Endicott, formerly a major industrial hub, is particularly sensitive to new large-scale developments that could further strain local utilities or risk groundwater contamination—a concern that has previously mobilized the community.

## What happens next

The village board is set to vote on the moratorium legislation on July 20. If it passes, the village will establish a dedicated study committee to evaluate how data centers align with local environmental and infrastructure goals. This pause would effectively block any new project applications until mid-2027, allowing for the creation of village-specific zoning and resource standards.
