## OpenAI Secures 457MW AI Compute Capacity from TensorWave

### Six-year contract deploys AMD Helios GPU racks to cut token costs

Artificial intelligence developer OpenAI has signed a six-year computing agreement with neocloud provider TensorWave to secure 457 megawatts of dedicated data center capacity. Under the contract, TensorWave will supply single-tenant infrastructure powered by AMD Instinct MI455X GPUs across multiple facilities. The commitment highlights how frontier AI laboratories are turning to multi-hundred-megawatt power reservations and alternative silicon architectures to manage escalating compute expenses.

## Key details

Under the six-year deployment agreement, TensorWave will deliver dedicated IT capacity utilizing AMD's newly unveiled Helios rack-scale systems. Operations are scheduled to launch in the first half of 2027 and reach full deployment across multiple data center locations by early 2028. TensorWave described the transaction as the largest customer infrastructure deal in its history.

The Helios systems combine AMD Instinct MI455X accelerators, sixth-generation AMD Epyc "Venice" server processors, AMD ROCm software, and Pensando networking into unified liquid-cooled server racks. Each individual Helios rack delivers up to 2.9 exaflops of peak FP4 compute, 1.4 exaflops of peak FP8 compute, 31 terabytes of high-bandwidth memory (HBM4), and 1.7 petabytes per second of memory bandwidth. According to AMD specifications, the architecture achieves a 15% improvement in peak FP4 throughput, 50% greater HBM capacity, and up to 30% more tokens delivered per dollar compared to competing rack configurations.

TensorWave, which completed a $350 million Series B financing round in June 2026, reported that it has secured roughly 2 gigawatts of long-term data center power capacity across the United States. The company previously contracted 20 megawatts of facility capacity with TecFusions across sites in Pennsylvania and Arizona in January 2026.

### Why this matters

A single contract for 457 megawatts represents an enormous physical footprint on regional electric grids, equal to the continuous electricity demand of roughly 350,000 American homes. As training and inference cluster requirements outgrow individual metropolitan utility substations, frontier AI companies must lock in multi-site allocations years in advance.

Furthermore, securing AMD silicon at this scale represents a strategic shift in AI cost economics. By contracting for hardware promising a 30% reduction in tokens per dollar, OpenAI is seeking to insulate its operational margins against soaring semiconductor prices and the capital constraints imposed by single-vendor cloud ecosystems.

### Context

Escalating electricity tariffs and interconnection queues stretching beyond five years have transformed energization timelines into the defining bottleneck for artificial intelligence scaling. Cloud providers and model developers have increasingly shifted toward long-term power purchase agreements, colocation pre-leases, and specialized GPU clouds to ensure guaranteed electrical supply.

OpenAI previously engaged in multi-gigawatt utility service agreements in Georgia and partnerships with other specialized infrastructure operators. Diversifying 457 megawatts of its future inference and training pipeline into AMD hardware demonstrates that access to power and lower token economics are overriding traditional software ecosystem lock-in.

## Risks and open questions

Deploying 457 megawatts of next-generation AMD hardware introduces technical integration hurdles, particularly surrounding software optimization in ROCm for large-scale distributed training workflows. If software parity lags or early hardware yields fall short, deploying clusters across multiple geographically dispersed sites could complicate latency and interconnect efficiency.

Additionally, multi-year capacity contracts create financial and regulatory exposure. Local utilities and public service commissions are increasingly examining whether large-scale commercial data centers pay adequate grid tariffs, raising the prospect of unexpected transmission surcharges or cooling water restrictions before the 2027 to 2028 operational window.

## What happens next

TensorWave and OpenAI plan to begin site preparation and hardware integration ahead of initial cluster energization in early 2027. Market analysts will watch whether other hyperscale AI labs follow OpenAI by signing direct hundred-megawatt contracts with specialized GPU neoclouds to hedge against power shortages and hardware pricing premiums.
