## Pentagon Negotiates $5B Loan for Fluidstack Data Center Hardware

### Federal funding would target manufacturing capacity for critical AI infrastructure components rather than facility footprints.

The U.S. Department of Defense is in negotiations to provide $5 billion in debt financing to AI cloud provider Fluidstack. The proposed loan would fund component manufacturing capacity needed for hyperscale AI data centers rather than direct facility expansion.

## Key details

According to a Wall Street Journal report, the $5 billion loan is being negotiated through the Pentagon's Office of Strategic Capital. Rather than expanding physical data center footprints, the capital would be directed toward building manufacturing capacity for essential data center hardware and components. The deal remains subject to final agreement and regulatory approvals.

Fluidstack, originally founded in London in 2017 before relocating its headquarters to the United States, is currently expanding compute facilities across several states, including New York, Indiana, Louisiana, and Texas. The company operates under a strategic arrangement with Google and Anthropic, in which Google guarantees data center leases to help Fluidstack secure capital at favorable rates. Under this framework, Anthropic leases compute capacity powered by Google's Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) and other hardware.

### Why this matters

The potential $5 billion federal loan highlights severe supply chain bottlenecks in AI data center hardware manufacturing. While much public attention focuses on facility site selection and power grid connections, component shortages—ranging from power distribution equipment to specialized server hardware—have become primary constraints on AI compute expansion. Direct federal intervention indicates that data center supply chains are now viewed as strategic national security assets.

### Context

The Pentagon's Office of Strategic Capital was established to provide loans and financial support to commercial technologies deemed essential to national security, having previously supported rare-earth supply chains and defense technology firms. As hyperscalers and neocloud providers compete for limited manufacturing capacity, government financing reflects growing concern over hardware availability and domestic supply chain resilience.

## What happens next

If finalized, the $5 billion transaction will require formal disclosure and administrative clearance from federal oversight bodies. Industry analysts will monitor whether Fluidstack's manufacturing investments help alleviate broader hardware supply bottlenecks for domestic AI infrastructure.
