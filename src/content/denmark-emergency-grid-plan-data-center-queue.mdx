## Denmark Passes Emergency Grid Law Moving Data Centers to Back of Queue

### New four-tier prioritization system puts hyperscale compute behind critical infrastructure and green transition projects.

Denmark has officially adopted an emergency grid management law that strips hyperscale data centers of equal grid access and places them at the back of the connection queue. The legislation, passed by the Danish Parliament on October 1, 2026, and taking effect on October 12, abolishes the nation's traditional first-come, first-served grid allocation model.

## Key details

Under the newly adopted emergency framework, Denmark's Ministry of Climate, Energy, and Utilities granted national TSO Energinet and regional distribution companies broad authority to prioritize grid access based on societal necessity. Connection requests are now categorized into four distinct tiers:

- **Category 1:** Highest priority reserved for critical societal functions, including defense, healthcare, emergency services, essential municipal load growth, and specialized data centers directly tied to critical infrastructure.
- **Category 2:** Projects supporting the broader green transition and electrification of society.
- **Category 3:** Dedicated energy storage systems and co-located battery storage facilities.
- **Category 4:** Lowest priority assigned to large energy consumers, explicitly placing hyperscale commercial data centers at the end of the line.

The law allows grid operators to reject or indefinitely defer Category 4 connection applications to preserve capacity for domestic heating, industrial electrification, and essential public services.

### Why this matters

The legislative shift highlights how European power grids are reaching capacity limits under the weight of AI data center expansion. By explicitly relegating commercial hyperscale facilities to the lowest priority category, Denmark is forcing developers to secure off-grid generation, construct dedicated battery storage, or relocate planned compute facilities to regions with surplus power capacity.

### Context

While Denmark's data center footprint has historically concentrated around Copenhagen—hosting operators such as Digital Realty, atNorth, Penta Infra, GlobalConnect, Stack Infrastructure, and Microsoft's newly launched cloud region—surging power demand has strained local transmission lines. Denmark's policy move reflects a broader Nordic trend; neighboring Finland is conducting consultations through October 9, 2026, on a similar proposal to deprioritize grid requests exceeding 10 MW.

## What happens next

The emergency plan officially takes effect on October 12, 2026. Grid operator Energinet will begin re-evaluating pending connection queues across all four categories, likely freezing or delaying several proposed commercial data center expansions. Meanwhile, attention shifts to Finland as regulators review public comments on their proposed 10 MW grid rationing threshold.
