## NTT and B.Grimm Power Sign MoU for 400MW Data Center Campus in Thailand

### The non-binding agreement targets a $3.3 billion hyperscale buildout in Chonburi, pairing NTT's campus expansion with dedicated utility capacity

NTT Global Data Centers and Thai power producer B.Grimm Power have signed a memorandum of understanding to pursue a 400 MW data center campus in Thailand, [Data Center Dynamics reports](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ntt-signs-mou-with-bgrimm-for-400mw-data-center-project-in-thailand/). The proposed development carries an estimated investment value of THB 113 billion ($3.3 billion).

## Key details

B.Grimm Power announced the agreement through its subsidiary Amata B.Grimm Power, partnering with NTT Global Data Centers (Thailand) to study a joint digital and energy infrastructure project for cloud and AI workloads. The two companies signed the MoU on November 19 in Tokyo during the Thailand-Japan Investment and Business Dialogue, hosted by Thailand's Board of Investment and Japan's Keidanren business federation, with Thai Deputy Prime Minister and Finance Minister Ekniti Nitithanprapas in attendance, according to [Kaohoon International](https://www.kaohooninternational.com/en/bgrimm-partners-with-ntt-gdc-to-develop-400mw-hyperscale-data-center-campus-in-thailand/).

Under the division of roles, Amata B.Grimm Power will plan the power and utility supply, while NTT will handle data center design, construction, and operations. Neither company disclosed a construction timeline, cooling design, or confirmed customer commitments for the 400 MW campus.

The partnership builds on an existing industrial footprint at the Amata City Chonburi Industrial Estate near Bangkok. NTT already operates two facilities there: Bangkok 2, a 3.6 MW site launched in 2015 across 3,800 square meters, and Bangkok 3, a 12 MW facility spanning 4,000 square meters. A fourth facility, Bangkok 4, broke ground in the summer and is scheduled to go live in the second quarter of 2027, backed by an earlier 100 MW power purchase agreement with Amata B.Grimm. NTT also runs Bangkok 1, a 1.6 MW edge facility in Bang Na.

### By the numbers

- **400 MW**: planned power capacity of the proposed campus
- **THB 113 billion ($3.3 billion)**: estimated total project investment
- **100 MW**: capacity of the earlier power deal for NTT's Bangkok 4 facility at the same estate
- **3.3 GW**: B.Grimm Power's current installed generation capacity, with a target of 10 GW in power purchase agreements by 2030
- **15.6 MW**: combined capacity of NTT's existing Bangkok 2 and Bangkok 3 facilities in Chonburi

### Why this matters

Moving from a 12 MW campus to a 400 MW plan multiplies NTT's local power requirement by more than 25 times at a single industrial corridor. Securing generation and substation capacity through an industrial power producer like B.Grimm is a direct response to the grid bottlenecks slowing hyperscale builds across Southeast Asia. Because the utility already supplies NTT's Chonburi site, the partners can coordinate on-site power planning alongside data center permitting rather than waiting in standard utility interconnection queues.

### Context

The MoU lands at a sensitive moment for Thailand's data center boom. Earlier this year, [Thai regulators paused construction on 49 data center projects](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/thailand-pauses-construction-on-49-data-centers) while drafting stricter grid-integration and environmental rules. Large-scale developers are increasingly pairing directly with private power producers to prove their projects will not destabilize residential power supply or push infrastructure costs onto local ratepayers.

## Risks and open questions

An MoU is a feasibility and planning step, not a binding construction contract or a final investment decision. It remains unclear how much of the 400 MW load would rely on gas-fired cogeneration versus renewable PPAs, or how the campus would handle cooling water in Chonburi's tropical climate. Regulatory approval under Thailand's upcoming grid and environmental standards could also alter the project's schedule or power-sourcing mix.

## What happens next

Amata B.Grimm Power and NTT will conduct joint technical and commercial feasibility studies before committing capital to land, substations, or initial building phases. Meanwhile, NTT's Bangkok 4 facility at the same Chonburi estate remains on track for completion in the second quarter of 2027.
