## Missouri Farmers Rally Against $6.3 Billion AI Data Center Project

### Nodaway County residents cite 5-million-gallon daily water demand as threat to local supply

A coalition of farmers and residents in Nodaway County, Missouri, has organized a rapid and widespread opposition to a proposed $6.3 billion AI data center complex. The "Nodaway Says No" movement highlights a growing national trend of local communities challenging the massive resource demands of AI infrastructure, specifically regarding water security and grid stability.

## Key details

The proposed "White Cloud Acres" development south of Maryville, Missouri, is being evaluated by Scale Microgrids. If built, the project could consume up to 5 million gallons of water per day for cooling, according to estimates from the Environmental and Energy Study Institute. This demand is double the 2.5 million gallon daily capacity of Maryville’s existing water treatment plant, which serves the entire city and surrounding rural areas.

More than 7,000 residents have signed a petition against the project in just two months. Local farmers, including Tim Schafer, have raised additional concerns about noise pollution affecting livestock production and reproduction, as well as potential PFAS contamination and light pollution. Maryville Mayor John McBride has also publicly stated his opposition to the plan.

### Why this matters

This dispute underscores the physical limitations that water scarcity and infrastructure capacity are placing on the AI boom. When a single data center campus requires twice the water of the entire neighboring municipality, the economic and environmental trade-offs become a flashpoint for bipartisan community resistance.

### Context

The Nodaway County opposition mirrors recent moratoriums and protests in states like Arizona, South Carolina, and Nevada. As AI models require increasingly dense compute clusters, the transition from traditional air cooling to water-intensive liquid cooling is forcing data centers into direct competition with agriculture and residential needs for limited freshwater supplies.

## Risks and open questions

A primary unknown is whether Scale Microgrids will attempt to secure independent water rights or if the project will be downsized to meet local constraints. There are also concerns regarding Missouri’s currently limited regulatory framework for data center resource reporting, which residents argue lacks the transparency needed for such large-scale industrialization of rural land.

## What happens next

Scale Microgrids is expected to announce by the end of June 2026 whether it will proceed with the White Cloud Acres development. Local organizers plan to continue their "Nodaway Says No" campaign, focusing on legislative efforts at the state level to implement stricter resource reporting and community impact requirements for all future data center projects in Missouri.
