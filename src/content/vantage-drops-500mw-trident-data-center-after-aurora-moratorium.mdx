## Vantage Drops 500MW Trident Data Center After Aurora Moratorium

### Aurora City Council halts new data center applications through September 2027 as neighborhood opposition pushes a 334 MW IT campus off its approved site

Vantage Data Centers withdrew its planned 500 MW Trident campus from its site in Aurora, Colorado, on November 23, 2026, during the same City Council meeting where lawmakers unanimously passed a temporary moratorium on new data centers. The two-story facility was planned to cover 860,000 square feet with 334 MW of IT load, which [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/vantage-drops-plans-for-trident-data-center-after-colorados-aurora-city-passes-moratorium/) reported would have made it the largest data center in Colorado.

## Key details

Aurora's moratorium took effect immediately after Monday night's unanimous vote and bars new data center applications on land west and south of the Interstate 70 and Powhaton Road intersection through September 30, 2027, or until the council adopts new land-use rules, according to [CBS Colorado](https://www.cbsnews.com/colorado/news/aurora-passes-partial-data-center-moratorium-trident-cancels-site-plans/) and [Sentinel Colorado](https://sentinelcolorado.com/metro/another-proposed-aurora-data-center-moratorium-and-trident-relocation-rule-face-vote/). Because the freeze exempts projects with previously accepted applications, Trident could legally have proceeded on its site near East 48th Avenue and E-470.

Vantage stepped back anyway after months of neighborhood protests. City Attorney Peter Schulte informed the packed council chamber during public comment that "Trident just announced they have abandoned the site next to your neighborhood for the data center," CBS Colorado reported. In a statement to Data Center Dynamics, Vantage said it initiated a review of alternative sites in response to community concerns and will no longer pursue the current location, though it intends to stay in Colorado.

The project had drawn anger largely over how it was cleared. Planning documents were public from August 2025, but the campus won administrative approval as a use by right under Airport District zoning without posted public notices. Residents from the Windler, Green Valley Ranch, and 55+ Reserve at Green Valley Ranch neighborhoods rallied against the facility, culminating in a five-hour council hearing where 40 residents spoke.

### By the numbers

- **500 MW**: Total planned power demand for the Trident campus, with 334 MW dedicated to IT equipment.
- **860,000 sq ft**: Planned footprint (80,000 square meters) across a two-story building.
- **September 30, 2027**: End date for Aurora's partial moratorium unless the City Council replaces it sooner with permanent rules.
- **3,000+**: Construction jobs Vantage estimated the build would create, alongside 30 to 50 permanent company roles.
- **160 MW**: Planned capacity of a separate QTS data center in Aurora that is mostly built and excluded from the freeze.

### Why this matters

A 500 MW data center concentrates utility-scale power demand onto a single parcel next to suburban housing. Vantage had planned closed-loop cooling paired with local water replenishment investments, while QTS told Data Center Dynamics its Aurora facilities pay their own energy costs and "use no additional water once operational." Even with closed-loop designs, neighbors questioned long-term water supply, air quality, and continuous equipment noise near homes.

For developers, administrative by-right zoning did not shield the site once residents mobilized. Aurora had already rejected a six-month pause in August and an indefinite freeze in October before Mayor Mike Coffman opposed Trident and the full council sponsored the November moratorium.

### Context

Aurora's vote follows a wave of US cities using temporary pauses to catch up with hyperscale load requests. On the same day, [Providence reaffirmed a 100% zoning ban on commercial data centers](https://aiusageglobal.pages.dev/blog-detail/providence-reaffirms-100-data-center-ban-and-orders-grid-review) while ordering city planners to study substation limits, water demand, and residential rate impacts.

## Risks and open questions

Dropping the Aurora parcel does not cancel Vantage's 500 MW power demand; it shifts it elsewhere in the region. Sentinel Colorado reported that Vantage has been working with utility Xcel Energy and city staff to evaluate alternative sites farther from residential zoning, and the company has not named a replacement parcel.

Aurora's moratorium also leaves major pieces of the local buildout untouched. It excludes QTS's nearly finished 160 MW facility and does not cover land north and east of the I-70 and Powhaton Road boundary. Separately, CBS Colorado reported that council members discussed a proposed ordinance to ban future sales tax incentives for data centers, leaving the city's final fiscal rules unsettled.

## What happens next

The moratorium remains in place in west and south Aurora through September 30, 2027, while city staff and council members draft permanent siting, setback, and noise standards. Council members are also due to act on the proposed ban on data center sales tax incentives, while Vantage continues site and grid feasibility reviews with Xcel Energy elsewhere in Colorado.
