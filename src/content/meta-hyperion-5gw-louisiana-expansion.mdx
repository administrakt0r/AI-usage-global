## Meta Expands Hyperion AI Supercluster to 5GW in $50B Louisiana Deal

### Tech giant scales up Richland Parish campus to become world's largest AI infrastructure project.

Meta has officially announced a massive expansion of its Hyperion AI data center in Louisiana, increasing its planned capacity to 5 gigawatts. This $50 billion investment marks a significant escalation in the race for AI compute and highlights the extreme energy demands of next-generation model training.

## Key details

The revised plans for the Richland Parish campus more than double the initial 2 GW capacity target, with Meta now aiming for 5 GW of total compute power. The total investment has surged to over $50 billion, a fivefold increase from the project's original $10 billion estimate in late 2024. To power this massive load, Meta has secured an agreement with Entergy Louisiana for 5.2 GW of natural gas-fired generation capacity and up to 2.5 GW of new solar support.

Meta also committed $1 billion to local infrastructure improvements, including upgrades to roads, water systems, and wastewater facilities in the rural parish. The expansion follows a 20-year sales tax exemption signed by Louisiana Governor Jeff Landry, specifically designed to attract hyperscale AI investments to the state.

### Why this matters

The scale of the Hyperion project is unprecedented, representing a single-site power demand comparable to that of major metropolitan areas. By choosing to build 5.2 GW of on-site or dedicated natural gas power, Meta is signaling that grid constraints and renewable energy intermittency are being bypassed in favor of fossil-fuel baseload to ensure 24/7 operations for its Superintelligence Labs.

### Context

Louisiana is rapidly becoming a focal point for the "shadow grid" movement, where AI developers build private power infrastructure to avoid the multi-year delays of public grid interconnection. This 5 GW expansion fits into a broader trend of hyperscalers like Microsoft and Google seeking gigawatt-scale campus locations where energy and land are plentiful but local resources are often strained.

## Risks and open questions

While Meta and local officials tout the economic benefits, including significant tax revenue increases for schools, the environmental cost of a 5.2 GW natural gas commitment remains a point of contention. The project's reliance on fossil fuels highlights the growing gap between AI companies' net-zero pledges and the reality of their massive, immediate energy requirements. Additionally, the long-term impact on local water tables from cooling a 5 GW facility is yet to be fully disclosed.

## What happens next

Construction at the 1,100-acre Richland Parish site is expected to accelerate as Meta races to bring the first phases of the 5 GW cluster online by late 2026. Industry analysts will be watching closely to see if other hyperscalers follow suit with similar multi-gigawatt single-site announcements, and how Louisiana residents respond to the growing physical footprint of the "shadow grid" in their backyard.
